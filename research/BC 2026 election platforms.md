# Five parties offer sharply different BC futures

British Columbia's snap election on **October 24, 2026** presents voters with two clear dividing lines and several areas of quiet convergence. The sharpest split is on reconciliation and schools: the **Conservatives and OneBC would repeal DRIPA and SOGI 123**, the **Greens would strengthen both**, the **NDP would amend DRIPA after a December 2025 court ruling (text unpublished) and has ended provincial funding for SOGI 123's creator while defending anti-discrimination protections**, and **CentreBC opposes repealing anti-discrimination law** and rejects DRIPA repeal in favour of a treaty framework. The second split is fiscal: the NDP would raise income tax on roughly the top 3.88% of earners and carry deficits near $12-14 billion a year; the Conservatives promise a $1,000 income tax cut and "no new taxes" without a costing or a balanced-budget date; the Greens propose a wealth tax on fortunes above $50 million; OneBC promises 25% cuts to every tax bracket and balance in four years. On drugs, the carbon tax, LNG and a TransLink vehicle levy, the NDP and Conservatives now stand closer together than their rhetoric suggests, because the NDP government itself ended decriminalization, scrapped the consumer carbon tax and backs LNG expansion. The ten-year record shows an NDP that uses provincial power aggressively and then reverses under pressure, a Conservative party that has opposed nearly every NDP bill since 2024 while cycling through four leaders, and Greens who trade confidence votes for written deliverables. No party had released a complete, costed platform as of October 9, so every position below is "as stated so far." In **Surrey-Fleetwood**, a seat the NDP's Jagrup Brar held by about **753 votes** in 2024 and that 338Canada rates a toss-up, none of the five candidates has published a position that departs from their party, so local alignment equals party alignment.

## A snap vote called before any party finished its platform

Premier David Eby called the election on September 22 for October 24, framing it around US tariffs; advance voting runs October 16-21, mail-ballot requests close October 18, and the municipal elections fall one week earlier on October 17 ([Elections BC](https://elections.bc.ca/voting/ways-to-vote/)). Voters must be Canadian citizens, 18 or older on voting day, and BC residents since April 23, 2026 ([Elections BC](https://elections.bc.ca/voting/who-can-vote/)). The fixed-date law would otherwise have set the vote for October 2028, and two-thirds of British Columbians told Angus Reid the call came "too soon" ([Angus Reid](https://angusreid.org/85168-2/)).

The 2024 election left the NDP with **47 seats, the Conservatives 44 and the Greens 2**, a one-seat majority ([Global News](https://globalnews.ca/news/12068973/who-are-bc-party-leaders-2026-election/)). The Conservative caucus then fragmented. John Rustad was removed as leader in December 2025, Kerry-Lynne Findlay won the leadership in May 2026 and **resigned on September 20 after 113 days**, and the board named Cariboo-Chilcotin MLA **Lorne Doerkson interim leader**; under party rules an interim leader cannot seek the permanent job ([CP24](https://www.cp24.com/politics/2026/09/20/kerry-lynne-findlay-resigns-as-bc-conservative-leader/); [Global News timeline](https://globalnews.ca/news/12066889/kerry-lynne-findlay-timeline-resignation/)). Seven of the eight MLAs who had briefly formed a CentreBC caucus returned to the Conservatives when the writ dropped, leaving **Elenore Sturko** (Surrey-Cloverdale, running in Surrey South) as CentreBC leader and its only incumbent ([Penticton Western News](https://pentictonwesternnews.com/2026/09/22/surreys-elenore-sturko-is-the-sole-incumbent-mla-for-centrebc-ahead-of-election/)). The Greens are led by **Emily Lowan**, who holds no seat; OneBC by interim leader **Dallas Brodie**, expelled from the Conservative caucus in 2025 over residential-school comments ([Wikipedia: OneBC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/OneBC_(political_party))).

The Conservatives are the only party running in all 93 ridings; the NDP runs 92, the Greens 73-74, CentreBC 35 and OneBC 33-34, with sources differing by one ([Wikipedia candidates list](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Candidates_of_the_2026_British_Columbia_general_election)). **No party has published a full costed platform**: the NDP has no platform page and releases commitments daily, the Greens say "full platform coming soon," the Conservatives publish themed plan pages, and CentreBC promised its platform for October 10 ([BC Ballot](https://bcballot.ca/platforms/); [Surrey Now-Leader](https://surreynowleader.com/2026/10/09/centrebc-leader-promisesmanufacturing-boost)). Only Eby, Doerkson and Lowan are invited to the October 14 televised debate ([CFJC](https://cfjctoday.com/2026/10/06/b-c-campaign-trail-date-for-leaders-debate-is-set/)).

Post-call polls disagree. Angus Reid (Oct 1-3) had the **Conservatives at 48% and the NDP at 43%**, Liaison (Oct 7-8) 43-37, Leger 39-36, Pollara a 38-38 tie, and Innovative Research the NDP ahead 41-39 ([Wikipedia poll table](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2026_British_Columbia_general_election); [Angus Reid](https://angusreid.org/85168-2/)). The spread exceeds sampling error, so no single poll should be read as the standing. Voters' priorities are stable across pollsters: Ipsos found **cost of living (56%), health care (40%) and housing (34%)** far ahead of US trade (19%), crime (19%), drugs (15%), climate (12%) and land use or DRIPA (6%, rising to 9% among Conservative supporters) ([Ipsos](https://www.ipsos.com/en-ca/top-issues-british-columbians)). No poll found ranks SOGI as a voting issue.

## DRIPA and SOGI produce the only true either-or choices

### DRIPA: repeal, amend, or implement

The Declaration on the Rights of Indigenous Peoples Act requires the province, with Indigenous peoples, to "take all measures necessary to ensure the laws of British Columbia are consistent with the Declaration," to publish an action plan and annual reports, and lets cabinet sign agreements giving an Indigenous governing body joint authority or a consent role over specific statutory decisions ([BC Laws](https://www.bclaws.gov.bc.ca/civix/document/id/complete/statreg/19044)). It passed in 2019 with **no recorded opposition**: the committee division was 82-0, and BC Liberal MLAs, including John Rustad, voted for it ([Votes and Proceedings, Nov 26, 2019](https://lims.leg.bc.ca/pdms/ldp/41st4th/votes/v191126.htm)). Those consent agreements have been used to approve mines, including Eskay Creek with the Tahltan in January 2026 ([BC Government](https://news.gov.bc.ca/releases/2026MCM0008-000078)).

The consensus broke after the BC Court of Appeal ruled on **December 5, 2025** (Gitxaała) that the free-entry mineral claims system was inconsistent with UNDRIP and that DRIPA's alignment duty can be assessed by courts ([Blakes](https://www.blakes.com/insights/b-c-court-of-appeal-rules-previous-free-entry-mineral-tenure-regime-inconsistent-with-undrip/)). Eby called the exposure "unlimited legal liability" and cited more than 20 new or amended lawsuits ([The Narwhal](https://thenarwhal.ca/bc-declaration-act-explainer/)). He then moved from amendment (January) to a proposed three-year suspension framed as a confidence vote (April 2) before dropping it on April 13 when NDP MLA Joan Phillip refused to support it, and agreed with the First Nations Leadership Council on April 20 to seek a solution "before the fall legislative session" ([The Tyee](https://thetyee.ca/News/2026/04/14/BC-Backs-Off-DRIPA-Amendments/); [FNLC joint statement](https://www.fnlc.ca/premiers-first-nations-leadership-council-statement-about-dripa/)). The Supreme Court of Canada granted leave to appeal Gitxaała in May 2026 ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gitxaa%C5%82a_v._British_Columbia), secondary). Separately, the August 2025 **Cowichan Tribes** ruling declared Aboriginal title over part of the Tl'uqtinus lands in Richmond, including privately owned parcels; it rests on section 35 of the Constitution, predates DRIPA, and is under appeal ([BLG](https://www.blg.com/en/insights/2026/06/recent-developments-in-aboriginal-title-litigation)).

The parties now split three ways. **Doerkson: "I will repeal DRIPA,"** arguing it "has not created the security for British Columbians that it was meant to achieve" and that the province is "in court everywhere"; the party page promises partnerships with First Nations but names no replacement and does not address existing consent agreements or private property ([Conservative plan](https://conservativebc.ca/plan/first-nations-dripa/); [The Narwhal](https://thenarwhal.ca/bc-election-2026-dripa/)). **OneBC** goes further: repeal DRIPA, declare UNDRIP of no effect in BC, stop voluntary land and resource transfers to bands, and seek constitutional change to repeal the Indian Act ([OneBC](https://1bc.ca/priorities)). **Eby** says "we do have to respond to that court decision, it's not up for debate," but has published no amendment text, timeline or consent position ([The Narwhal](https://thenarwhal.ca/bc-election-2026-dripa/)). The **Greens** would legislate DRIPA implementation, fund public education on it and create an expert advisory committee; Lowan said a government "willing to weaken Indigenous rights for political expediency cannot expect our support" ([BC Ballot DRIPA explainer](https://bcballot.ca/platforms/issues/dripa-and-indigenous-relations/); [CP24](https://www.cp24.com/news/canada/2026/02/09/bc-greens-wont-renew-accord-with-ndp-citing-stalled-undelivered-promises/)). **CentreBC's** only formal statement, issued in August under then-leader Mike Bernier, calls repeal "more about political messaging than practical solutions" and proposes a single provincial framework for treaties and reconciliation agreements; Sturko has not been found on record ([CentreBC](https://www.centrebc.ca/news/statement-reconciliation-certainty-and-prosperity/)).

Both sides have serious arguments. Repeal supporters cite investment uncertainty and property-owner anxiety after Cowichan; Angus Reid found **47% support repeal and 33% oppose** ([Angus Reid](https://angusreid.org/85168-2/)). The FNLC called unilateral suspension "a unilateral betrayal," the BC Assembly of First Nations asked parties to avoid "fearmongering," and Nanwakolas Council president Dallas Smith said repeal alone would not give industry the clarity it wants ([The Narwhal](https://thenarwhal.ca/bc-election-2026-dripa/)). Chawathil chief Aaron Pete argued from a middle position that leaving DRIPA unchanged is not credible but repeal alone solves nothing ([Resource Works](https://resourceworks.com/dripa-reconciliation-promises-to-be-a-top-election-issue/)). A fact that cuts across the debate: repealing DRIPA would **not end the constitutional duty to consult** or stop title litigation such as Cowichan, which is grounded in section 35 ([The Narwhal](https://thenarwhal.ca/bc-declaration-act-explainer/)). A citizen initiative petition to repeal DRIPA opens for signatures on October 26 ([Resource Works](https://resourceworks.com/dripa-reconciliation-promises-to-be-a-top-election-issue/)).

### SOGI 123: a resource at the centre of a larger culture fight

SOGI 123 is an **optional teaching resource, not a curriculum**, created by the ARC Foundation with the Ministry of Education in 2016 under the BC Liberal government, after the Human Rights Code added gender identity as a protected ground; school districts choose whether to use it ([SOGI Education](https://www.sogieducation.org/); [CBC via Yahoo](https://ca.news.yahoo.com/where-b-c-provincial-political-130000614.html)). The NDP government cut ARC's provincial funding from $350,000 to $200,000 in 2025/26 and **to zero in the 2026/27 budget**, while the ministry says the resources remain online ([Castanet](https://www.castanetkamloops.net/news/Kamloops/627143/SD73-schools-wont-feel-change-as-province-ends-funding-for-ARC-Foundation-superintendent)).

**Doerkson: "SOGI has to be repealed"** "while providing a safe place for our kids," calling it "an absolute lightning rod for division" and pledging to refocus on reading and arithmetic; the page gives no mechanism ([Conservative plan](https://conservativebc.ca/plan/safe-schools/)). The 2024 Conservative platform went further, pledging to remove SOGI-derived lesson plans and policy guides and to notify parents immediately of a child's suicidal intentions ([Global News](https://globalnews.ca/news/10812384/affordability-safety-scrapping-carbon-price-bc-conservatives-full-platform)). **OneBC** would repeal SOGI 123, "remove all gender ideology content" and enact a Parental Bill of Rights ([OneBC](https://1bc.ca/priorities)). **Eby** ended the relationship with ARC but says anti-bullying work continues with teachers in the lead ([CBC via Yahoo](https://ca.news.yahoo.com/where-b-c-provincial-political-130000614.html)). The **Greens** "unequivocally support SOGI 123," and **Sturko** says every student "deserves to feel safe and respected and included" and opposes repealing anti-discrimination protections, without committing to SOGI 123 by name (same source). The BC Teachers' Federation calls it one optional tool among many.

Legislative votes show where caucuses lean, with a caveat. In February 2026 most Conservative MLAs voted to allow first reading of independent MLA Tara Armstrong's **Gender Ideology and Child Protection Act** (defeated 38-49) and **Human Rights Code Repeal Act** (defeated 37-50); NDP and Green MLAs and Sturko voted no ([Votes and Proceedings Feb 19](https://lims.leg.bc.ca/pdms/ldp/43rd2nd/votes/v260219.htm); [Feb 26](https://lims.leg.bc.ca/pdms/ldp/43rd2nd/votes/v260226.htm)). Conservatives describe first-reading votes as a procedural courtesy, and at least one MLA said she had not read the bill ([Rocky Mountain Goat](https://therockymountaingoat.com/2026/03/bird-conservatives-vote-to-give-first-reading-to-human-rights-code-repeal-act/)). Sturko cited that Human Rights Code vote as the reason she cannot return to the Conservatives ([Penticton Western News](https://pentictonwesternnews.com/2026/09/22/surreys-elenore-sturko-is-the-sole-incumbent-mla-for-centrebc-ahead-of-election/)). **No party has a 2026 position on pronoun policy, parental notification of name changes, or trans athletes in schools**, so a voter tool cannot score those sub-issues.

## Drug policy has converged everywhere except the Greens

BC declared a public health emergency in 2016. Toxic-drug deaths peaked at **2,511 in 2023**, fell to **1,826 (preliminary) in 2025**, and reached **109 in May 2026**, the lowest monthly count since February 2020 ([BCCSU](https://www.bccsu.ca/blog/news/toxic-drug-deaths-in-b-c-hit-record-high-last-year-coroner-says/); [The Free Press](https://thefreepress.ca/2026/02/20/1826-people-died-from-toxic-drugs-in-b-c-in-2025-a-21-decrease-from-2024/); [BC Government](https://archive.news.gov.bc.ca/releases/news_releases_2024-2028/2026PSSG0042-000540.htm)). The coroner does not attribute the decline to any policy, and a federal study found BC's decriminalization was not a factor in the national 2024 decline ([UBCM chronology](https://www.ubcm.ca/node/106160)). Any party claiming credit for the drop is making a contested causal claim.

The NDP's own record is the story of the past three years. It launched **decriminalization** of small amounts in January 2023, banned public use in 2024, and let the pilot **expire on January 31, 2026**, with Health Minister Josie Osborne saying it "hasn't delivered the results" and Eby saying "it didn't work" ([National Newswatch/CP](https://nationalnewswatch.com/2026/01/14/b-c-ending-its-3-year-drug-decriminalization-pilot-project)). It moved prescribed safer supply to **witnessed dosing** for all patients by December 2025, shrinking the program from over 5,000 to about 2,200 people ([BCCSU](https://www.bccsu.ca/blog/news/b-c-to-require-most-drug-users-receiving-safe-supply-to-have-their-doses-witnessed/)). It expanded **involuntary care**, with 132 beds in progress including **60 in Surrey** ([CKPG](https://ckpgtoday.ca/2026/07/10/premier-announces-location-of-prince-george-involuntary-care-facility/)), but a BC Supreme Court judge ruled in July 2026 that Mental Health Act treatment provisions are unconstitutional and gave the province six months to amend them ([Global News](https://globalnews.ca/news/12005744/sections-bc-involuntary-care-rules-found-unconstitutional/)). In the campaign Eby promised involuntary care in the Kelowna region and said supervised consumption sites "save lives" but must be "well run" ([Kelowna Capital News](https://kelownacapnews.com/2026/10/02/eby-announces-b-c-grocery-price-guard-social-disorder-during-kelowna-visit/)).

The **Conservatives** would end "safe supply," take people with severe addiction, mental illness or brain injury into care through "compassionate intervention," amend the Mental Health Act so police can connect people to mandatory treatment, and end drug use in hospitals, shelters and supportive housing; the 2026 plan is silent on supervised consumption sites, which the 2024 platform pledged to close ([Conservative Safe Streets](https://conservativebc.ca/safe-streets-for-bc/); [CBC 2024](https://amp.cbc.ca/lite/story/1.7331415)). **OneBC** would end safe supply and consumption programs, convert sites to recovery centres and use involuntary rehab ([OneBC](https://1bc.ca/priorities)). The **Greens** are alone in proposing to **restore decriminalization**: their policy brief says they will treat drug use "as a health and social issue, not a criminal one, by decriminalising possession of drugs for personal use," add regulated pharmaceutical alternatives and expand supervised and inhalation sites, with care framed as voluntary ([BC Greens brief](https://bcgreens.ca/wp-content/uploads/2026/10/BC-Greens-Toxic-Drugs-Policy-Brief-2026-1.pdf)). **CentreBC** has no drug policy; Sturko's record as an MLA supports compassionate involuntary care and opposed non-medical safer supply ([Cloverdale Reporter](https://www.cloverdalereporter.com/news/surrey-south-mla-elenore-sturko-introduces-bill-aimed-at-helping-prevent-suicides/); [JURIST](https://www.jurist.org/news/2026/03/canada-dispatch-british-columbias-failed-drug-decriminalization-pilot-leaves-toxic-crisis-unresolved/)).

The evidence debate is genuine. Harm-reduction researchers estimate supervised sites prevented 3,945 deaths in 2023 ([BCCSU](https://www.bccsu.ca/blog/news/b-c-conservatives-vow-to-shut-down-safe-consumption-sites/)), and BC's Human Rights Commissioner criticized expanding involuntary treatment while scaling back harm reduction ([Filter](https://filtermag.org/bc-human-rights-overdose-inaction/)); police chiefs and the government cite public disorder and diversion of prescribed drugs as reasons for the shift ([UBCM](https://www.ubcm.ca/node/106160)).

### Public safety: numbers on one side, silence on the other

BC's Crime Severity Index fell to **92.4 in 2025** from 104.6 in 2023, but the violent crime index rose 2.2% in 2025 and is up about 32% since 2017 according to the market-oriented Fraser Institute ([Fraser Institute](https://www.fraserinstitute.org/commentary/crime-stats-belie-bc-governments-safer-province-claims); [StatCan](https://www150.statcan.gc.ca/n1/daily-quotidien/260722/cg-a003-eng.htm)). Bail and sentencing are federal law; provinces fund police, prosecutors and courts. Surrey extortion is the most visible local crime issue: **133 reported cases with 49 shootings in 2025**, and **142 cases but 21 shootings in 2026 through September 28** ([Surrey Police Service](https://www.surreypolice.ca/extortions)).

The **Conservatives** have the most specific plan: 250 more police through the Justice Institute, 500 RCMP officers and 150 civilian staff over five years in rural and regional areas, 50 Crown prosecutors, help funding 20 federal trial judges, and public reporting on repeat offending and bail compliance, with no costs and no mention of extortion ([Conservative Safe Streets](https://conservativebc.ca/safe-streets-for-bc/); [Langley Advance Times](https://langleyadvancetimes.com/2026/10/06/b-c-conservatives-plan-to-hire-more-police-stop-illegal-drug-use-in-hospitals-shelters/)). The **NDP's** published items cover security funding for the Jewish community and an extremism summit; Eby also said he wants an RCMP prolific-property-offender program expanded province-wide ([BC NDP](https://www.bcndp.ca/releases/bc-ndp-commits-jewish-community-security-investment-and-summit-confront-organized-hate); [Kelowna Capital News](https://kelownacapnews.com/2026/10/02/eby-announces-b-c-grocery-price-guard-social-disorder-during-kelowna-visit/)). **CentreBC's** anti-extortion plan proposes a FINTRAC-RCMP-CBSA cell in Surrey, $2,000-$5,000 security micro-grants, a 48-hour victim protection standard and a dedicated court docket ([CentreBC](https://centrebc.ca/news/news-release/centrebc-unveils-3-step-plan-to-crush-transnational-extortion-terrorizing-surrey/)). **OneBC** targets repeat violent offenders, would ban masks at protests and keep BC out of federal firearms buybacks ([OneBC](https://1bc.ca/priorities)). The **Greens have published no public-safety commitment** ([BC Ballot](https://bcballot.ca/platforms/bc-green-party/)).

## Pocketbook plans differ mainly on who pays

### Housing: taxes on empty units versus cutting costs

Metro Vancouver's housing market has softened: purpose-built rental vacancy hit **3.7% in 2025**, the highest in over 30 years, rent growth was the slowest in 20 years, and Surrey one-bedroom asking rents fell about 12% year over year ([CMHC](https://www.cmhc-schl.gc.ca/professionals/housing-markets-data-and-research/market-reports/rental-market-reports-major-centres); [BC Government](https://news.gov.bc.ca/releases/2025HMA0111-001096)). Starts held near the ten-year average at **44,193 in 2025** but CMHC forecasts declines to 2028 ([BC Budget 2026](https://www.bcbudget.gov.bc.ca/2026/pdf/2026_Budget_and_Fiscal_Plan.pdf); [Business Examiner](https://businessexaminer.ca/be-magazine/item/cmhc-summer-2026-outlook-vancouver-housing-starts-condo/)). Homelessness rose: the 2025 Metro Vancouver count found 5,232 people, up 9%, with unsheltered homelessness up 30% ([Global News](https://globalnews.ca/news/11311816/metro-vancouver-2025-homeless-count)).

The NDP record is demand-side taxes plus provincial zoning mandates. Its **speculation and vacancy tax passed 43-39 in 2018** with Green support and BC Liberal opposition ([Votes and Proceedings](https://lims.leg.bc.ca/pdms/ldp/41st3rd/votes/v181122.htm)); **Bill 44** (up to four units on single-family lots) passed 45-29 in 2023 with BC United and both Greens opposed, while **Bill 47** (density near transit) passed unanimously ([Votes and Proceedings Nov 29](https://lims.leg.bc.ca/pdms/ldp/42nd4th/votes/v231129.htm); [Nov 30](https://lims.leg.bc.ca/pdms/ldp/42nd4th/votes/v231130.htm)). A home-flipping tax took effect in 2025 and BC Builds finances middle-income rentals ([BC Budget 2026](https://www.bcbudget.gov.bc.ca/2026/pdf/2026_Budget_and_Fiscal_Plan.pdf)).

For 2026 the **NDP** would raise the speculation tax from 1% to 2% for Canadians and from 4% to 5% for foreign owners, and add an **Unsold Condo Tax** starting at 2% in a unit's second unsold year, with no unit target and no revenue estimate ([BC NDP](https://www.bcndp.ca/releases/eby-turn-more-empty-condos-homes-people-can-afford)). The **Conservatives** have one housing line, to "bring down the cost of building and buying a home," plus a plan to strip Metro Vancouver's housing role back to the province and cities; Doerkson says "every new tax on housing ends up in the price a family pays" ([Conservative statement](https://conservativebc.ca/statement-from-interim-leader-lorne-doerkson-on-the-ndps-housing-tax-plan/); [Daily Hive](https://dailyhive.com/vancouver/bc-conservatives-metro-vancouver-regional-district-reform-platform-promise)). Their 2024 rent and mortgage tax rebate, rezoning deadlines and Bill 44 changes have **not been repeated in 2026** and should not be attributed to the current platform ([BC Ballot](https://bcballot.ca/platforms/conservative-party/)). The **Greens** would build or protect 26,000 permanently affordable non-market homes a year and introduce vacancy control, which ties allowable rent increases to the unit rather than the tenant ([BC Greens](https://bcgreens.ca/our-plan/); [Daily Hive](https://dailyhive.com/vancouver/bc-greens-vacancy-control-rentals)). **OneBC** would scrap the Step Code energy rules and block municipal rent control; **CentreBC** says only that policy should be tied to affordability outcomes ([BC Ballot](https://bcballot.ca/platforms/)).

### Cost of living and inequality: three tax philosophies

BC had the **highest provincial poverty rate in Canada in 2024 at 13.0%** by the Market Basket Measure, although the government reports overall poverty down 29% and child poverty down 36% since 2016 ([The Tyee](https://www.thetyee.ca/News/2026/05/18/BC-Poverty-Reduction-Plan-Working/)). The minimum wage is **$18.25** and indexed to inflation by law ([BC Government](https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/employment-business/employment-standards-advice/employment-standards/wages/minimum-wage)).

The **NDP** proposal, effective 2027, raises the rate on income between $190,405 and $265,545 from 16.8% to 18.8%, between $265,545 and $1 million from 20.5% to 22.5%, and adds a **24.5% bracket above $1 million**; nothing changes below $190,405, the party says 3.88% of earners are affected, and revenue reaches $1 billion in 2028/29 ([BC NDP](https://www.bcndp.ca/releases/build-bc-strong-eby-will-ask-wealthy-pitch-bit-more); [Daily Hive](https://dailyhive.com/vancouver/david-eby-bc-ndp-higher-income-taxes-top-earners-platform)). That pitch omits what the NDP government already did in Budget 2026: it **raised the lowest bracket from 5.06% to 5.60%** for every taxpayer and **froze bracket indexation for 2027-2030**, which raises taxes at all incomes as inflation rises ([BC Government](https://www2.gov.bc.ca/budgettaxchanges)). The NDP also promises to suspend 10 cents a litre of fuel tax with a BC Utilities Commission "price guard" on gas margins, cap retail margins on dairy, eggs and poultry, and freeze ICBC rates to 2030 ([BC NDP](https://www.bcndp.ca/releases/ebys-plan-will-cut-gas-prices-30-cents-litre-crack-down-gouging); [BC NDP](https://www.bcndp.ca/releases/eby-freeze-car-insurance-rates-another-three-years)). Its 2024 promise of a $1,000 grocery rebate was paused in February 2025, which opponents cite on credibility ([Global News](https://globalnews.ca/news/11017886/bc-pauses-tax-rebate-donald-trump-tariffs/amp)).

The **Conservatives** pledge **no new taxes and no tax increases** for the full term and would raise the basic personal amount from $13,216 to **$32,000**, worth about $1,050 a year to anyone earning roughly $40,000 or more and removing provincial income tax for full-time minimum-wage workers ([Conservative release](https://conservativebc.ca/conservatives-will-cut-your-taxes-by-1000/); [Global News](https://globalnews.ca/news/12075070/bc-election-september-27-conservative-platform/)). Because the credit is non-refundable, people who already owe no provincial tax gain nothing, and the benefit is flat rather than rising with income ([Daily Hive](https://dailyhive.com/vancouver/bc-conservatives-election-annual-income-tax-cut-promise)). The party has published **no cost**; the only estimates, about $2.5 billion a year, come from an NDP candidate and a reporter's calculation ([Medicine Hat News/CP](https://medicinehatnews.com/news/national-news/2026/10/08/b-c-campaign-trail-conservatives-promise-1000-tax-cut-by-boosting-exemption-level/)). It would also remove PST on Canadian alcohol during the trade war and replace ICBC's no-fault model with a "hybrid" restoring court recovery for serious injuries, with no premium estimate ([Global News](https://globalnews.ca/news/12090331/bc-conservatives-plan-no-fault-insurance-repeal/)).

The **Greens'** **Fair Share Act** would levy a one-time 5% tax on net wealth above $50 million (10% above $100 million, 20% above $1 billion), then 2-5% annually; the party projects $22.2 billion in year one and $5.4 billion a year after, affecting about 0.1% of families, but publishes no calculation, valuation method or response to relocation risk ([BC Greens](https://bcgreens.ca/fair-share-act-would-unlock-22-2b-for-childcare-transit-and-housing/); [Daily Hive](https://dailyhive.com/vancouver/bc-greens-wealth-tax-fair-share-act)). They would also raise social and disability assistance to the poverty line. **OneBC** would cut every bracket, including corporate, by 25% and cut PST by 2 points; **CentreBC** would remove PST on farm equipment and food production ([OneBC](https://1bc.ca/priorities); [CentreBC](https://www.centrebc.ca/news/budget-2026-managing-decline-is-not-leadership/)).

### Fiscal: no party shows a path to balance

BC ran surpluses as recently as 2022/23 but Budget 2026 projected a **$13.3 billion deficit**, raised to **$13.8 billion** in September, with taxpayer-supported debt rising to **37.4% of GDP by 2028/29** and interest consuming 8.2 cents of every revenue dollar ([BC Budget 2026](https://www.bcbudget.gov.bc.ca/2026/pdf/2026_Budget_and_Fiscal_Plan.pdf); [BC Government](https://news.gov.bc.ca/releases/2026FIN0031-001066)). All four rating agencies downgraded the province in March and April 2026; Moody's cited "limited progress and visibility toward balanced budgets" ([Moody's](https://www2.gov.bc.ca/assets/gov/british-columbians-our-governments/government-finances/debt-management/moodys-ratings-march-2026.pdf); [DBRS](https://www2.gov.bc.ca/assets/gov/british-columbians-our-governments/government-finances/debt-management/dbrs-rating-report.pdf)). Eby defended the budget as a choice not to cut services "to meet a credit rating" ([National Newswatch](https://nationalnewswatch.com/2026/03/19/premier-eby-defends-budget-after-credit-drop-but-opposition-says-b-c-is-going-broke)). The NDP plan keeps deficits above $11 billion through 2028/29 and cuts about **15,000 public-sector positions** ([BC Budget 2026](https://www.bcbudget.gov.bc.ca/2026/pdf/2026_Budget_and_Fiscal_Plan.pdf)). The Conservatives voted against the 2025 Supply Act and the 2026 budget bill, but their 2026 materials give **no deficit target or balance date** and rely on growth from LNG, forestry and mining; their 2024 aim to balance "within two terms" has not been repeated ([Votes and Proceedings](https://lims.leg.bc.ca/pdms/ldp/43rd2nd/votes/v260305.htm); [Daily Hive](https://dailyhive.com/vancouver/bc-conservatives-election-annual-income-tax-cut-promise)). Only **OneBC** promises balance, within four years, while also promising the largest tax cuts, uncosted ([BC Ballot](https://bcballot.ca/platforms/)).

### Economy and US tariffs: two routes to the same LNG

Growth is weak: the province forecasts **0.9% real GDP growth in 2026**, unemployment was 6.5% in August and employment is down 0.6% this year, though non-US exports rose 16% ([BC Government](https://news.gov.bc.ca/releases/2026FIN0031-001066)). Budget 2026 assumes US tariffs of 35% on non-CUSMA goods, 50% on steel, aluminum and copper, and an extra 10% on softwood ([BC Budget 2026](https://www.bcbudget.gov.bc.ca/2026/pdf/2026_Budget_and_Fiscal_Plan.pdf)). The NDP's tariff response, **Bill 7**, passed 48-44 in May 2025 after the government dropped a section letting cabinet amend laws by order ([Votes and Proceedings](https://lims.leg.bc.ca/pdms/ldp/43rd1st/votes/v250528.htm); [BCBC](https://www.bcbc.com/news/statement-on-amendments-to-bill-7)). Its fast-track project laws, **Bills 14 and 15**, passed only on the Speaker's casting vote after a 46-46 tie, with Conservatives, Greens and independents opposed and First Nations leaders objecting to the lack of consultation (same source; [Global News](https://globalnews.ca/news/11199658/bc-bill-15-critics/)).

The **NDP** now promises "Built in B.C. and Canada" procurement rules, doubling critical-mineral exports in five years, cutting mine approval timelines by over 60%, a $500 million tariff and wildfire relief loan fund, and a $5,000 youth hiring credit ([BC Ballot](https://bcballot.ca/platforms/bc-ndp/)). The **Conservatives** promise to **double natural gas production by 2032 and triple it by 2035**, a 120-day permitting law for projects over $250 million, red tape back to 2017 levels with a "one-in, two-out" rule, and a three-year PST exemption on business machinery and software ([Conservative release](https://conservativebc.ca/make-british-columbia-an-energy-superpower/); [Conservative release](https://conservativebc.ca/supercharge-british-columbias-economy/)). The permitting law resembles in aim the Bill 15 fast-track the party voted against. The **Greens** want Buy BC procurement, an end to raw-log exports, oil and gas royalty reform and a windfall tax; **OneBC** wants a higher allowable cut, a pipeline to Prince Rupert or Kitimat and a Western Canada alliance ([BC Ballot](https://bcballot.ca/platforms/)). LNG Canada Phase 2 reached a final investment decision in late September, a project both main parties claim ([CTV/CP](https://www.ctvnews.ca/vancouver/article/ebys-election-campaign-gets-lift-from-lng-canada-announcement/)).

## Services and environment show smaller gaps than the rhetoric

**Health care.** About **1,259,425 British Columbians (23%) lacked a regular primary-care provider** at the end of April 2026, up from 18.2% in 2017, even as a new family-doctor payment model raised average gross pay and the government reports 4,000 attachments a week ([The Tyee](https://thetyee.ca/News/2026/05/31/BC-Family-Doctor-Crisis-Gets-Even-Worse/)). BC was first in Canada to set minimum nurse-patient ratios ([BCNU](https://www.bcnu.org/news-and-events/news/2026/minimum-nurse-patient-ratios-focus-bc-nurses-conference)). The **NDP** promises 20 primary-care clinics, fast-tracking international medical graduates, 1,000 more training seats, 8 MRIs and 10 CT scanners, coordinated surgical waitlists, 500 home-care workers and an end to home-care fees, funded by the top-bracket tax ([Global News](https://globalnews.ca/news/12097726/bc-leaders-campaign-trail-health-care-supporting-seniors-october-9); [BC NDP](https://www.bcndp.ca/releases/getting-care-sooner-eby-will-cut-wait-times-add-new-diagnostic-equipment)). The **Conservatives** promise to keep ERs open, a $50 million rural fund, a 100-day expert commission on waits, health funding held at 2026 levels and care that stays "universal and publicly funded"; unlike 2024, the 2026 release contains no private-clinic plan, but their seniors plan would let long-term-care funding follow patients to non-government providers ([Conservative release](https://conservativebc.ca/keep-emergency-rooms-open/); [Sitka Media](https://www.sitkamedia.ca/watch-bc-conservatives-unveil-plan-to-support-seniors/)). The **Greens** want a community health centre in every riding and public-only delivery; **OneBC** would allow private care alongside public; **CentreBC** emphasizes home care and long-term care ([BC Ballot](https://bcballot.ca/platforms/)).

**Climate and energy.** BC is **not on track for its legislated 2030 target** of 40% below 2007; the province projected only about 20% by 2030 ([The Tyee](https://thetyee.ca/News/2025/05/01/BC-Admits-Climate-Goals/)). The NDP ended the **consumer carbon tax on April 1, 2025**, kept industrial carbon pricing, paused EV rebates and dropped the 2035 EV sales mandate as "no longer realistic" ([CBC](https://www.cbc.ca/lite/story/1.7493181); [National Newswatch](https://nationalnewswatch.com/2025/11/18/b-c-to-redraw-ev-sales-mandate-scrapping-goal-of-100-per-cent-by-2035)). Both main parties back LNG expansion; the Conservatives have **no 2026 statement on industrial carbon pricing, the Low Carbon Fuel Standard or CleanBC**, though Rustad called in 2025 for the industrial tax to go ([Western Standard](https://www.westernstandard.news/amp/story/watch/watch-rustad-calls-on-bc-ndp-to-axe-industrial-carbon-tax/63616)). The **Greens** would stop new fossil-fuel development and oppose the oil pipeline; CentreBC supports "evidence-based action" and a conditional pipeline ([BC Greens](https://bcgreens.ca/our-plan/); [CentreBC](https://www.centrebc.ca/news/ottawa-and-bc-just-moved-toward-centrebc-pipeline-conditions/)).

**Education and child care.** Budget 2026 **froze new $10-a-day child-care enrolment for three years**, a "stabilization" the minister said responds to a system families experience "like a lottery" ([Global News](https://globalnews.ca/news/11673463/10-a-day-daycare-program-paused/)). The NDP has no 2026 education or child-care commitment; the Greens would lift the freeze and reach universal free child care by 2031; CentreBC calls child care "economic infrastructure"; the Conservatives' only education pledge is SOGI repeal ([BC Greens](https://bcgreens.ca/announce-plan-to-lift-the-freeze-on-10-a-day-daycare-with-universal-free-child-care-by-2031-alongside-new-candidate-for-victoria-swan-lake/); [BC Ballot](https://bcballot.ca/platforms/)). Surrey schools carry the province's heaviest growth: the district projects 97,069 students by 2033 and the NDP's 2017 promise to remove portables by 2020 was not met ([Global News](https://globalnews.ca/news/11350841/surrey-schools-ndp-building)).

**Transit.** The **Surrey-Langley SkyTrain** is under construction at $5.996 billion for late-2029 opening ([SLS status report](https://surreylangleyskytrain.gov.bc.ca/app/uploads/sites/938/2026/06/SLS_Monthly-Status-Report_April_2026.pdf)). Both main parties ruled out a TransLink vehicle levy, leaving TransLink's 2028 funding gap of at least $112 million a year unresolved ([Daily Hive](https://dailyhive.com/vancouver/bc-ndp-david-eby-translink-vehicle-levy)). The Greens promise "fast, frequent, and free" transit, starting with free passes for under-25s and over-65s; the Conservatives have no transit commitment ([Global News](https://globalnews.ca/news/12097726/bc-leaders-campaign-trail-health-care-supporting-seniors-october-9)). Only the **Greens** promise **proportional representation** legislation within 100 days; voters rejected PR 61.3% to 38.7% in 2018 ([BC Greens](https://bcgreens.ca/our-plan/); [CBC](https://www.cbc.ca/news/canada/british-columbia/electoral-reform-referendum-results-1.4954538)).

## Ten-year records predict how each party would govern

**The NDP (in government since 2017)** has used provincial power more aggressively than any recent BC government: taxes on speculation and flipping, province-wide zoning overrides, a forced Surrey police transition (Bill 36, 56-27 in 2023), fast-track project laws and cabinet tariff powers ([Votes and Proceedings](https://lims.leg.bc.ca/pdms/ldp/42nd4th/votes/v231025.htm)). It has also reversed itself repeatedly under pressure: removing Bill 7's override clause, pausing the $1,000 rebate, scrapping the carbon tax it once defended, ending decriminalization, restricting safer supply, dropping the EV mandate, shelving the DRIPA suspension and ruling out the vehicle levy (each cited above). **Inference:** a re-elected NDP would most likely bring a negotiated DRIPA amendment in its first session, keep supervised consumption sites and witnessed-only safer supply, expand involuntary care while meeting the January 2027 court deadline, continue the Surrey police transition and SkyTrain build, and keep deficits above $10 billion. Uncosted late-campaign promises carry delivery risk given the paused 2024 rebate.

**The Conservatives (official opposition since 2024)** voted against nearly every NDP bill in 2025-26, including Bills 7, 14 and 15, the 2025 Supply Act and the 2026 budget bill, and supported first reading of OneBC and independent bills on property rights, gender identity and Human Rights Code repeal ([Votes and Proceedings](https://lims.leg.bc.ca/pdms/ldp/43rd1st/votes/v251029.htm)). Many current Conservative figures were BC Liberals who voted for DRIPA in 2019, and SOGI 123 began under the BC Liberal government; the party's current repeal stance is therefore a departure from its predecessors, which Rustad explained by saying he had "no choice" in 2019 ([The Tyee](https://thetyee.ca/News/2026/02/10/First-Nations-Would-Not-Exist-Without-Canada-Rustad/)). **Inference:** DRIPA repeal and SOGI repeal are the highest-probability early bills because they appear in the 2024 platform, under three successive leaders and in 2026; repeal would not end title litigation. The tax cut plus "no new taxes" plus "no health cuts" implies larger deficits unless growth or unnamed spending cuts close the gap. A Conservative government would also face governance risk: an interim leader who needs a constitutional change to stay on, and a caucus that shrank from 44 to as few as 24 before regrouping ([CP24](https://www.cp24.com/politics/2026/09/20/kerry-lynne-findlay-resigns-as-bc-conservative-leader/)).

**The Greens** supported the NDP on confidence under formal accords (2017-2020 and December 2024-February 2026), extracting the 2017 donations ban and a PR referendum, and then voted against bills they saw as weak on oversight or climate, including Bills 14 and 15, the carbon tax repeal and the 2026 budget bill at third reading ([Votes and Proceedings](https://lims.leg.bc.ca/pdms/ldp/43rd2nd/votes/v260305.htm)). They let the 2024 accord lapse, saying nearly two-thirds of first-year commitments stalled ([CP24](https://www.cp24.com/news/canada/2026/02/09/bc-greens-wont-renew-accord-with-ndp-citing-stalled-undelivered-promises/)). **Inference:** Green influence depends on a minority result; in that case a deal with the NDP is more plausible than with the Conservatives, whose DRIPA and SOGI positions the Greens reject.

**OneBC** has a short record of private members' bills on gender transition, land acknowledgements and property rights, all defeated, and an unstable leadership (Brodie was removed by the board in December 2025 and reinstated a week later) ([Wikipedia: OneBC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/OneBC_(political_party))). It is standing aside for eight Conservative candidates who share its SOGI views ([CTV](https://www.ctvnews.ca/vancouver/article/onebc-pledges-not-to-run-against-eight-principled-bc-conservative-candidates/)). **CentreBC** was founded in March 2025, has had four leaders, and its positions should be treated as provisional until its platform is read ([Wikipedia: CentreBC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CentreBC)).

## Conclusion

The election offers fewer real choices than the campaign noise suggests, and a fair voter-advice tool should weight where parties genuinely differ. On decriminalization, the consumer carbon tax, LNG growth and the vehicle levy, the NDP has moved so far toward its opponents' 2024 positions that a question on those topics barely separates the two largest parties; it separates the Greens from everyone else. The questions that do sort voters cleanly are DRIPA (repeal, amend or implement), SOGI 123 (remove or keep), who should pay more tax (top earners, the very wealthy, or nobody), and the trade-off between police and enforcement versus harm reduction. Health care and housing, the issues voters rank highest, divide the parties more by method (public clinics versus commissions, empty-unit taxes versus deregulation) than by goal, so questions there should probe method.

Two forward-looking risks deserve equal weight with stated positions. The NDP's decade shows a government that announces boldly and retreats when a constituency pushes back, so its uncosted promises and an unpublished DRIPA amendment carry real uncertainty about final form. The Conservatives offer clearer commitments but no costing, no balance date and a leader who may not survive the leadership rules, so their fiscal promises carry the larger arithmetic risk. Platforms are still arriving (CentreBC's on October 10, a full Green platform pending, possible NDP and Conservative costings before advance voting on October 16), and the tool should be refreshed before it is used. In Surrey-Fleetwood, where a few hundred votes decided 2024 and two right-of-centre alternatives to the Conservatives are on the ballot, a voter's local choice is in practice a choice of party.
