## Housing

### Takeaway
BC prices and rents have softened in 2025-26 (Metro Vancouver composite benchmark roughly 20% above its Dec 2016 level in nominal terms, but falling about 5-6% year over year in 2026; purpose-built rental vacancy at a 30-plus-year high of 3.7%), while housing starts have stayed near the 10-year average and are forecast to decline. Homelessness counts rose in 2025. The NDP's 2026 housing offer is more taxes on empty/unsold units; the Greens offer renter protections and non-market housing; the Conservatives have released only a general "bring down the cost" statement in 2026 (their 2024 platform was a rent/mortgage rebate, scrapping the speculation tax and zoning mandates), and OneBC offers scrapping the Step Code and blocking municipal rent control.

### Cited Findings

#### Data and trends 2016-2026
- Metro Vancouver (REBGV) MLS HPI composite benchmark: $897,600 in Dec 2016 (up 17.8% year over year); detached $1,483,500, townhome $661,800, apartment $510,300. - [Better Dwelling](https://betterdwelling.com/city/vancouver/vancouver-real-estate-composite-gets-another-downtick-december/); [REBGV Dec 2016 stats package](https://www.realestatenorthshore.com/media/uploads/documents/2017/01/04/12-rebgv-stats-pkg-december-2016.pdf)
- Greater Vancouver benchmark Dec 2025: detached $1,887,800, townhouse $1,056,600 (apartment value truncated in extraction). - Budget 2026 chart 3.7 (PRIMARY): https://www.bcbudget.gov.bc.ca/2026/pdf/2026_Budget_and_Fiscal_Plan.pdf
- September 2026 composite benchmark reported at $1,075,900, down 5.5% year over year; 1,717 sales, 25% below the 10-year seasonal average; inventory 16,394, 24.3% above long-term average. A blog gave a conflicting ~$1.24M (up 2.1%), which conflicts with other 2026 data and should be disregarded. - [CTV News/Canadian Press](https://www.ctvnews.ca/vancouver/article/vancouver-area-home-sales-down-84-per-cent-in-september-as-prices-fall-board/) (SEARCH-LEVEL, not opened)
- Arithmetic from the two readings above: $1,075,900 versus $897,600 is about +20% nominal over ten years (composite, Dec 2016 to Sept 2026); this is my calculation from cited figures, and the two readings are a Dec and a Sept month.
- B.C. in 2025: home sales down 5.7% from 2024 (5,852/month vs 10-year average 7,642); MLS average sale price down 2.9%; Greater Vancouver benchmark down 2.1% and Fraser Valley down 4.2% in 2025. - Budget 2026 (PRIMARY): https://www.bcbudget.gov.bc.ca/2026/pdf/2026_Budget_and_Fiscal_Plan.pdf
- Housing starts: B.C. 44,193 in 2025 (down 3.6% from 2024, above the 10-year average of 43,187); Vancouver CMA starts down 3.3% in 2025; Vancouver and Kelowna home completions at record highs in 2025 (+20.5% and +86.3%). - Budget 2026 (PRIMARY): https://www.bcbudget.gov.bc.ca/2026/pdf/2026_Budget_and_Fiscal_Plan.pdf
- Vancouver CMA starts: 28,112 (2024) and 27,185 (2025); CMHC summer 2026 outlook forecasts further declines with condo construction at historically low levels and no recovery in starts before 2028. - [Business Examiner on CMHC outlook](https://businessexaminer.ca/be-magazine/item/cmhc-summer-2026-outlook-vancouver-housing-starts-condo/) (SEARCH-LEVEL; page returned 403 to me)
- CMHC 2025 Rental Market Report (Vancouver): purpose-built vacancy 3.7%, condo apartments 1.5%; overall vacancy highest in more than 30 years; 2-bedroom purpose-built average rent $2,363 (+2.2%); lowest same-sample rent growth in 20 years; CMHC links softening to federal changes affecting non-permanent residents, new completions and return-to-office. CMHC forecast vacancy 3.6% (2026), 3.5% (2027), 3.4% (2028). - [CMHC rental market reports](https://www.cmhc-schl.gc.ca/professionals/housing-markets-data-and-research/market-reports/rental-market-reports-major-centres); [analysis of CMHC 2025 report](https://morehousing.substack.com/p/cmhc-report-2025) (SEARCH-LEVEL)
- Surrey: CMHC says Surrey rental stock grew significantly in 2025 but leasing got harder, with higher vacancies expected; asking one-bedroom rent in Surrey down 12% year over year (Rentals.ca, Nov 2025, cited by BC government). - [CMHC](https://www.cmhc-schl.gc.ca/professionals/housing-markets-data-and-research/market-reports/rental-market-reports-major-centres); [BC Gov News 2025HMA0111](https://news.gov.bc.ca/releases/2025HMA0111-001096) (SEARCH-LEVEL)
- Surrey detached benchmark approx $1.37M in May 2026 (low-authority aggregator, approximate); Fraser Valley detached benchmark $1,366,500 in May 2026 (-7.9% year over year). - [Wealth North Surrey report](https://wealthnorth.ca/housing/market/surrey/) (SEARCH-LEVEL, low authority)
- Homelessness: Metro Vancouver 2025 point-in-time count (March 10-11, 2025) found 5,232 people, +9% from 4,821 in 2023; unsheltered 1,893, +30%; City of Vancouver 2,715 (highest ever recorded there, +12%); Delta unsheltered +115%; counts are widely understood to be undercounts (a City memo estimates up to 20%). 2020 to 2023 had risen 32% (CTV). - [Global News](https://globalnews.ca/news/11311816/metro-vancouver-2025-homeless-count); [City of Vancouver memo](https://vancouver.ca/files/cov/2026-04-01-memo-to-mayor-council-2025-homeless-count-vancouver.pdf); [CTV 2023](https://bc.ctvnews.ca/metro-vancouver-homeless-count-shows-32-per-cent-increase-since-2020-1.6590402) (SEARCH-LEVEL)

#### Policies tried (actions taken)
- Foreign buyers' property transfer tax: Bill 28 took effect Aug 2, 2016 (BC Liberal government) at 15% in Metro Vancouver, raised to 20% on Feb 21, 2018 (NDP government). - [Wikipedia Bill 28](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bill_28_(British_Columbia)); [Real Estate Coal Harbour](https://realestatecoalharbour.com/blog/bc-foreign-buyer-tax/) (SEARCH-LEVEL; sources differ on the starting rate and the exact initial scope)
- Provincial Speculation and Vacancy Tax (introduced 2018 by NDP). Rates before Budget 2026: 1% domestic / 3% foreign and untaxed worldwide earners (reported as up from 0.5%/2% for 2026). Budget 2026 raises the 3% rate to 4% effective Jan 1, 2027 (revenue +$6M/+$23M/+$23M) and adds a $250 late-declaration penalty. - Budget 2026 (PRIMARY): https://www.bcbudget.gov.bc.ca/2026/pdf/2026_Budget_and_Fiscal_Plan.pdf; [BC Gov News 2025FIN0047](https://news.gov.bc.ca/releases/2025FIN0047-001277) (SEARCH-LEVEL)
- Budget 2026 summary of housing actions (government's own claim): since 2017 more than $19B in operating and capital committed; "estimated 95,000 homes" delivered or in development; BC Builds launched Feb 2024 (middle-income rental on public/non-profit land with low-cost financing); Home Flipping Tax on profits from homes sold within 2 years; small-scale multi-unit housing (townhomes, duplexes, triplexes) via zoning changes; housing targets for over 30 municipalities; PTT exemptions for first-time buyers; annual renter's tax credit up to $400; Rental Assistance Program cap raised to $60,000 (average benefit about $700/month); SAFER cap raised to $40,000 (average about $337/month); over $2B a year in housing spending in Budget 2026. - Budget 2026 (PRIMARY): https://www.bcbudget.gov.bc.ca/2026/pdf/2026_Budget_and_Fiscal_Plan.pdf
- Bill 44 (small-scale multi-unit housing, introduced Nov 1, 2023) requires municipalities to allow more housing on single-family lots. - [BCREA summary](https://www.bcrea.bc.ca/advocacy/provincial-housing-policy-summary/) (SEARCH-LEVEL)
- Budget 2026 total tax measures include expanding the purpose-built rental PTT exemption (newly built, non-stratified rentals of 4+ units held as rentals 10+ years). - Budget 2026 (PRIMARY)
- Budget 2026 reallocated about $1.4B of housing-strategy funding over three years and paused new intakes to the $3.3B Community Housing Fund (launched 2018), per Green-party description; verify with budget documents. - [Daily Hive on Greens](https://dailyhive.com/vancouver/bc-greens-vacancy-control-rentals) (SEARCH-LEVEL)
- Government cleared a nearly 1,000-application housing permit backlog and cut average land/water application times for housing and electrification projects from 322 to 152 days (government claim). - [BC Look West page](https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/employment-business/look-west-strategy/delivering-major-projects-faster) (PRIMARY, updated April 29, 2026)

#### Party 2026 positions (platform/announcements)
- NDP (Sept 25 announcement): raise speculation and vacancy tax from 1% to 2% (domestic) and from 4% to 5% (foreign), put additional revenue into affordable homes; new Unsold Condo Tax on finished condos empty over a year, starting at 2% and rising 1% per additional year unsold. Greens say their 2025 vacancy-control bill was rejected. - [BC Ballot](https://bcballot.ca/platforms/); [BC Greens release](https://bcgreens.ca/announce-plan-to-support-renters-unveil-two-new-candidates-for-south-island/); [Daily Hive](https://dailyhive.com/vancouver/bc-greens-vacancy-control-rentals)
- Conservatives (Sept 25 statement, Doerkson): will "bring down the cost of building and buying a home" - no specifics or figures published. The Conservative "Our Plan" page has no housing section. - [BC Ballot](https://bcballot.ca/platforms/); [Conservative plan page](https://www.conservativebc.ca/plan)
- Conservatives 2024 platform (Rustad era, for record not 2026 promise): income tax rebate for rent/mortgage interest/strata ($1,500/month rising to $3,000 reported, SEARCH-LEVEL figure), scrap the speculation and vacancy tax, short-term rental limits and small-scale density legislation, 6-month rezoning limits. - [Global News](https://globalnews.ca/news/10816468/in-their-words-b-c-party-leaders-affordability-housing); [CBC](https://amp.cbc.ca/lite/story/1.7354687) (SEARCH-LEVEL). I found no 2026 reaffirmation of these.
- OneBC: eliminate the Step Code and its green building requirements; block municipal rent control programs. - [BC Ballot](https://bcballot.ca/platforms/)
- CentreBC: link housing policy to affordability outcomes rather than unit counts (Feb 16, 2026); no figures. - [BC Ballot](https://bcballot.ca/platforms/)
- Conservative interim-leader criticism of the NDP housing tax plan is on the Sept 25 statement cited above; Conservatives also say they would roll Metro Vancouver housing policy back to local/provincial governments (Oct 5). - [BC Ballot](https://bcballot.ca/platforms/)

#### Supporter and critic arguments (for neutral explainer)
- Supporters of vacancy/unsold-condo taxes argue they push units into use and into the rental market (NDP's stated rationale: "turn the empty units into homes"). Critics (Conservatives) argue the 2026 tax package raises costs; developers' groups' reactions were not found. - [BC Ballot](https://bcballot.ca/platforms/); [Daily Hive](https://dailyhive.com/vancouver/david-eby-bc-ndp-snap-election-trump)
- Supporters of vacancy control argue it removes the incentive for no-fault evictions (Greens). Critics of rent control generally (OneBC "disincentivize development") argue it discourages new rental supply. - [BC Ballot](https://bcballot.ca/platforms/)
- Expert critique: BIV and Globe coverage of housing policy and a Policy Options analysis of broad tax-based housing promises exist but I did not obtain 2026-specific independent expert commentary. See Gaps.

#### Plain-language and expert-level explainer anchors (derived from cited facts above)
- Plain: Prices and rents in Metro Vancouver have stopped rising and are falling a bit; more rental homes are empty than usual; fewer new homes are being started; more people are sleeping outside. Parties disagree on whether to tax empty homes harder (NDP), protect renters and build non-market homes (Greens), cut costs/rules to build more (Conservatives, OneBC), or remove environmental building rules (OneBC).
- Expert: Composite benchmark about +20% over ten years nominal; 2025 B.C. starts 44,193 vs 10-year average 43,187; Vancouver CMA purpose-built vacancy 3.7%; policy levers are demand-side taxes (foreign buyer tax, SVT, flipping tax, unsold-condo tax), supply-side zoning mandates (Bill 44, targets), public financing (BC Builds), and tenancy rules (vacancy control).

### Inferences
- Inference: With vacancy at a 30-year high and asking rents falling in Surrey, vacancy control and rent-review proposals bear on a market in which market rents are decreasing; the Greens' rent-review-when-rents-fall idea is aimed at precisely that condition.
- Inference: CMHC's outlook of no starts recovery before 2028 suggests supply-side promises (permitting speed, financing, tax relief) would show results after the next term's first years; none of the 2026 party housing offers were costed.
- Inference: The Conservative Party's 2024 positions (scrap SVT, scrap Bill 44 style density mandates, rent/mortgage rebate) are the best documented guide to housing direction, but the 2026 interim leader has released only a general statement, so the carry-over is unconfirmed.

## Cost of living and inequality (poverty, income tax, minimum wage, property tax, wealth tax, rebates, ICBC, child care)

### Takeaway
BC had the highest provincial poverty rate in Canada in 2024 (13.0%, Market Basket Measure), though the government reports large reductions since 2016. Income tax has been raised on top earners twice under the NDP (2018, 2020) and Budget 2026 raised the lowest rate; the NDP proposes a new millionaire bracket, the Greens propose a one-time and annual wealth tax above $50M, and the Conservatives propose a $32,000 basic personal amount and no new taxes. The consumer carbon tax and its rebate were eliminated Apr 1, 2025. ICBC rates are frozen; the Conservatives and OneBC would end no-fault insurance.

### Cited Findings

#### Poverty, inequality and wages (data)
- Poverty (Market Basket Measure): B.C. 13.0% in 2024, highest among provinces (Ontario 12.5%, Quebec 7%); under-18 rate 12.6%, nearly double 2020. Government report (2023 data): overall poverty down 29.4%, child poverty down 36.2%, seniors' poverty down 20.9% since 2016; 48,000 fewer children in poverty in 2023. Original legislated 2024 targets were a 50% cut in child poverty and 25% in overall poverty; new 2034 targets are 60% overall, 75% children, 50% seniors. The advisory committee credits the federal Canada Child Benefit for most 2015-2019 income-poverty reduction. - [The Tyee](https://www.thetyee.ca/News/2026/05/18/BC-Poverty-Reduction-Plan-Working/) (author Andrew MacLeod, legislative bureau chief; numbers attributed to Statistics Canada and the provincial poverty-reduction report, not opened directly)
- Child poverty (a different measure, after-tax Census Family Low Income Measure): 16.7% of BC children and youth in 2022 (147,570 children). - [First Call BC Report Card 2024](https://firstcallbc.org/wp-content/uploads/2024/12/First_Call_Report_Card_2024_Final-Dec9.pdf) (SEARCH-LEVEL; advocacy coalition)
- Income inequality: recent (2023-24) BC Gini/top-1% data not found. Older data: top 1% income share 10.3% in 2018 with entry threshold $244,800, and paid 22.4% of provincial income tax (Business Council of BC, business association); BC Stats Gini averaged 0.29 in the 1980s-90s and 0.33 in 2000-2009. The Business Council argues inequality has not risen since 2000; CCPA-BC contests this. - [Business Council of BC](https://www.bcbc.com/insight/2021/01/28/a-snapshot-of-b-c-s-top-earners-and-the-income-taxes-they-pay); [The Tyee 2014](https://thetyee.ca/News/2014/01/28/BC-Income-Inequality/); [CCPA-BC](https://www.policyalternatives.ca/news-research/how-the-pie-is-sliced-in-bc/) (SEARCH-LEVEL)
- General minimum wage: $13.85 (June 1, 2019), $14.60 (2020), $15.20 (2021), $15.65 (2022), $16.75 (2023), $17.40 (2024), $17.85 (2025), $18.25 (June 1, 2026, +2.1%). 2019 to 2026 is about +31.8% (my arithmetic). Annual increases tied to prior-year inflation were put into law in 2024. - [BC Government minimum wage page](https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/employment-business/employment-standards-advice/employment-standards/wages/minimum-wage) (PRIMARY); [BC Gov News 2026LBR0021](https://news.gov.bc.ca/releases/2026LBR0021-000581) (SEARCH-LEVEL for the 2024 law)
- Inflation 2.9% in July 2026, forecast to average 2.5% in 2026 and return to 2% in 2027. - [BC Gov News, first quarterly report, Sept 14, 2026](https://news.gov.bc.ca/releases/2026FIN0031-001066) (PRIMARY)

#### Taxes and rebates: actions taken
- Top income tax bracket history: 2018 NDP budget reinstated a higher bracket, 16.8% (up from 14.7%) on income above roughly $150,000-$155,000 (sources differ on the exact threshold); 2020 NDP budget (Feb 18, 2020, Finance Minister Carole James) added a new top rate of 20.5% on taxable income above $220,000 effective Jan 1, 2020 (combined federal-provincial top rate 53.5%, up from 49.8%). - [PwC 2020 BC budget](https://pwc.com/ca/en/services/tax/budgets/2020/british-columbia.html); [CBC](https://amp.cbc.ca/lite/story/1.5467749)
- Budget 2026 (Feb 17, 2026): first income tax bracket raised from 5.06% to 5.60% for 2026 (revenue +$476M/+$493M/+$513M), BC tax reduction credit increased by $115 (taxpayers under $25,570 save up to $690; partial credit up to $44,950); indexation of brackets and non-refundable credits paused 2027-2030 ($60M/$309M/$590M; Alberta did similar in 2019 and Manitoba in 2025); additional school tax on residential property over $3M rises from 0.2% to 0.3% ($3M-$4M) and 0.4% to 0.6% (over $4M) from 2027, affecting an estimated 2.3% of residential folios ($35M/$139M/$142M); PST expansion to accounting, architecture/engineering/geoscience (30% of price), rental/strata management, security services from Oct 1, 2026 ($261M/$534M/$563M); PST exemptions removed for clothing materials, basic cable and landlines; repeal of northern and rural home owner benefit from Jan 1, 2027. Total new tax measures $757M (2026/27), $1,521M (2027/28), $1,937M (2028/29). - Budget 2026 (PRIMARY): https://www.bcbudget.gov.bc.ca/2026/pdf/2026_Budget_and_Fiscal_Plan.pdf
- The PST expansion to professional services was paused by the government on Sept 18, 2026 "amid ongoing trade uncertainty". - [BC Gov News listing, Finance ministry](https://news.gov.bc.ca/releases/2026FIN0031-001066) (PRIMARY listing; the pause release itself was not opened). RBC's separate figure of $787M for higher school tax conflicts with the budget table above and was not used. - [RBC](https://www.rbc.com/en/economics/canadian-analysis/provincial-and-fiscal-outlooks/provincial-budgets-and-economic-statements/b-c-budget-2026-falls-short-on-fiscal-course-correction-amid-challenges/)
- Government statement: through eliminating MSP premiums, the BC Family Benefit, renter's tax credit and tax reduction credit increases, taxpaying British Columbians pay less provincial tax in 2026 than in 2022 at the same income (government claim; the budget notes 40% of taxpayers see net savings after the credit, per RBC). - Budget 2026 (PRIMARY, p.80)

## Fiscal: deficits, debt, credit ratings, fiscal plans and public-sector size

### Takeaway
BC moved from surpluses in 2016/17 to 2018/19 and 2021/22 to 2022/23 to deficits of $13.3B (Budget 2026) and $13.8B (Sept 14, 2026 update) in 2026/27, and taxpayer-supported debt is planned to rise from $46.2B (2019/20) to $189B (2028/29), or from 15.0% to 37.4% of GDP. Four rating agencies downgraded the province in March-April 2026 (Moody's Aa2, S&P A, Fitch AA-, Morningstar DBRS AA). The NDP plan keeps deficits (about $11-12B outer years) with a 15,000-FTE public-sector cut; the Conservatives promise tax cuts without new taxes and have not published a deficit path; the Greens propose new wealth and corporate taxes; OneBC promises balance within four years.

## Economy and US tariffs (trade, forestry, LNG, mining, jobs)

### Takeaway
Budget 2026 assumes 35% US tariffs on non-CUSMA goods, 50% on steel, aluminum and copper, 25% on autos and 10% on softwood on top of existing duties, but B.C. is less exposed than other provinces (nearly half of goods exports go to non-US destinations). Growth is weak: 2026 GDP 0.9%, unemployment 6.5% in August 2026, employment down 0.6% year to date. The NDP leans on the Look West strategy and Buy BC/Canada procurement; the Conservatives on doubling LNG by 2032 and cutting red tape; the Greens on Buy BC, raw-log export limits and royalties; OneBC on tax cuts and a pipeline.

### Cited Findings

#### Tariff assumptions and economic data
- Budget 2026 includes US tariffs enacted as of Jan 12, 2026: 35% on non-CUSMA Canadian exports (10% for energy and potash); 50% on steel, aluminum and copper; 25% on autos (foreign-produced content), non-compliant auto parts, upholstered furniture, cabinets and vanities; 10% on softwood timber and lumber on top of anti-dumping and countervailing duties; 25% medium and heavy vehicles; and retaliatory 25% Canadian tariffs on US steel, aluminum and non-compliant vehicles. Almost 90% of Canadian exports to the US were duty-free in Oct 2025 (US Census data); B.C. less exposed than other provinces with nearly half of goods exports to non-US destinations. B.C. disputes that lumber is subsidized or dumped and supports the federal WTO/CUSMA Chapter 10/NAFTA Chapter 19 challenges. - Budget 2026 (PRIMARY): https://www.bcbudget.gov.bc.ca/2026/pdf/2026_Budget_and_Fiscal_Plan.pdf
- First Quarterly Report (Sept 14, 2026): "another round of U.S. tariffs"; goods exports up 4.2% year to date to July 2026, driven by copper; non-US exports +16% (China, South Korea, India); manufacturing shipments +9.1% year to date to June; international tourism +6.6% (FIFA-related); latest tariffs expected to challenge forestry, aluminum and manufacturing; real GDP forecast 0.9% in 2026, 1.9% in 2027, average 2.1% in 2028-2030; unemployment rate 6.5% in August 2026; employment down 0.6% year to date to August 2026; Middle East conflict and tighter federal immigration policy also cited. - [BC Gov News](https://news.gov.bc.ca/releases/2026FIN0031-001066) (PRIMARY)
- 2025 labour market: employment +1.1% (+32,200 jobs); unemployment averaged 6.2% (0.6 points higher than 2024, 0.6 below the national average); forestry/fishing/mining/oil and gas lost 9,200 jobs; construction +15,400, manufacturing +7,500. Population growth may turn negative in 2026 due to federal immigration cuts (RBC). - Budget 2026 (PRIMARY); [RBC](https://www.rbc.com/en/economics/canadian-analysis/provincial-and-fiscal-outlooks/provincial-budgets-and-economic-statements/b-c-budget-2026-falls-short-on-fiscal-course-correction-amid-challenges/)
- Forestry: Budget 2025/26 provided $50M of new provincial and reallocated federal funding to the forestry sector ($15M to key organizations, $15M for FireSmart, $20M reallocated federal funds via Northern Development Initiative Trust, including $8M-plus from federal tariff revenues) and a temporary Stumpage Payment Deferral Program. ### Inferences
- Inference: Most party offers are demand-side or procurement-based responses to tariffs; the single largest measurable exposure in Budget 2026 is forestry (10% additional tariff on top of duties, higher costs, mill closures), and only the Greens and OneBC (and, with a deferral program, the sitting NDP government) have forestry-specific 2026 offers; the Conservatives have none published.
- Inference: LNG targets (double by 2032, triple by 2035) depend on final investment decisions by private proponents and on new electricity supply (North Coast Transmission Line), which is also an NDP project; the practical difference between NDP and Conservative LNG positions is the speed/royalty/regulatory emphasis, not whether to support LNG.
- Inference: With 2026 growth forecast at 0.9% and employment falling, the unemployment rate (6.5% in August 2026) is the fastest-moving indicator in the campaign; the next Labour Force Survey release before Oct 24 could change the narrative.
