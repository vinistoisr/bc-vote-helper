Research date: 2026-10-09. Purpose: neutral voter-advice tool. Labels: "Cited Findings" mix 2026 stated positions (marked STATED 2026) and actions taken (marked RECORD); "Inferences" are labelled inference. bcndp.ca releases are partisan party documents. Fetch method: pages were opened directly via fetch unless marked "search summary" (lower confidence). These notes supersede or correct earlier notes where flagged "CORRECTION".

## 1. What exactly is the NDP's Oct 4 tax proposal (brackets, thresholds, the $190,405 vs $1M question)?

### Takeaway
The conflict is resolved. The new 24.5% bracket applies only to taxable income above $1,000,000. The $190,405 figure is where the two existing top brackets begin to rise by 2 points (the first bracket that rises starts at $190,405); income below $190,405 is unchanged. Starts in the 2027 tax year. The bracket-indexing freeze for 2027-2030 is NOT a new NDP campaign promise: it is a Budget 2026 measure already in force (CORRECTION to the earlier note, which treated it as unverified).

### Cited Findings
- STATED 2026 (party release, Oct 4): "a 2% point increase on the top two tax brackets", plus a new bracket above $1 million, "would start in the 2027 tax year". Quoted thresholds: "The portion of their income between $190,405 to $265,545 will be taxed at 18.8%"; "between $265,545 and $1,000,000 will be taxed at 22.5%"; "above $1,000,000 will be taxed at 24.5%." "There is no change to the taxation on the portion of income of any taxpayer that is below $190,405." "The changes only impact the top 3.88% of British Columbian income earners." Projected revenue "$225M in 2026/27, $925M in 2027/28, and $1,000M in 2028/29." The release text does not state the prior rates (16.8% and 20.5% are implied by subtracting 2 points and are confirmed by Daily Hive below). - [BC NDP release, Oct 4 2026](https://www.bcndp.ca/releases/build-bc-strong-eby-will-ask-wealthy-pitch-bit-more)
- Daily Hive (Oct 5) states the same thresholds with prior rates: $190,405-$265,545 from 16.8% to 18.8%; $265,545-$1M from 20.5% to 22.5%; over $1M at 24.5%; "the first $190,405 stays at the same rates for everyone"; same 3.88% and revenue figures. - [Daily Hive, Oct 5 2026](https://dailyhive.com/vancouver/david-eby-bc-ndp-higher-income-taxes-top-earners-platform)
- Why the earlier headline said "$1 million": the Canadian Press first report (Oct 4, from Comox) said only that Eby would create "a new bracket for people who earn $1 million or more", gave no rates or other thresholds, and said details would follow at a Victoria stop later that day. The "$1M" headline therefore describes only the new third tier, not the whole proposal; about 3.88% of earners are affected, far more than millionaires. - [CP via CP24, Oct 4 2026](https://www.cp24.com/news/canada/2026/10/04/bcs-eby-promises-new-tax-on-millionaires-to-help-pay-for-health-care/)
- Eby quotes (CP): asking people "who earn a little bit more to pitch-in in a moment like this"; "We are glad when people are successful, it's fantastic." Daily Hive: "when people are struggling, we think that it's fair to ask those at the top to pitch in a bit more." - same two sources.
- RECORD (indexing): Budget 2026 (tabled Feb 17, 2026) pauses inflation indexing of personal income tax brackets and non-refundable credits at 2026 levels for the 2027 through 2030 tax years, resuming in 2031. The government page says indexation "is temporarily paused at their 2026 levels" and "will resume for 2031 and subsequent taxation years." So the Daily Hive line that the government "has suspended annual inflation adjustments" is a past government action, not part of the Oct 4 proposal. - [BC Gov budget tax changes](https://www2.gov.bc.ca/budgettaxchanges)
- RECORD (lowest bracket): same budget raised the lowest personal income tax rate from 5.06% to 5.60% (first $50,363 of 2026 taxable income), appearing on paycheques after July 1, 2026. This affects all taxpayers and is not mentioned in the NDP's 2026 campaign tax pitch. - [BC Gov budget tax changes](https://www2.gov.bc.ca/budgettaxchanges)
- Wording gap in the party's own release: it says "a billion dollars in new revenue each year" but its figures reach $1,000M only in 2028/29 (also in earlier note). Cost of the Conservative tax plan per NDP release (party claim, Oct 8): about $3B a year; "$190 million each year would go to the top 3.88% of income earners" is the party's claim about a Conservative measure and is unverified here. - [BC NDP release, Oct 8](https://www.bcndp.ca/releases/doerksons-tax-plan-confirms-cuts-health-care-tax-break-those-top)

### Inferences
- Plain statement for the tool: "Income above $190,405 would be taxed 2 points higher in each existing top bracket, and income above $1M would face a new 24.5% rate; nothing changes below $190,405." The $190,405 to $265,545 band is a salary range (for example senior professionals), not a millionaire-only tax. Combined with the already-enacted bracket freeze and the 5.60% bottom rate, the NDP's overall income-tax trajectory is upward at every level; the campaign pitch covers only the top-end change.

### Gaps
- Primary Budget 2026 document for the 5.60% bottom rate read only through the gov.bc.ca tax-changes page, not the backgrounder.
- Whether the Oct 4 release's revenue table counts the increase on the new bracket only or all three changes: not stated.

## 2. What has Eby said in the campaign about amending DRIPA after Gitxaala (changes, timeline, consent)?

### Takeaway
Eby has said only that the government must respond to the Court of Appeal decision and that this is "not up for debate"; he has not published amendment text, a timeline, or a position on whether First Nations consent is required. No NDP commitment on DRIPA appears on any NDP platform page or in the launch release as of Oct 9. The last concrete, public NDP position is the April 2026 joint statement with the First Nations Leadership Council promising no spring legislation and a solution "before the fall legislative session."

### Cited Findings
- STATED 2026 campaign: Eby, Sept 25: "We do have to respond to that court decision, it's not up for debate" and "We've been working with First Nations over the summer to see if we can find a path forward that we all agree on." The Narwhal says he "stood by his vow to change the law" at the start of the campaign, and says the article does not give amendment content, timeline, or whether First Nations consent is required (the FNLC is not mentioned in it). It described him as seeking "a satisfactory amendment." - [The Narwhal, Oct 7 2026](https://thenarwhal.ca/bc-election-2026-dripa/)
- The NDP launch release (Sept 22) was read in full: it has no item on DRIPA, Indigenous issues, public safety, drugs, policing, schools, child care or taxes. - [BC NDP launch release](https://www.bcndp.ca/releases/david-eby-launches-election-plan-build-bc-strong-amid-trumps-attacks)
- BC Ballot's NDP page (updated Oct 9, 97 commitments tracked) lists "No commitment published" for Indigenous relations and states DRIPA is not mentioned. Third-party aggregator. - [BC Ballot NDP](https://bcballot.ca/platforms/bc-ndp/)
- RECORD (joint statement, 2026-04-20): the province "will not be introducing legislation to suspend or amend DRIPA or UN Declaration-related provisions in the Interpretation Act, in the spring legislative session"; the province and FNLC commit to working with all First Nations leaders on a path that considers the government's legal concerns while "upholding the title and rights and human rights of First Nations"; and to "genuine collaboration to find solutions as soon as possible, and before the fall legislative session." - [FNLC joint statement](https://www.fnlc.ca/premiers-first-nations-leadership-council-statement-about-dripa/) (page undated; April date from [Daily Hive](https://dailyhive.com/vancouver/bc-dripa-premier-david-eby-fnlc-spring-2026-legislation-cancelled) search summary)
- RECORD timeline: Dec 2025 Gitxaala ruling; Jan 2026 Eby said DRIPA would be amended; Apr 1 called amendment "non-negotiable"; Apr 2 shifted to a three-year suspension while the Supreme Court of Canada appeal proceeds; FNLC leaders called it an "absolute betrayal"; Eby first called the bill a confidence vote and then said it would not be; NDP MLA Joan Phillip said she would not support it. - [Chronicle Journal / CP summary (search summary)](https://www.chroniclejournal.com/news/national/eby-called-changes-to-indigenous-law-non-negotiable-but-series-of-backdowns-ensued/article_3fe797a6-7780-544b-8468-ed72e87819fd.html); [Tyee, Apr 14](https://thetyee.ca/News/2026/04/14/BC-Backs-Off-DRIPA-Amendments/)
- First Nations reaction in the campaign: BC Assembly of First Nations urged parties to reject "divisive politics" and uphold DRIPA (Regional Chief Terry Teegee). - [The Narwhal](https://thenarwhal.ca/bc-election-2026-dripa/)
- Later Eby comment (search summary, Rob Shaw/North Shore News column; fetch blocked 403): said he still intends to change the law but has met "a wall" with the FNLC. Treat as lower confidence; not verified from the page. - [North Shore News](https://www.nsnews.com/economy-law-politics/rob-shaw-ebys-new-strategy-after-dripa-impasse-blame-first-nations-leaders-12139427)

### Inferences
- Likely (inference): if re-elected the NDP will bring a DRIPA/Interpretation Act amendment in the fall session, negotiated rather than unilateral given the April reversal and a thin legislative majority, but "with consent" cannot be stated: the NDP has not said so. For scoring, label the NDP as "amend, form and consent terms unpublished", between Conservative repeal and Green "legislate implementation".

### Gaps
- No amendment text, no consent position, no post-April FNLC-NDP working document, no Oct campaign statement beyond Sept 25. Re-check bcndp.ca and the Oct 14 debate.

## 3. NDP campaign position on involuntary care (Bill 32 content), safer supply, and supervised consumption sites

### Takeaway
Involuntary care: campaign commitment only for the Kelowna region; no province-wide platform item. Bill 32 (Mental Health Amendment Act, 2025) removed "deemed consent" and expanded liability protection; it did not add patient safeguards, and a BC Supreme Court ruling on Jul 30-31 2026 found the Mental Health Act's involuntary-treatment provisions unconstitutional (six months to amend). The government had not stated a response by Oct 9. Supervised consumption sites: Eby said in Kelowna on Oct 2 that they "save lives" and "connect people to care" and must be "well run" (a stated, not published, position). Safer supply: no 2026 campaign statement found; record is witnessed dosing only.

### Cited Findings
- STATED 2026 (Kelowna, Oct 2, Kelowna Capital News): Eby on social disorder: "It's a very serious issue in Kelowna." On involuntary care: "one of those pieces includes involuntary care here in the region"; the province is working with local officials to find a site that "can be used immediately"; he acknowledged staffing is a challenge. On supervised consumption sites: they "save lives" and "connect people to care", and "have to be well run and well operated" so they do not affect neighbours. - [Kelowna Capital News, Oct 2 2026](https://kelownacapnews.com/2026/10/02/eby-announces-b-c-grocery-price-guard-social-disorder-during-kelowna-visit/)
- Global News account of the same stop quotes only the involuntary-care line and says his party would announce more on supporting communities. - [Global News, Oct 2 2026](https://globalnews.ca/news/12086166/bc-ndp-conservative-leaders-campaign-okanagan/)
- Not on any NDP platform page: BC Ballot says province-wide involuntary care, safer supply and supervised consumption are "not on the page"; only the Kelowna item is listed, flagged as reported and not party-published. - [BC Ballot NDP](https://bcballot.ca/platforms/bc-ndp/)
- RECORD (Bill 32): introduced Nov 2025 and passed in a confidence vote in the first week of Dec 2025; removes "deemed consent" from the Mental Health Act and extends liability protection for health workers and police to "care" and "professional service" (terms not yet defined); critics (Council of Canadians with Disabilities, per The Mainlander) say it adds no new patient safeguards. The Mainlander is an advocacy-leaning outlet; no direct Eby quote in it. Earlier summary: Eby said changes were about "immunizing" workers and would not alter how involuntary care decisions are made (search summary). - [The Mainlander, Dec 5 2025](https://themainlander.com/2025/12/05/bill32/)
- RECORD (court): Justice Lauren Blake, BC Supreme Court (reported Jul 31 2026): Mental Health Act sections giving hospital directors sweeping authority to order psychiatric treatment are unconstitutional (Charter ss. 7 and 15 per Tyee); six months to amend or the provisions lapse; the suit (Council of Canadians with Disabilities) did not challenge involuntary admission; the Attorney General's office said it was reviewing the decision. No appeal or amendment announcement found. - [Global News, Jul 31 2026](https://globalnews.ca/news/12005744/sections-bc-involuntary-care-rules-found-unconstitutional/); [The Tyee, Aug 4 2026 (search summary)](https://thetyee.ca/News/2026/08/04/BC-Involuntary-Care-Practices-Unconstitutional/)
- RECORD (safer supply): witnessed dosing for all prescribed-alternatives patients effective Dec 30, 2025; Eby acknowledged "some people are going to lose access." No 2026 campaign comment found. - [CTV (search summary)](https://guelph.ctvnews.ca/vancouver/article/relief-and-frustration-from-critics-as-bc-amends-safer-supply-policy); see also existing note issues_dripa_sogi_drugs_safety.md
- Related court ruling (federal law, not provincial): Sept 29 2026 BC Supreme Court (Justice Catherine Murray) overturned DULF compassion club trafficking convictions on constitutional grounds and gave Ottawa six months to rework the drug law; no BC government statement found. - [CP via CP24, Sept 29 2026 (search summary)](https://www.cp24.com/news/canada/2026/09/29/bc-court-to-rule-on-constitutional-challenge-of-activists-trafficking-convictions/)

### Inferences
- Inference: the NDP is likely to keep supervised consumption sites, keep witnessed-only safer supply, expand involuntary care incrementally, and bring Mental Health Act amendments before the ~January 2027 deadline (the deadline falls early in the next term). Province-wide involuntary-care scale-up is not promised.

### Gaps
- Mental Health Act bill text and Bill 32 primary text on leg.bc.ca not read; the AG decision on appeal not found; no explicit NDP safer-supply statement for 2026.

## 4. NDP public-safety and policing commitments for 2026 (police funding, bail, repeat offenders, Surrey extortion, SPS completion)

### Takeaway

No NDP 2026 commitment found on police funding totals, bail, Surrey extortion or Surrey Police Service completion. Not a published platform item; no cost or timeline. - [Kelowna Capital News, Oct 2 2026](https://kelownacapnews.com/2026/10/02/eby-announces-b-c-grocery-price-guard-social-disorder-during-kelowna-visit/)

- RECORD: Safer Communities Action Plan (2022) $230M over three years for police; 2025 budget $560M over three years for encampments, repeat violent offending, robbery and shoplifting (as in the existing note; search summaries); Repeat Violent Offending Intervention Initiative with 12 community hubs (announced in Nanaimo, date not confirmed, search summary). - [CTV Vancouver Island](https://vancouverisland.ctvnews.ca/vancouver/vancouver-island/article/bc-to-target-repeat-offenders-with-12-community-hubs)

## 6. Greens: does the drug-policy document explicitly support decriminalization? Any public-safety or policing commitment?

### Takeaway
Yes, explicitly. The BC Greens' Toxic Drugs Policy Brief (Oct 2026, PDF read in full) says the Greens will "decriminalis[e] possession of drugs for personal use" alongside housing, mental health and voluntary care. The Greens have published no public-safety or policing commitment; their only safety-related wording is in the drug brief (supervised sites to "support public safety").

### Cited Findings
- STATED 2026 (primary text, Toxic Drugs Policy Brief): "Treat drug use as a health and social issue, not a criminal one, by decriminalising possession of drugs for personal use. Alongside decriminalisation, implement the measures on housing, mental health, and voluntary care that the BC NDP failed to, to ensure people who use drugs have the full range of support needed." - [BC Greens Toxic Drugs Policy Brief 2026 (PDF)](https://bcgreens.ca/wp-content/uploads/2026/10/BC-Greens-Toxic-Drugs-Policy-Brief-2026-1.pdf)
- Same brief: "Provide regulated, pharmaceutical alternatives to the illicit drug supply" ("Our main priority must be keeping people alive"); "Enhance access to supervised consumption sites and overdose prevention sites" with capacity for safe inhalation spaces "to reduce harm and public drug use, and support public safety"; build out mobile and 24-hour overdose prevention sites; annual funding for drug-checking; transitional and public housing "with wrap-around healthcare and harm reduction principles"; match bed-based care with community outpatient treatment; an evidence-based standard of care for treatment; youth drug education. The brief frames care as "voluntary" and says the crisis will not be solved by "expanding a punitive system" or "unnecessary involvement with the criminal justice system"; it contains no endorsement of involuntary care. - same PDF
- The brief's factual claims (party framing, not independently verified): the Sept 29 2026 BC Supreme Court ruling quoted (Justice Murray: "a non-medical safe supply is urgently needed to save lives"); 81% of 2026 unregulated drug deaths occurred indoors; 6.6 million visits to supervised and overdose prevention sites in ten years with two deaths. The ruling's existence is corroborated by CP; the "81% indoors" figure is within the 82% for March in the existing note. - same PDF; [CP24 Sept 29 (search summary)](https://www.cp24.com/news/canada/2026/09/29/bc-court-to-rule-on-constitutional-challenge-of-activists-trafficking-convictions/)
- Green plan page (Oct 2026): "Fight the toxic drugs crisis with effective harm reduction and real investment in voluntary care"; "Uphold the rights of Indigenous, 2SLGBTQ+, and vulnerable communities"; no item on policing, crime, extortion, bail or courts. - [BC Greens Our Plan](https://bcgreens.ca/our-plan/)
- The Oct 2 press release page itself does not contain the word "decriminalis"; it links the brief (and quotes Lapointe on "regulated alternatives" and "voluntary treatment"). The wording above is from the PDF. - [BC Greens release Oct 2](https://bcgreens.ca/announce-toxic-drugs-policy-alongside-former-chief-coroner-and-candidate-for-saanich-south-lisa-lapointe/)
- BC Ballot (aggregator) lists "no commitment published" for Green public safety and policing as of Oct 9. - [BC Ballot Green](https://bcballot.ca/platforms/bc-green-party/)
- Other Green items captured from primary pages: Fair Share Act one-time levy at "5% on net wealth above $50 million, 10% above $100 million, and 20% above $1 billion" (about $22.2B first year, party estimate) then annual 2%/3%/5% at the same thresholds (about $5.4B a year, party estimate), affecting about 0.1% of BC families; proportional representation legislation in the first 100 days; 26,000 non-market homes a year (party figures, not independently costed). - [BC Greens Fair Share Act release](https://bcgreens.ca/fair-share-act-would-unlock-22-2b-for-childcare-transit-and-housing/); [BC Greens Our Plan](https://bcgreens.ca/our-plan/); [BC Greens Economic Vision executive summary (PDF)](https://bcgreens.ca/wp-content/uploads/2026/10/BCG_Executive-Summary.pdf)
- Context: the executive summary PDF calls itself "our economic direction rather than our full election platform", so a full Green platform has not been published as of Oct 9. - same PDF

### Inferences
- Inference: the Greens are the only party with a 2026 platform to restore decriminalization, in explicit opposition to the NDP's January 2026 end of the pilot; their stance is the most harm-reduction-oriented of the parties. The Greens' lack of any stated safety/policing position means any "public safety" score for them must be marked "no position stated"; a "community safety" reading would come only from their harm-reduction and housing items. The brief does not say how the Greens would obtain a federal exemption now that the pilot has ended (the federal exemption was needed in 2023); a Green caucus of two to six MLAs would not control the policy without NDP agreement (the Greens ended their accord with the NDP on 2026-02-09 per the existing note).

### Gaps
- Green policy book (bcgreens.ca/policy-book) not read, so no check for a standing policing or justice policy; full Green platform not yet released.
- No Green position found on involuntary care beyond the brief's voluntary framing.
