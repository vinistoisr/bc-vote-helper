# BC Greens and minor parties / independents, 2026 BC general election (research as of 2026-10-09)

Scope note: Voting day is 2026-10-24 (advance voting Oct 16-21). Findings below are separated into (a) stated platform/positions, (b) documented actions, and (c) inference (labelled). Many sources are Wikipedia or search-engine summaries of news items; where I could only reach a secondary summary, that is flagged. Sources were fetched 2026-10-09. No em dashes used.

## 1. BC Greens: leader, MLAs, candidates, and 2026 platform commitments by issue

### Takeaway
Emily Lowan (age 25, elected leader Sept 2025) leads the Greens into the election with 73-74 candidates. The platform is published in pieces ("Full platform coming soon" as of Oct 9); the centrepiece is the "Fair Share Act" wealth tax on net worth above $50 million. The Greens support DRIPA and SOGI 123, propose decriminalization plus regulated drug alternatives, vacancy control, proportional representation legislation in 100 days, and no new fossil fuel development.

### Cited Findings
Leader, MLAs, candidates
- Lowan has been Green leader since Sept 24, 2025, winning with over 60% of the vote; she is running in Saanich North and the Islands and holds no seat. Born Sept 18, 2000. (Wikipedia carries a maintenance notice that the article leans on sources close to its subject.) - [Wikipedia: Emily Lowan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emily_Lowan)
- Furstenau resigned as leader in Jan 2025 after losing her seat in 2024; Jeremy Valeriote was interim leader until Lowan won. - [Wikipedia: 2026 BC general election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2026_British_Columbia_general_election)
- Current Green MLAs are Rob Botterell (Saanich North and the Islands) and Jeremy Valeriote (West Vancouver-Sea to Sky), both elected 2024. Botterell announced he is not seeking a second term. - [Wikipedia: Green Party of BC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Green_Party_of_British_Columbia); [Wikipedia: Emily Lowan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emily_Lowan)
- Valeriote is listed as a Green candidate on the Elections BC candidate report (riding not confirmed in my text extraction). - [Elections BC candidate report](https://www.elections.bc.ca/docs/fin/GE-2026-10-24-Candidate-Website-Report.PDF)
- Green candidate count: 73 (Island Social Trends, Oct 8; Elections BC report text shows 74 "BC Green Party" matches in my extraction) vs 74 (Wikipedia candidates page, Oct 9). - [Island Social Trends](https://islandsocialtrends.ca/bc-2026-provincial-election-355-candidates-across-93-ridings/); [Wikipedia: Candidates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Candidates_of_the_2026_British_Columbia_general_election)
- Slogan "Believe in Better". - [Wikipedia: 2026 BC general election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2026_British_Columbia_general_election)
- Lowan was invited to the Oct 14 televised leaders' debate (with Eby and Doerkson); Brodie and Sturko were not. - [Wikipedia: 2026 BC general election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2026_British_Columbia_general_election); [Times Colonist](https://www.timescolonist.com/2026-bc-votes/ndp-conservative-green-leaders-to-debate-on-oct-14-in-bc-election-12863560)

Housing
- Build and protect 26,000 permanently affordable non-market homes per year; speed approvals for all housing types. - [BC Greens: Our Plan](https://bcgreens.ca/our-plan/)
- Vacancy control (allowable rent increases tied to the unit, not the tenant), restart the Community Housing Fund and Indigenous Housing Fund, end blanket no-pet clauses (announced Sept 29, 2026). - [Daily Hive](https://dailyhive.com/vancouver/bc-greens-vacancy-control-rentals); [BC Greens release](https://bcgreens.ca/announce-plan-to-support-renters-unveil-two-new-candidates-for-south-island/)

Wealth, taxes, inequality
- Fair Share Act (announced Oct 6, 2026): taxes net wealth over $50 million. One-time tax 5% above $50M, 10% above $100M, 20% above $1B. Annual tax from year two: 2% above $50M, 3% above $100M, 5% above $1B. Party projects about $22.2 billion in year one and $5.4 billion per year after. Revenue is to fund universal child care, free transit, affordable housing and debt paydown. The policy brief does not show the underlying calculations, the number of affected households, or valuation methods; critics quoted generically call the rates excessive and raise relocation and liquidity concerns. - [Daily Hive](https://dailyhive.com/vancouver/bc-greens-wealth-tax-fair-share-act)
- Windfall tax on large corporations; renegotiate oil and gas royalties; end fossil fuel subsidies; require existing LNG projects to cover their own power costs. - [BC Greens: Our Plan](https://bcgreens.ca/our-plan/)
- Three-year PST exemption on machinery, equipment and business software (as part of a four-pillar economic plan); economic vision PDF dated Oct 7. - [BC Greens Economic Vision PDF](https://bcgreens.ca/wp-content/uploads/2026/10/BCG_Economic-Vision.pdf) (cited via BC Ballot; the PST detail is from a search summary, not verified on the PDF itself)
- Raise social and disability assistance above the poverty line and index to inflation; raise minimum wage toward a living wage; expand union access via sectoral bargaining; commercial rent control; disclosure rules for large firms in essential sectors (e.g. groceries). - [BC Greens: Our Plan](https://bcgreens.ca/our-plan/); [BC Ballot](https://bcballot.ca/platforms/bc-green-party/)
- Lowan has previously described "ultra-wealthy" as people with at least $100 million in assets (earlier proposal, differs from the $50M threshold in the Oct 6 Act). - [Wikipedia: Emily Lowan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emily_Lowan)

Health
- Community Health Centre in every riding/region; complete nurse-to-patient ratios; keep public health dollars in public care; public coverage of mental health care; expanded dementia care (Oct 4 and 6). - [BC Greens: Our Plan](https://bcgreens.ca/our-plan/); [BC Ballot](https://bcballot.ca/platforms/bc-green-party/)
- Independent health impact assessment of LNG and fracking activities (Oct 6). - [BC Ballot](https://bcballot.ca/platforms/bc-green-party/)

Climate, energy, LNG

- Lowan opposes the memorandum of understanding on a pipeline to the North Coast. - [Wikipedia: Emily Lowan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emily_Lowan)

Drugs
- Toxic Drugs policy announced Oct 2, 2026 with former chief coroner Lisa Lapointe (Saanich South candidate). Framing: toxic drugs are the leading cause of death; "regulated alternatives" and voluntary treatment. - [BC Greens release](https://bcgreens.ca/announce-toxic-drugs-policy-alongside-former-chief-coroner-and-candidate-for-saanich-south-lisa-lapointe/)
- BC Ballot summarizes the policy as regulated pharmaceutical alternatives to the illicit supply and decriminalization of personal possession. The release itself does not state decriminalization; the policy brief PDF was not read. - [BC Ballot](https://bcballot.ca/platforms/bc-green-party/)

DRIPA and Indigenous rights
- Plan page: "honour Indigenous rights in full"; Lowan supports implementing DRIPA. Page does not name DRIPA. - [BC Greens: Our Plan](https://bcgreens.ca/our-plan/); [Wikipedia: Emily Lowan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emily_Lowan)

SOGI and 2SLGBTQ+

Democracy and electoral reform
- Introduce proportional representation legislation within the first 100 days; Lowan supports regional-riding PR. - [BC Greens: Our Plan](https://bcgreens.ca/our-plan/); [Wikipedia: Emily Lowan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emily_Lowan)

Other
- Transit "fast, frequent, and free"; restart $10-a-day child care pledge toward universal coverage; Public safety: BC Ballot shows no published Green commitments in that category as of Oct 9. - [BC Greens: Our Plan](https://bcgreens.ca/our-plan/); [BC Ballot](https://bcballot.ca/platforms/bc-green-party/)

## 2. Green record 2016-2026

### Takeaway
2017-2020: a confidence-and-supply agreement (CASA) with the NDP; Greens say they secured donation reform, an electoral reform referendum (which failed) and UNDRIP legislation commitments. 2024-2026: a "Co-operation and Responsible Government Accord" signed Dec 2024; the Greens declined to renew it on Feb 9, 2026 citing undelivered commitments. In votes 2025, Greens opposed fast-track bills 14 and 15, and the carbon tax repeal (as passed).

### Cited Findings
2017 CASA
- Greens won 3 seats in May 2017 (16.84% of vote), signed a confidence and supply agreement with the NDP on May 29, 2017, and the NDP and Greens defeated the Liberal government by one vote. - [Wikipedia: Green Party of BC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Green_Party_of_British_Columbia); [CBC 2017](https://www.cbc.ca/news/canada/british-columbia/bc-ndp-green-agreement-1.4136539)
- Terms included legislation banning corporate, union and out-of-province donations, an electoral reform referendum, a climate plan, and making UNDRIP law. - [Signed agreement PDF (NDP caucus)](https://bcndpcaucus.ca/wp-content/uploads/sites/5/2017/05/BC-Green-BC-NDP-Agreement_vf-May-29th-2017.pdf); [The Tyee, May 30, 2017](https://thetyee.ca/News/2017/05/30/NDP-Green-Agreement/); [Daily Hive full text](https://dailyhive.com/vancouver/bc-ndp-bc-greens-agreement-in-full)
- Bill 3 (Election Amendment Act, 2017) banning corporate, union and out-of-province donations passed third reading Nov 2017 with Green support. Individual limit: $1,200 per year in enacted-law coverage, $2,500 in an earlier proposal (sources differ). - [Straight](https://www.straight.com/news/968556/bc-ndp-government-bans-corporate-and-union-donations-and-introduces-public-subsidy); [Andrew Weaver, Nov 22, 2017](http://www.andrewweavermla.ca/2017/11/22/final-hurdle-banning-big-money-bc-politics-cleared/)
- 2018 referendum: 61.3% kept first past the post, 38.7% chose PR; turnout 42.6%. - [CBC](https://www.cbc.ca/news/canada/british-columbia/electoral-reform-referendum-results-1.4954538)
- DRIPA passed unanimously in 2019. - [The Narwhal, Oct 7, 2026](https://thenarwhal.ca/bc-election-2026-dripa/)
- Green seats: 2017: 3; 2020: 2 (15.08%); 2024: 2 (8.24%). - [Wikipedia: Green Party of BC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Green_Party_of_British_Columbia)

2024 Accord
- Signed Dec 12-13, 2024 as the "Co-operation and Responsible Government Accord"; four years, reviewable annually; items included community health centres, mental health coverage, non-profit housing, homelessness, CleanBC update, Fairy Creek protection, electoral reform talks. Greens retain independence to vote against bills while supporting budgets and confidence. - [Capital Daily](https://www.capitaldaily.ca/news/bc-ndp-greens-sign-governance-deal); [BC Greens](https://bcgreens.ca/bcndp-bc-greens-finalize-agreement/); [Wikipedia: 2026 BC general election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2026_British_Columbia_general_election)
- Feb 9, 2026: Greens announced they would not renew; accord expired March 2026. Lowan said nearly two thirds of first-year commitments were stalled or undelivered. Delivered: ending spousal claw-backs on disability assistance. Undelivered per Valeriote: public buses on Highway 99, $50 million for psychologists, $15 million for community health centres. NDP said it would work vote by vote; Eby's office said Greens refused to renew at "current levels" of confidence support. - [CP24 / CP](https://www.cp24.com/news/canada/2026/02/09/bc-greens-wont-renew-accord-with-ndp-citing-stalled-undelivered-promises/); [Surrey Now-Leader](https://surreynowleader.com/2026/02/09/b-c-greens-not-renewing-deal-to-support-governing-ndp/)
- Date conflict: Wikipedia's Green page text says Feb 2026 but cites a CBC article dated Jan 9; CP/Lethbridge Herald/CP24 coverage is dated Feb 9, 2026 - [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Green_Party_of_British_Columbia); [CP24](https://www.cp24.com/news/canada/2026/02/09/bc-greens-wont-renew-accord-with-ndp-citing-stalled-undelivered-promises/)

Votes 2025-2026
- Bill 14 (Renewable Energy Projects Act) and Bill 15 (infrastructure fast-track): Greens said they support renewables but not Bill 14 as drafted (weak regulator oversight, transparency), and opposed Bill 15 over undefined "provincially significant" projects. Final division records not retrieved. - [BC Greens](https://bcgreens.ca/bc-greens-support-renewable-energy-not-bill-14-as-currently-drafted/); [Castanet](https://www.castanetkamloops.net/news/BC/549318/B-C-Greens-won-t-back-government-s-vague-infrastructure-bill)
- Bill 7 (Economic Stabilization (Tariff Response) Act, 2025) passed 48-44; Greens opposed it in final form after pushing amendments and welcoming removal of Part 4; I could not confirm each Green MLA's individual vote from a division list. - [CBC](https://www.cbc.ca/news/canada/british-columbia/bill-7-tariffs-greens-conservatives-ndp-1.7489262); [BC Greens](https://bcgreens.ca/bc-greens-applaud-removal-of-part-4-from-tariff-response-bill-say-further-safeguards-needed/); [Houston Today](https://www.houston-today.com/news/bc-government-passes-budget-tariff-response-act-other-votes-coming-8035130)
- Consumer carbon tax repeal (rate set to $0, April 1, 2025): Botterell was the lone dissenting vote at first reading; Greens objected to one-day passage and absence of a replacement climate plan; bill passed with a single no vote. - [CBC](https://www.cbc.ca/news/canada/british-columbia/carbon-tax-up-for-discussion-monday-1.7497589); [BC Gov News](https://news.gov.bc.ca/releases/2025FIN0014-000280)
- Budgets: Under the accord, Greens supported the budget while voting no on bills (stated in search summary of Houston Today article). - [Houston Today](https://www.houston-today.com/news/bc-government-passes-budget-tariff-response-act-other-votes-coming-8035130)
- DRIPA amendments: Eby planned (March-April 2026) to pause parts of DRIPA after the Gitxaala Court of Appeal decision, as a confidence vote; the government backed off on April 13, 2026 because NDP MLA Joan Phillip would not support it. CP reported the Greens signalled opposition. - [The Tyee, April 14, 2026](https://thetyee.ca/News/2026/04/14/BC-Backs-Off-DRIPA-Amendments/); [CBC](https://www.cbc.ca/news/canada/british-columbia/dripa-suspension-confidence-vote-first-nation-leaders-opposed-9.7158996). As of Oct 7, Eby still says he will respond to the ruling and the Conservatives say they will repeal DRIPA. - [The Narwhal](https://thenarwhal.ca/bc-election-2026-dripa/)
- Source conflict: The Tyee article says the Gitxaala decision was "December 2026", which is impossible; The Narwhal dates it December 2025. - [The Tyee](https://thetyee.ca/News/2026/04/14/BC-Backs-Off-DRIPA-Amendments/); [The Narwhal](https://thenarwhal.ca/bc-election-2026-dripa/)
- Bill 14/15 timeline: Greens-NDP accord coverage of 2025 votes is from mid-2025; the Greens also criticized the 2025 BCGEU strike handling and, per Lowan's profile, opposed the North Coast pipeline MOU. - [Wikipedia: Emily Lowan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emily_Lowan)

### Inferences
- Pattern 2017-2026: Greens trade confidence for named policy deliverables and then vote against bills they view as lacking oversight (Bills 7, 14, 15) or climate backing (carbon tax repeal). The accord was allowed to lapse when deliverables stalled.
- In a minority legislature, likely behaviour: support on confidence only for written, specific commitments (PR legislation, health centres, vacancy control, wealth tax); Lowan said the Greens did not rule out voting with Conservatives on confidence measures (Feb 2026 CP), but the Conservative platform (DRIPA repeal, SOGI repeal) is opposed to Green positions on those issues. A Conservative-Green deal looks less likely than an NDP-Green deal; not predicted with certainty.
- With 2 seats and 73-74 candidates, the Greens are most likely to hold a balance-of-power role only if the NDP/Conservative split leaves one party short of 47.

### Gaps
- No division list verifying individual Green votes on Bill 7, Bill 14 or Bill 15. No primary document confirming CleanBC (Dec 2018) as a CASA deliverable. No exact accord expiry date. 2025 and 2026 budgets: no direct vote record found.

## 3. OneBC

### Takeaway
OneBC is a right-populist, socially conservative party founded by ex-Conservative MLAs Dallas Brodie and Tara Armstrong, registered June 2025, running 33-34 candidates. Its published priorities (1bc.ca) include repealing DRIPA and SOGI 123, cutting every tax bracket by 25%, ending safe supply, and allowing private health care.

### Cited Findings
- Registered with Elections BC June 9, 2025; founded by Brodie and Armstrong, who were elected as Conservatives in 2024. Brodie was removed from the Conservative caucus March 7, 2025 over comments about residential schools. - [Wikipedia: OneBC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/OneBC_(political_party))
- Leadership: Brodie interim leader since June 9, 2025; board removed her Dec 13, 2025; reinstated Dec 21, 2025 after Armstrong, Paul Ratchford and Tim Thielmann resigned from the board. Armstrong left the caucus in Dec 2025. - [Wikipedia: OneBC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/OneBC_(political_party)); [CBC](https://www.cbc.ca/news/canada/british-columbia/mla-dallas-brodie-ousted-says-onebc-board-9.7015705)
- Party status: lost official status Dec 2025 (needs 2 MLAs); regained Sept 22, 2026 when Surrey South MLA Brent Chapman crossed from the Conservatives. - [Wikipedia: OneBC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/OneBC_(political_party)); [Surrey Now-Leader, Sept 22](https://surreynowleader.com/2026/09/22/surrey-south-mla-chapman-leaving-b-c-conservatives-for-one-bc/)
- Conflict: Wikipedia's election page says Chapman is not standing for re-election (announced Oct 3, 2026), and the Times Colonist headline said OneBC "fails to get party status" before the election; Wikipedia's OneBC page says status regained Sept 22. - [Wikipedia: 2026 BC general election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2026_British_Columbia_general_election); [Times Colonist](https://www.timescolonist.com/2026-bc-votes/onebc-overwhelmed-with-candidates-but-fails-to-get-party-status-12821173)
- Candidates: 34 per Wikipedia (Oct 8-9; includes Jenny Yamagata added in Burnaby North after the deadline); 33 per Island Social Trends and VPL; Elections BC report text shows 34 "OneBC" matches in my extraction. Brodie said 300+ people applied. - [Wikipedia: Candidates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Candidates_of_the_2026_British_Columbia_general_election); [Island Social Trends](https://islandsocialtrends.ca/bc-2026-provincial-election-355-candidates-across-93-ridings/); [Times Colonist](https://www.timescolonist.com/2026-bc-votes/onebc-overwhelmed-with-candidates-but-fails-to-get-party-status-12821173)
- Brodie runs in Vancouver-Quilchena; slogan "Excellence in British Columbia"; excluded from Oct 14 debate. - [Elections BC report](https://www.elections.bc.ca/docs/fin/GE-2026-10-24-Candidate-Website-Report.PDF); [Times Colonist](https://www.timescolonist.com/2026-bc-votes/ndp-conservative-green-leaders-to-debate-on-oct-14-in-bc-election-12863560)
- OneBC will not run against eight Conservative candidates whose SOGI positions it shares (Heather Maahs, Kristina Lowen, Anna Kindy, Sharon Hartwell, Macklin McCall, Sheldon Clare, David Williams, Lawrence Mok). - [CTV](https://www.ctvnews.ca/vancouver/article/onebc-pledges-not-to-run-against-eight-principled-bc-conservative-candidates/); [CBC search summary](https://www.cbc.ca/news/canada/british-columbia/dallas-brodie-onebc-q-a-2026-election-9.7366117) (CBC page returned 403; headline: "aims to balance budget, repeal SOGI and unite the right")

Stated platform (party's own priorities page, https://1bc.ca/priorities)
- Reconciliation: repeal DRIPA and declare UNDRIP of no effect in BC; stop voluntary transfers of cash, land and resource control to band governments; seek constitutional reform to repeal the Indian Act and convert reserves into townships; end mandatory land acknowledgements. - [OneBC priorities](https://1bc.ca/priorities)
- Taxes and economy: cut every tax bracket by 25% including corporate; cut PST by 2 points; approve a pipeline to Prince Rupert or Kitimat; audit 176,000+ regulations; balance budget within 4 years. - [OneBC priorities](https://1bc.ca/priorities)
- Health: allow private health care alongside public; repeal the HPOA; phase out ICBC monopoly and end no-fault. - [OneBC priorities](https://1bc.ca/priorities)
- Education: remove SOGI-123 and "politicized content"; standardized testing; Parental Bill of Rights. Statement: "we will repeal SOGI 123 and remove all gender ideology content from British Columbia classrooms." - [OneBC priorities](https://1bc.ca/priorities); [Yahoo/CBC](https://ca.news.yahoo.com/where-b-c-provincial-political-130000614.html)
- Housing: eliminate the Step Code; block municipal rent control. - [OneBC priorities](https://1bc.ca/priorities)
- Drugs: end safe supply and consumption programs; convert sites to recovery centres; involuntary rehab and housing for the severely addicted; roll back decriminalization. - [OneBC priorities](https://1bc.ca/priorities)
- Crime, guns, other: focus on repeat violent offenders; ban mask-wearing at protests; bar provincial participation in federal firearms buybacks; Western Canada Alliance with Alberta and Saskatchewan. - [OneBC priorities](https://1bc.ca/priorities)
- Immigration: June 2025 release and party material state "end mass immigration into BC, the importation of foreign grievances, and the flow of fentanyl from the Chinese Communist Party" (search summary of 1bc.ca; I did not see this on the priorities page itself); an opinion piece notes immigration levels are set federally. - [Wikipedia: OneBC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/OneBC_(political_party)); [1bc.ca](https://1bc.ca/)
- Elections: June 2025 release calls for ending mail-in and early voting and hand-counting; ban teacher strikes; "defund the reconciliation industry". - [Wikipedia: OneBC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/OneBC_(political_party))
- Climate: no stated climate-plan commitments found on the priorities page; only a pipeline approval.
- BC Ballot tracks 46 OneBC commitments. - [BC Ballot](https://bcballot.ca/platforms/)

Actions (private members' bills)
- Oct 6, 2025: bill on gender-affirming care defeated before first reading, 48-40. Oct 23, 2025: bill banning land acknowledgements by public institutions failed at first reading, 88-5. Dec 2, 2025: documentary "Making a Killing" made with caucus funds. - [Wikipedia: OneBC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/OneBC_(political_party)); [CBC search result: land acknowledgements bill](https://www.cbc.ca/news/canada/british-columbia/dallas-brodie-onebc-q-a-2026-election-9.7366117)

### Inferences
- OneBC is most likely to vote with the Conservatives in the legislature on DRIPA, SOGI, drugs and taxes; its seat count is small and its leadership has been unstable (board removal Dec 2025), so durability is uncertain.
- Political scientists compare it to the federal People's Party of Canada (Wikipedia citing an academic).

### Gaps
- No climate, immigration or housing-supply details on the current priorities page; the CBC Q&A with Brodie was blocked (403) and not read; OneBC DRIPA positions confirmed only via the party's own site. OneBC candidate count 33 vs 34 unresolved. Chapman status unresolved.

## 4. CentreBC, BC United and other parties

### Takeaway
CentreBC (leader Elenore Sturko, 35 candidates) is a centre-right breakaway from BC United; BC United is not running candidates. Smaller parties run a handful of candidates each, none in Surrey-Fleetwood except the Surrey seats listed.

### Cited Findings
CentreBC
- Founded March 28, 2025 by Karin Kirkpatrick (former BC United MLA) after Kevin Falcon suspended BC United's 2024 campaign. Leaders: Kirkpatrick to June 20, 2026; Mike Bernier named July 18, 2026; Bernier resigned Sept 18; Peter Milobar led briefly; after the Sept 22 writ Milobar returned to the Conservatives (with seven ex-CentreBC MLAs) and Elenore Sturko (Surrey-Cloverdale/Surrey South) became leader. - [Wikipedia: CentreBC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CentreBC); [Wikipedia: 2026 BC general election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2026_British_Columbia_general_election)
- Candidates: 35. Slogan "Balanced Government for British Columbia". Platform unreleased as of Oct 9; due Saturday Oct 10 (per Black Press coverage). Announced: double the Manufacturing Jobs Fund to $60 million, public scorecards for grants, costed programs. - [Agassiz-Harrison Observer, Oct 9](https://agassizharrisonobserver.com/2026/10/09/centrebcs-sturko-promisesmanufacturing-boost/); [BC Ballot](https://bcballot.ca/platforms/)
- SOGI: Sturko ties SOGI policies to the 2016 Human Rights Code change, says every student "deserves to feel safe and respected and included at school", opposes repealing anti-discrimination protections. - [Yahoo/CBC](https://ca.news.yahoo.com/where-b-c-provincial-political-130000614.html)
- Housing (Feb 16): link policy to affordability outcomes; long-term care homes and home care; Surrey anti-extortion plan (Nov 2025). - [BC Ballot](https://bcballot.ca/platforms/)

BC United
