# BC Conservatives 2026: housing, climate/carbon and fiscal positions (gap-closing)

Research date: 2026-10-09. Scope: replace inferences from the 2024 Rustad platform with 2026 Conservative positions on housing, climate and fiscal policy, plus health delivery, ICBC, rent control, Metro Vancouver and Surrey. Method: conservativebc.ca plan hub, BC Ballot's tracker of party releases (updated 2026-10-09), Canadian Press copy, Global News, Daily Hive, Western Standard, and 2024-era coverage for baselines. Several outlets (CBC, BIV, National Observer, Business Examiner) returned HTTP 403, so some items rest on search summaries and are flagged. Existing notes in this folder (party_bc_conservative.md, election_landscape.md) were read first; contradictions with them are called out under each question.

Headline result: as of 2026-10-09, the Conservatives have NOT released a 2026 housing plan beyond one sentence, have said nothing on industrial carbon pricing, CleanBC or the 2030 target, and have published no costed platform or deficit path. The plan hub lists 10 items (the BC Ballot count is 94 commitments); none is on housing detail, carbon, or Surrey. The scoring tool therefore cannot score the Conservatives on 2026 housing mandates, the speculation tax, OBPS or a balanced-budget date from current commitments. Where 2024 positions are used, they must be labelled as 2024 and not confirmed for 2026.

Convention: (a) 2026 platform/stated positions, (b) actions/record, (c) inference, labelled.

## 1. Has the party released a 2026 housing plan, and does it keep the 2024 pledges?

### Takeaway
No. The only 2026 housing commitment is "bring down the cost of building and buying a home" (Sept 25), issued as a reaction to the NDP's unsold-condo tax and higher speculation tax. The speculation tax, Bills 44/46/47, the rent/mortgage rebate and the 6-month rezoning limits are not mentioned in any 2026 Conservative release found.

### Cited Findings
- The conservativebc.ca/plan hub (fetched 2026-10-09) lists 10 plan items dated Oct 5-8: $1000 Tax Cut, Safe Schools, Insurance, Repeal DRIPA, Safe Streets, Keep Emergency Rooms Open, BC Energy Superpower Plan, Wildfire Action Plan, No PST on Canadian Alcohol, No New Taxes. There is no housing, carbon or Surrey item - [Conservative BC plan hub](https://conservativebc.ca/plan/)
- BC Ballot's page of 94 Conservative commitments (updated 2026-10-09) has a single housing entry: lower the cost of building and buying a home so more homes get built (Sept 25). It finds no mention of speculation tax, Bill 44/46/47, rent, rent control, carbon, OBPS, CleanBC, climate target, deficit, balanced budget, Surrey, SkyTrain, Surrey police or extortion - [BC Ballot, Conservative commitments](https://bcballot.ca/platforms/conservative-party/)
- Doerkson's Sept 25 statement responds to Eby's housing tax plan (2% of assessed value on condos left unsold more than a year, rising 1 point a year; speculation and vacancy tax raised to 2% domestic and 5% foreign). Quotes: "There is no problem the NDP don't think can be fixed by raising your taxes"; "NDP taxes and red tape are the reason homes are too expensive"; "every new tax on housing ends up in the price a family pays." The statement does not mention the speculation tax by name as something to scrap, nor Bill 44, zoning, a rebate or rent. It says a Conservative government would lower the cost of building and buying, and the release did not specify how - [Conservative BC statement](https://conservativebc.ca/statement-from-interim-leader-lorne-doerkson-on-the-ndps-housing-tax-plan/); [The Progress, Sept 25](https://theprogress.com/2026/09/25/eby-promises-increase-to-b-c-s-speculation-tax-create-new-unsold-condo-tax/); [Building.ca](https://building.ca/b-c-s-eby-says-ndp-would-tax-new-unsold-condos-raise-speculation-vacancy-fees/)
- Doerkson's statement also says Eby "promised every family $1,000 last election and cancelled it before his first budget" (a reference to the NDP's 2024 cost-of-living credit) - [Conservative BC statement](https://conservativebc.ca/statement-from-interim-leader-lorne-doerkson-on-the-ndps-housing-tax-plan/) (relayed via fetch summary, short excerpt only)
- Pledge of "no new taxes and no increases to existing taxes" for the full term, stated Sept 27 in Surrey - [Conservative BC release](https://conservativebc.ca/conservative-party-of-bc-pledges-no-new-taxes-on-british-columbians/); [Business Examiner](https://businessexaminer.ca/bc-news/item/bc-conservatives-rule-out-any-tax-increases-under-a-doerkson-government/) (via search summary; page returned 403). A pledge to hold existing taxes steady is compatible with keeping, but does not say whether the party keeps or scraps, the existing speculation tax. This is not a stated position on it.
- Metro Vancouver (Oct 5): strip the regional district to water and sewage, returning "regional housing, economic development, air quality and climate policy" to the province and municipalities; smaller board; no cost savings estimate, schedule or funding plan; Metro Vancouver's chair called the "out of control" claim a political attack, and Metro says under 8% of its operating budget goes to non-utility services. The article does not discuss municipal amalgamation - [Daily Hive](https://dailyhive.com/vancouver/bc-conservatives-metro-vancouver-regional-district-reform-platform-promise); [party release](https://conservativebc.ca/conservative-platform-delivering-essential-services-and-cutting-waste-at-metro-vancouver/)
- Seniors plan (Oct 9, Coquitlam): reverse the NDP's changes to the property-tax deferment program ("a punitive tax on our seniors"), review long-term-care design rules to build beds sooner, make long-term-care funding portable across qualified public or non-government providers, expand publicly funded home care with accredited provider choice, expand HandyDART, a Long-Term Care Access and Transparency Act with monthly wait-list reporting. No price tag - [Sitka Media](https://www.sitkamedia.ca/watch-bc-conservatives-unveil-plan-to-support-seniors/); [Global News Oct 9](https://globalnews.ca/news/12097726/bc-leaders-campaign-trail-health-care-supporting-seniors-october-9); [Daily Hive](https://dailyhive.com/vancouver/bc-conservatives-property-tax-deferment-program-promise)

2024 baseline (NOT confirmed for 2026), for comparison:
- Rustad Rebate: exempt up to $3,000 a month of rent or mortgage interest from provincial income tax - [Storeys](https://storeys.com/bc-conservatives-housing-plan-election-2024/); a search summary of CBC/CFJC coverage also says $3,000 - [CFJC](https://cfjctoday.com/?p=990527). The earlier note in this folder cited $1,500 a month from CBC; I could not reproduce that (the CBC page returned 403). Treat $3,000 as the better-supported figure and the $1,500 figure as unresolved. Cost: Rustad said about $3 billion a year once fully implemented - [Global News 2024](https://globalnews.ca/news/10812384/affordability-safety-scrapping-carbon-price-bc-conservatives-full-platform)
- Rezoning and permit limits: rezoning and development permits within six months, building permits within three months, with the province issuing permits itself if cities miss deadlines; pre-zoning with cities - [Storeys](https://storeys.com/bc-conservatives-housing-plan-election-2024/)
- Bill 44: sources conflict. Global's platform summary says repeal "unless the municipalities support it for their community" - [Global News 2024](https://globalnews.ca/news/10812384/affordability-safety-scrapping-carbon-price-bc-conservatives-full-platform). Storeys says the plan appeared to back off repeal and instead offered a $1-billion-a-year Civic Infrastructure Renewal Fund to municipalities allowing small-scale multi-unit housing on at least two-thirds of residential land - [Storeys](https://storeys.com/bc-conservatives-housing-plan-election-2024/). Bill 47 (transit-oriented areas): amend to require space for grocery stores, small business and services nearby (Storeys). Bill 46 not found in either source.
- Speculation and vacancy tax 2024: the platform summaries do not list it. The party leader declined to commit to cancelling it ("something we need to look at"), while candidate Mike Morden was recorded promising to repeal it; these claims come from NDP releases and coverage of them - [BC NDP release](https://www.bcndp.ca/releases/new-video-shows-rustad-and-his-candidates-would-scrap-speculation-tax) (partisan source); [CFJC](https://cfjctoday.com/?p=990527). The earlier assumption that the 2024 platform pledged to "scrap" the tax is not supported by the platform summaries read here.
- Rent control: the NDP claimed Rustad said "I'm not a fan of rental control" in a private meeting - [BC NDP release](https://www.bcndp.ca/releases/rustads-plan-raise-rent-caps-could-cost-renters-hundreds-dollars-month) (partisan, unverified). No Conservative platform text on rent control found for 2024 or 2026.
- Findlay-era (2026): reported first housing steps were cutting red tape, firm approval timelines and defending property rights; coverage called housing one of her least detailed areas - [search summary of BIV Q&A and Rob Shaw column](https://www.biv.com/news/commentary/rob-shaw-findlay-offers-first-glimpse-of-bc-conservative-policy-reset-12363252) (page 403; unverified). She resigned Sept 20, so this is not a current commitment.
- March 2025 party convention policy declaration (20 pages): housing "without unnecessary bureaucratic interference" (Article 19); no mention of Bill 44 per the Tyee analysis - [The Tyee, Sept 14, 2026](https://thetyee.ca/Analysis/2026/09/14/BC-Conservative-Platform-Road-Map-1950s/) (opinion analysis of the declaration, not the document itself)

### Inferences
- Inference: the 2026 housing message is tax-and-red-tape framed ("no new taxes", lower cost to build). That is consistent with keeping a 2024 direction of opposing new housing taxes, but there is no 2026 evidence on the speculation tax, the density mandates, the rebate or timed approvals. A tool should score the Conservatives on housing using only: oppose the NDP's new unsold-condo tax and higher speculation tax, cut red tape, no new taxes, Metro Vancouver housing powers returned to province and municipalities. Do not attribute the rebate, Bill 44 repeal or six-month limits to 2026.
- Inference: the rebate (about $3B/year in 2024) sits awkwardly with the 2026 framing; the 2026 $32,000 exemption is the tax-relief centrepiece instead. Not stated by the party.

### Gaps
- No Conservative statement on the speculation and vacancy tax, Bill 44/46/47, the rebate or rezoning deadlines in any 2026 source found. Check conservativebc.ca/plan again before launch; a housing plan could still be released.
- The 2024 platform PDF was not read; rebate cap ($1,500 vs $3,000) and Bill 44 handling (repeal vs incentive fund) conflict between sources.
- Doerkson has not been reported answering rent control questions.

## 2. 2026 position on industrial carbon pricing (OBPS), Low Carbon Fuel Standard, CleanBC and the 2030 target

### Takeaway
No 2026 Conservative release or direct quote on any of these was found. The consumer carbon tax is already gone (ended by the NDP April 1, 2025), so "scrap the carbon tax" is no longer a live promise. The only documented Conservative positions on industrial pricing, the LCFS and CleanBC date from 2024-2025.

### Cited Findings
- The 94 BC Ballot-listed commitments contain no entry on carbon pricing, OBPS, CleanBC, climate targets or emissions caps. The only climate-adjacent item is working with Ottawa so BC gets emissions credit when its LNG replaces coal - [BC Ballot](https://bcballot.ca/platforms/conservative-party/); [party LNG release](https://conservativebc.ca/bc-conservatives-welcome-lng-canada-phase-2-we-will-treat-energy-as-an-urgent-mission/)
- Energy platform (Sept 28-29): double LNG production by 2032 and triple by 2035; a 120-day permitting law for major projects over $250 million; "get projects to yes while keeping environmental standards" - [party release](https://conservativebc.ca/make-british-columbia-an-energy-superpower/); [party economy release](https://conservativebc.ca/supercharge-british-columbias-economy/)
- Doerkson's 2024 statement on joining the Conservatives cited "axing the carbon tax" as a reason for change of government - [Western Standard Day 8 via search summary](https://www.westernstandard.news/bc/day-8-of-the-2026-bc-election-carney-comes-to-town/77389) (secondary; not independently confirmed)
- Industrial pricing, 2025 record: on March 31, 2025 in Question Period Rustad asked whether Eby would "add a double backflip to his routine and axe the industrial carbon tax?", said "our industry is struggling. People don't want to invest in this province," and called the carbon tax a "job killer." Eby replied that Rustad had voted for the BC Liberal-era tax. Rustad earlier said BC "cannot just shift this tax to industry" - [Western Standard, April 1, 2025](https://www.westernstandard.news/amp/story/watch/watch-rustad-calls-on-bc-ndp-to-axe-industrial-carbon-tax/63616); [Global News](https://globalnews.ca/news/11032579/rustad-coal-carbon-tax-tariff/) (search summary)
- 2024 platform: scrap the carbon price including "hidden carbon taxes"; the Global summary does not mention industrial pricing, the LCFS, CleanBC or the 2030 target - [Global News 2024](https://globalnews.ca/news/10812384/affordability-safety-scrapping-carbon-price-bc-conservatives-full-platform). The Starfish's 2024 overview says the party planned to remove the LCFS alongside the carbon tax and to remove the carbon tax "regardless of the federal government's directive"; it does not say what they would do about industrial pricing or CleanBC - [The Starfish Canada](https://thestarfish.ca/journal/2024/10/an-overview-of-bc-party-climate-agendas-for-the-2024-election) (search-summary-level verification; this is the only source for the LCFS pledge)
- March 2025 convention declaration: will "scrap the carbon tax" and repeal "harmful" NDP taxes and fees; the declaration never uses the term "climate change"; backs pipelines and reversing the NDP's natural gas heating plans - [The Tyee, 2026-09-14](https://thetyee.ca/Analysis/2026/09/14/BC-Conservative-Platform-Road-Map-1950s/) (analysis article, not the document)
- Findlay-era (May to Sept 2026): reported pledge to scrap the carbon tax and a 12-month cut of every provincial tax on gasoline and diesel (up to 44 cents a litre per search summary) - [search summary citing BIV and Findlay campaign](https://www.biv.com/news/commentary/rob-shaw-findlay-offers-first-glimpse-of-bc-conservative-policy-reset-12363252) (403; unverified). Not carried into Doerkson's releases.
- Context (not Conservative policy): BC's OBPS replaced the CleanBC Industrial Incentive Program in April 2024 and is now BC's sole carbon pricing instrument; a Nov 2025 review recommended extending it to 2040; CleanBC targets are 40% below 2007 by 2030, 60% by 2040, 80% by 2050, and a review expects BC to fall short - [ICAP](https://icapcarbonaction.com/en/ets/canada-british-columbia-output-based-pricing-system); [CleanBC industry page](https://cleanbc.gov.bc.ca/about-climate-change/drivers/industry/); [Policy Note](https://www.policynote.ca/industrial-emissions/)

### Inferences
- Inference: no 2026 text commits the party to keeping or ending the OBPS, the LCFS, CleanBC or the 2030 target. The nearest evidence of direction is Rustad's 2025 call to axe the industrial carbon tax, the 2024 LCFS removal pledge (single source), the Doerkson 2026 LNG doubling and a 120-day permitting law, which together suggest low priority on emissions targets. This is an inference and should be shown as "no 2026 commitment found" in the tool.
- Inference: LNG doubling by 2032 would be hard to reconcile with the 2030 target as currently legislated; the party says it wants environmental standards kept in permitting and credit for coal displacement but has not addressed the legislated target.

### Gaps
- No direct 2026 quote from any Conservative leader on the OBPS, LCFS, CleanBC or the 2030 target. The Starfish LCFS claim is uncorroborated by a primary source. The 2024 platform PDF was not read.
- How the Conservative caucus voted on the 2025 consumer carbon tax repeal bill (Bill 8) was not found (also a gap in party_bc_conservative.md).

## 3. Costed platform, deficit path, and the $32,000 basic personal amount

### Takeaway
No costed platform or deficit path exists as of Oct 9. The party has not said when the budget would balance. The $32,000 basic personal amount has no party costing; NDP candidate Spencer Chandra Herbert says $2.5 billion and a Global News reporter's preliminary estimate is also about $2.5 billion. Doerkson says it is paid for by growth ("supercharge the economy") and says he will keep frontline services intact; no specific cuts named.

### Cited Findings
- Basic personal exemption raised from $13,216 to $32,000 ("the most generous in Canada"); up to $1,000 a year for an individual, about $2,000 a year or $167 a month for a typical two-income family; a full-time minimum-wage worker would pay no provincial income tax; people earning roughly $40,000 or more get the full $1,000; the party release includes no total cost - [party release 2026-10-08](https://conservativebc.ca/conservatives-will-cut-your-taxes-by-1000/); [Western Standard Day 18](https://www.westernstandard.news/bc/day-18-of-the-bc-provincial-election-the-agou-d%C3%A9j%C3%A0-vu/77743)
- Cost estimates: NDP candidate Spencer Chandra Herbert said it would "blow a $2.5 billion hole" in the budget and suggested health care cuts would follow (partisan claim) - [Canadian Press via Medicine Hat News](https://medicinehatnews.com/news/national-news/2026/10/08/b-c-campaign-trail-conservatives-promise-1000-tax-cut-by-boosting-exemption-level/). Global News reporter Ben O'Hara-Byrne estimated a preliminary figure of about $2.5 billion; the Western Standard calls this a reporter's calculation, not a Conservative costing - [Western Standard Day 18](https://www.westernstandard.news/bc/day-18-of-the-bc-provincial-election-the-agou-d%C3%A9j%C3%A0-vu/77743). The two $2.5B figures are not independent evidence of each other; I found no official estimate.
- Funding: Doerkson said "we have to supercharge the economy in British Columbia and get things going because that is our revenue," pointing to LNG, forestry and mining, and said he is "absolutely committed to keeping those frontline services intact." The article names no specific cuts - [Daily Hive, Oct 8](https://dailyhive.com/vancouver/bc-conservatives-election-annual-income-tax-cut-promise). The NDP's own tax plan (new bracket above $1 million, higher top rates) is estimated by the NDP at about $1 billion a year - same source.
- Benefit shape: the Western Standard's arithmetic puts the benefit at about $1,050 at the 5.6% rate, capped and not growing with income (about $940 at $30,000 and $1,050 at $1 million) - [Western Standard Day 18](https://www.westernstandard.news/bc/day-18-of-the-bc-provincial-election-the-agou-d%C3%A9j%C3%A0-vu/77743) (partisan-leaning outlet, arithmetic is checkable)
- Deficit: the province's projected 2026-27 deficit is $13.8 billion per the Ministry of Finance Sept update; no Conservative release gives a balance date or deficit target - [Canadian Press via Medicine Hat News](https://medicinehatnews.com/news/national-news/2026/10/08/b-c-campaign-trail-conservatives-promise-1000-tax-cut-by-boosting-exemption-level/); [BC Ballot](https://bcballot.ca/platforms/conservative-party/)
- Spending cuts named: none found for the income tax cut. Other fiscal items: PST scrapped on machinery, equipment and business software for three years (the only item a Global News article describes as costed, without a dollar figure); PST removed on Canadian alcohol during the trade war; an expert panel on tax simplification within 180 days; red tape to 2017 levels; Metro Vancouver rollback with no savings figure - [Global News Oct 8](https://globalnews.ca/news/12092479/b-c-conservatives-back-in-battleground-surrey-on-election-trail/); [party release](https://conservativebc.ca/supercharge-british-columbias-economy/)
- Spending adds without costing: $50M health emergency fund, 250 police via JIBC, 500 RCMP and 150 civilian staff over five years, 50 Crown prosecutors, seniors plan; BC Ballot and the releases show no price tags - [BC Ballot](https://bcballot.ca/platforms/conservative-party/); [Sitka Media](https://www.sitkamedia.ca/watch-bc-conservatives-unveil-plan-to-support-seniors/)
- 2024 baseline: eliminate the nearly $9 billion deficit "within two terms"; voter approval for any new tax - [Global News 2024](https://globalnews.ca/news/10812384/affordability-safety-scrapping-carbon-price-bc-conservatives-full-platform). March 2025 party declaration: "dollar-for-dollar" rule that new non-essential spending be matched by savings (the term "non-essential" is undefined) - [The Tyee](https://thetyee.ca/Analysis/2026/09/14/BC-Conservative-Platform-Road-Map-1950s/). Neither is repeated in 2026 releases found.

### Inferences
- Inference: with a $13.8B deficit, a roughly $2.5B-a-year income tax cut (unofficial estimate), "no new taxes" and "no cuts to health care," the 2026 materials do not show a balance date. The 2024 "two terms" aim and the dollar-for-dollar rule are the last stated fiscal discipline commitments, and they are not reaffirmed in 2026. A tool should say "no deficit target stated in 2026."

### Gaps
- No official costing of the $32,000 exemption; both $2.5B figures are third-party or partisan. The Conservatives may release a costed platform before Oct 16; recheck.
- Doerkson's Oct 13-14 debate statements on the deficit do not exist yet.
- Whether the party's 2024 "two terms" balance target still stands is not stated.

## 4. Has Doerkson confirmed or changed Rustad- or Findlay-era positions on private health delivery, ICBC, rent control, Metro Vancouver amalgamation?

### Takeaway
Health delivery: 2026 releases state "universal and publicly funded" and do not mention private clinics, while the Oct 9 seniors plan opens long-term care and home care funding to "non-government" accredited providers. ICBC: Doerkson has confirmed and extended the Findlay and Rustad-era direction of ending no-fault in favour of a "hybrid" with tort rights for serious injury, per a leaked Sept 29 letter. Rent control and amalgamation: no Doerkson statement found. Metro Vancouver: a rollback, not amalgamation.

### Cited Findings
- Health: Doerkson "committed to protecting health care funding and keeping BC's system universal and publicly funded" and to "ensuring British Columbians never need anything more than their health card to get care"; no mention of private clinics, private delivery or private partnerships in the release - [party release 2026-10-05](https://conservativebc.ca/keep-emergency-rooms-open/) (BC Ballot's paraphrase of "not private" is its own wording, not found in the release text). Other items: 100-day expert commission on wait times, $50M emergency fund, rural practice support, train more doctors, no cuts.
- Seniors plan (Oct 9): long-term care funding made portable so eligible seniors "choose a qualified public or non-government provider"; publicly funded home care with seniors choosing an accredited provider - [Sitka Media](https://www.sitkamedia.ca/watch-bc-conservatives-unveil-plan-to-support-seniors/)
- 2024 baseline: July 18, 2024 plan for a publicly funded system delivered through "government and non-government facilities, including private, out-of-province and public providers"; patients waiting too long sent out of province; rehire staff dismissed over COVID vaccination; no cost estimate, "initial spike" in spending acknowledged; the NDP said it implied $4 billion in cuts (partisan) - [Global News 2024](https://globalnews.ca/news/10631278/bc-conservatives-hybrid-health-care-system); [CBC](https://amp.cbc.ca/lite/story/1.7268626)
- Findlay campaign site: repeal Bill 36 (HPOA) to "restore medical freedom and self-regulation"; "Repeal ICBC's no-fault system" - [voteklf.ca](https://voteklf.ca/) (campaign site; not a party platform)
- ICBC (2026): Doerkson's letter dated Sept 29, 2026 to the Insurance Bureau of Canada, Law Society of BC, Trial Lawyers Association of BC and CBA-BC commits to legislation within a year of election for a "hybrid system"; "Accident victims will get back their right to recover for their serious injuries"; cites Ontario and Alberta models. The letter was leaked by the union representing ICBC workers. Eby called it a "secret deal" with trial lawyers and warned of a doubling of premiums; Doerkson said he made "no promises" and that no-fault "has not supported" catastrophically injured people well. CTV's summary says the letter also says everything is on the table, including private competition and restoring limits on minor-injury claims; the letter gives no premium estimate - [Global News](https://globalnews.ca/news/12090331/bc-conservatives-plan-no-fault-insurance-repeal/); [CP24, Oct 6](https://www.cp24.com/news/canada/2026/10/06/bc-conservatives-plan-to-end-no-fault-insurance-leaked-letter-shows/); [CTV](https://www.ctvnews.ca/vancouver/article/bc-conservatives-plan-to-end-no-fault-insurance-leaked-letter-shows/) (private competition detail from search summary)
- Public ICBC page (Oct 7): "meaningful rights to full compensation" for seriously injured people; lawyer fees under law society caps; expand choice "where competition improves price and service"; keep ICBC as a public insurance option with service standards; "hybrid" is not defined and the page says it "eliminates the overwhelming majority of litigation and legal costs" - [party insurance page](https://conservativebc.ca/plan/insurance/). Source conflict worth noting: the leaked letter (restore court recovery for serious injury) and the party page (eliminate most litigation) describe the same plan differently. CentreBC leader Elenore Sturko asked Doerkson to publish the full proposal - [Canadian Press via Medicine Hat News](https://medicinehatnews.com/news/national-news/2026/10/08/b-c-campaign-trail-conservatives-promise-1000-tax-cut-by-boosting-exemption-level/)
- Rent control: no 2026 position found. Only an NDP release claiming Rustad said "I'm not a fan of rental control" - [BC NDP release](https://www.bcndp.ca/releases/rustads-plan-raise-rent-caps-could-cost-renters-hundreds-dollars-month) (partisan, unverified). OneBC, not the Conservatives, pledges to block municipal rent control per the earlier note in election_landscape.md.
- Metro Vancouver: rollback, no amalgamation proposal; returns housing, economic development, air quality and climate policy to province and municipalities; keep utility politics out - [Daily Hive](https://dailyhive.com/vancouver/bc-conservatives-metro-vancouver-regional-district-reform-platform-promise)
- Leadership confirmation of positions: Doerkson (interim) is the leader who issued all of these releases; as interim leader he cannot seek the permanent leadership; the party said Sept 23 it would seek a constitutional change so he could stay if he becomes premier - [party_bc_conservative.md (Wikipedia: Lorne Doerkson)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lorne_Doerkson)

### Inferences
- Inference: Doerkson has kept the ICBC no-fault repeal direction and the DRIPA and SOGI repeals (already in existing notes), softened the 2024 explicit private-delivery language to a "universal and publicly funded" message while still opening long-term care and home care to non-government providers. This should be shown as "private clinics: no 2026 statement; non-government providers in seniors care: yes."
- Inference: ICBC specifics are unresolved and the party has said "no promises"; a tool should describe it as "end no-fault in favour of a hybrid with court recovery for serious injury" and note premium effects are disputed and unquantified.

### Gaps
- No Doerkson statement on rent control, amalgamation or on whether private clinics from the 2024 plan remain. No platform document to check.
- The CTV article's private-competition claim is from a search summary.

## 5. Surrey commitments: Surrey Police Service, SkyTrain, Cloverdale hospital, extortion

### Takeaway
None found. The party has campaigned in Surrey on Sept 27, Oct 7 and Oct 8 but announced province-wide policy there. There is no Conservative 2026 commitment on the Surrey Police Service transition, the Surrey-Langley SkyTrain, a Cloverdale hospital or extortion.

### Cited Findings
- Surrey has 10 of 93 ridings; the Conservatives won six of Surrey's 10 seats in 2024; Elenore Sturko, one of those MLAs, now leads rival CentreBC; the NDP's Garry Begg won Surrey-Guildford by 22 votes in 2024 - [Global News Oct 8](https://globalnews.ca/news/12092479/b-c-conservatives-back-in-battleground-surrey-on-election-trail/)
