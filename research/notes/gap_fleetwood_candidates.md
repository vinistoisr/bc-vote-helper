Scope: gap-closing note. It builds on riding_surrey_fleetwood.md and legislative_voting_record.md in the same folder. All dollar figures CAD. No em dashes used. Where a source is a party's own page, that is stated, and the content is the party's characterization.

Headline for the tool designer: no candidate in Surrey-Fleetwood has a published position that differs from their party. Candidate pages for four of five contain only biographies, and the NDP incumbent's page has nine generic party pillars plus four local commitments. Local scoring should therefore follow party positions, with the candidate layer limited to documented background and, for the incumbent, a voting record (party-line in all recorded divisions).

## 4. Jagrup Brar's record: key votes and statements

### Takeaway
Brar voted yea (with his caucus) on every recorded division checked: Bill 36, 44, 46 (2023), Bills 7, 14, 15 (2025) and the 2026 budget measures bill. BC Ballot reports he was present for all 105 recorded divisions in the 43rd Parliament and never broke with his party. I found no statement from him on the Gitxaala ruling, DRIPA amendments, or Surrey extortion.

### Cited Findings
Votes (I extracted the names lists myself from Votes and Proceedings; "Brar" is the only member of that surname; Jagrup Brar is listed in the Yeas each time):
- Bill 36, Police Amendment Act, 2023, third reading, Oct 25, 2023, passed 56-27: Brar Yea - [Votes and Proceedings Oct 25, 2023](https://lims.leg.bc.ca/pdms/ldp/42nd4th/votes/v231025.htm)
- Bill 44 (small-scale multi-unit housing) and Bill 46 (development financing), third readings Nov 29, 2023, passed 45-29 and 44-28: Brar Yea on both - [Votes and Proceedings Nov 29, 2023](https://lims.leg.bc.ca/pdms/ldp/42nd4th/votes/v231129.htm)
- Bill 47 (transit-oriented areas), Nov 30, 2023: passed nemine contradicente with no recorded names, so no individual vote exists; all parties supported it - [Votes and Proceedings Nov 30, 2023](https://lims.leg.bc.ca/pdms/ldp/42nd4th/votes/v231130.htm)
- Bill 7, Economic Stabilization (Tariff Response) Act: second reading Apr 3, 2025 (48-43) and third reading May 28, 2025 (48-44): Brar Yea on both - [V&P Apr 3, 2025](https://lims.leg.bc.ca/pdms/ldp/43rd1st/votes/v250403.htm), [V&P May 28, 2025](https://lims.leg.bc.ca/pdms/ldp/43rd1st/votes/v250528.htm)
- Bills 14 (Renewable Energy Projects) and 15 (Infrastructure Projects), third readings May 28, 2025, each tied 46-46 and passed on the Speaker's casting vote: Brar Yea on both - [V&P May 28, 2025](https://lims.leg.bc.ca/pdms/ldp/43rd1st/votes/v250528.htm). (An earlier note says CBC reported 47-46; the Votes and Proceedings show the tie and casting vote.)
- Bill 3, Budget Measures Implementation Act (No. 2), 2026, third reading Mar 5, 2026, passed 46-42: Brar Yea. The nays included the Conservative, Green (Valeriote, Botterell), OneBC and independent members - [V&P Mar 5, 2026](https://lims.leg.bc.ca/pdms/ldp/43rd2nd/votes/v260305.htm)
- Procedural: Brar also voted yea on Bill 32 (Mental Health Amendment Act (No. 2), 2025) and Bill 24 third readings on Dec 2, 2025, and yea on the first reading of independent Tara Armstrong's Drug Recovery and Community Safety Act (84-2, Mar 5, 2026; first readings are routinely allowed, so this is not an endorsement). He voted nay on first reading of independents' Property Rights Protection Act (Oct 29, 2025), Gender Ideology and Child Protection Act (Feb 19, 2026) and Human Rights Code Repeal Act (Feb 26, 2026) - [V&P Dec 2, 2025](https://lims.leg.bc.ca/pdms/ldp/43rd1st/votes/v251202.htm), [V&P Oct 29, 2025](https://lims.leg.bc.ca/pdms/ldp/43rd1st/votes/v251029.htm), [V&P Feb 19, 2026](https://lims.leg.bc.ca/pdms/ldp/43rd2nd/votes/v260219.htm), [V&P Feb 26, 2026](https://lims.leg.bc.ca/pdms/ldp/43rd2nd/votes/v260226.htm). These per-division readings came from my own text extraction; the Armstrong and mid-list items are lower confidence than the headline votes above because I parsed them with a script.
- BC Ballot's profile: present for all 105 divisions in the 43rd Parliament, "never broke with his party"; 71 bills and motions listed, 50 For and 21 Against; six runs, five wins; 2024 campaign raised $105,649 and spent $107,989; salary at dissolution $183,064. The page was truncated in my read - [BC Ballot, Jagrup Brar](https://bcballot.ca/mlas/jagrup-brar/) (third-party aggregator)
- Position: Minister of Mining and Critical Minerals since Nov 18, 2024 and still the minister at dissolution; his 2026-27 service plan is dated Feb 6, 2026 and signed by him - [BC Budget, Ministry service plan](https://www.bcbudget.gov.bc.ca/2026/sp/pdf/mine.pdf) (PDF text not extractable by me; date and signature from a search summary), [BC Ballot](https://bcballot.ca/mlas/jagrup-brar/)

Statements as minister:
- Jan 27, 2026, on the Eskay Creek mine approval under the first Declaration Act Section 7 consent agreement: "The Eskay Creek mine is a priority project for our government, and I am pleased to see it moving forward following careful decision-making by both the Tahltan Central Government and the provincial government." He also called it an "important milestone toward meaningful reconciliation" (quote truncated in my read). Premier Eby's quote in the same release said those who want to end work with First Nations "would not have delivered these thousands of jobs" - [BC Government release, Jan 27, 2026](https://news.gov.bc.ca/releases/2026MCM0008-000078)
- Permits: a Sept 22, 2026 ministry release says mineral-exploration permits are being approved quicker under a new process; in a May 2026 interview (search summary) Brar claimed about a dozen critical-mineral projects worth about $48 billion and about 35 percent less permitting time - [BC Government news](https://news.gov.bc.ca/ministries/mining-and-critical-minerals), [search summary of the interview]; I did not open the interview itself
- Context: the BC Court of Appeal's Gitxaala ruling (Dec 5, 2025, 2-1) held the mineral claims regime inconsistent with UNDRIP Article 32(2); the province sought leave and the Supreme Court of Canada granted leave to appeal on May 21, 2026 - [Wikipedia, Gitxaala v. British Columbia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gitxaa%C5%82a_v._British_Columbia) (secondary), [Blakes](https://www.blakes.com/insights/b-c-court-of-appeal-rules-previous-free-entry-mineral-tenure-regime-inconsistent-with-undrip/)
- DRIPA amendment status (updates earlier notes): after the ruling Eby said the Act needed to be amended, then proposed a time-limited suspension of certain sections; NDP MLA Joan Phillip said she did not support it; the suspension would not be a confidence matter; then the premier's office said no DRIPA legislation would be introduced in that session and the government would pursue negotiations with First Nations. Exact dates not confirmed - [CTV News](https://www.ctvnews.ca/vancouver/article/dripa-pause-wont-be-confidence-vote-bc-election-prospect-recedes/), [Castanet](https://www.castanetkamloops.net/news/BC/609779/Premier-David-Eby-backs-down-again-on-B-C-Indigenous-law). This updates the landscape note's "DRIPA amendments shelved April 2026".
- Brar's campaign page (party-authored): nine "Progress For You" pillars, including standing up to Trump, strengthening health care, lowering costs, bringing housing costs down, safe inclusive communities and gun laws, mental health and addictions care, protecting air land and water, and supporting education. Local commitments: a new hospital, extending SkyTrain, a stronger economy with good jobs, more housing supply. No timelines, costings or locations. Bio: former executive director of SEEDS; lives in Surrey with wife and two children. It says Conservatives would "cozy up to Trump, cut healthcare, and hike costs on working people" - [Jagrup Brar campaign site](https://jagrupbrar.bcndp.ca)

### Inferences
- Because he voted with the NDP caucus on every recorded division I checked, his record is a proxy for NDP government votes on police (Surrey transition), housing supply (Bills 44, 46), tariff response and fast-track permitting (Bills 7, 14, 15) and the 2026 budget. Local scoring of the NDP candidate can use the NDP party scoring without adjustment.
- His ministerial work on a consent-based Section 7 agreement shows he implements DRIPA in practice; his silence on the Gitxaala ruling in the sources I found means no personal position can be stated.
