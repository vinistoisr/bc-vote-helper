# Conservative Party of BC: 2026 platform, record and likely direction

Research date: 2026-10-09. Election: voting day 2026-10-24 (snap election called 2026-09-22 by Premier Eby; fixed date would have been 2028-10-21 per Wikipedia). Scope: BC Conservatives only. Method note: most figures below come from the party's own pages (via the BC Ballot tracker, which indexes party releases), Canadian Press copy on CP24/CTV/Global, and Wikipedia as a secondary aggregator. Elections BC, BC Hansard and Votes and Proceedings were NOT directly reached, so legislature vote tallies rely on news reports. The platform is still being rolled out in pieces (BC Ballot reported 94 commitments as of 2026-10-09, all dated after the 2026-09-22 writ). No single costed platform document was found.

Convention: (a) platform and stated positions, (b) actions and record, (c) inference, labelled as inference.

## 1. Who leads the party now, and the complete leadership timeline since 2024

### Takeaway
The party is led by an interim leader, Lorne Doerkson (MLA for Cariboo-Chilcotin), appointed by the board on 2026-09-20 after Kerry-Lynne Findlay resigned after 113 days. Since the 2024 election the party has had four leaders in sequence (Rustad, Halford interim, Findlay, Doerkson interim), each change driven by caucus or board revolt.

  - 2024-10-19: Rustad becomes Leader of the Opposition after the party wins 44 seats (best result in over 70 years per Wikipedia); NDP won 47 - [Wikipedia: John Rustad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Rustad); seat counts also in [CP24 on Findlay resignation](https://www.cp24.com/politics/2026/09/20/kerry-lynne-findlay-resigns-as-bc-conservative-leader/)

### Inferences
- Inference: the party goes into the election with a first-time interim leader, no permanent leadership mandate, and a platform assembled in the 17 days after the writ, which raises uncertainty about how closely a Doerkson government would follow it.
- Inference: because the repeated MLA exits were largely over internal management and Findlay's leadership rather than over policy (Bird and Banman excepted, who cited direction/electability), the policy platform appears more stable than the leadership.

### Gaps
- Elections BC and the BC Legislature were not reached directly; seat counts and caucus membership are from news and Wikipedia and vary by date.
- The CTV article "caucus is down to 30, how it lost 14 members" returned only a headline; the full list of departures is not independently verified beyond the Global timeline.
- Whether a permanent leadership race will occur after the election is not reported in the sources found.

## 2. 2026 platform: commitments and costings

### Takeaway
The 2026 platform is a set of themed releases (Sept 23 to Oct 8) rather than one costed document. Only a few figures are stated; the party has published no overall fiscal plan, and the main new headline items are a $1,000 income tax cut via a higher basic personal exemption, "no new taxes", repeal of DRIPA and SOGI, ending "safe supply" with compassionate intervention, and doubling LNG by 2032.

### Cited Findings
Source for the item list and dates unless otherwise noted: [BC Ballot, Conservative commitments page (updated 2026-10-09)](https://bcballot.ca/platforms/conservative-party/), which links each item to the party release. Party plan hub: [conservativebc.ca/plan](https://conservativebc.ca/plan/).

Taxes
- "No new taxes" and no increase to existing taxes for the whole time in government (2026-09-27). The party points to the NDP's lowest-bracket rate rise from 5.06% to 5.6% and a bracket freeze through 2030 as a "quiet tax hike". No costings were provided; the article cites a projected provincial deficit of $13.8 billion - [Global News 2026-09-27](https://globalnews.ca/news/12075070/bc-election-september-27-conservative-platform/); [party release](https://conservativebc.ca/conservative-party-of-bc-pledges-no-new-taxes-on-british-columbians/)
- Raise the basic personal exemption from $13,216 to $32,000; claimed saving about $1,000 a year for nearly every working British Columbian and about $2,000 for an average couple (about $167/month for a typical two-income family). The release states no cost and no funding source, and does not mention the carbon tax or deficit - [party release 2026-10-08](https://conservativebc.ca/conservatives-will-cut-your-taxes-by-1000/)
- Scrap PST for three years on immediate-investment machinery, equipment and business software; expert panel to report in 180 days on simplifying taxes - [party release](https://conservativebc.ca/supercharge-british-columbias-economy/) via BC Ballot
- Remove the PST at the till on Canadian beer, wine and spirits during the US trade war; keep US alcohol off shelves - [party release](https://conservativebc.ca/conservatives-to-remove-pst-from-canadian-alcohol/); reported by [Surrey Now-Leader](https://surreynowleader.com/2026/09/29/doerkson-promises-to-cut-pst-on-canadian-alcohol/)

Housing
- Only one housing commitment listed: "Bring down the cost of building and buying a home" (2026-09-25), framed as a response to the NDP's proposed tax on unsold condos and higher speculation and vacancy tax; blames NDP taxes and red tape - [party statement](https://conservativebc.ca/statement-from-interim-leader-lorne-doerkson-on-the-ndps-housing-tax-plan/); [Global News](https://globalnews.ca/news/12075070/bc-election-september-27-conservative-platform/)
- Metro Vancouver: "roll back uncontrolled bloat"; return Metro's housing, economic development, air quality and climate policy to local and provincial governments; apply subsidiarity tests (do not do what another level or the private sector does) - [party release 2026-10-05](https://conservativebc.ca/conservative-platform-delivering-essential-services-and-cutting-waste-at-metro-vancouver/)

Health
- Keep emergency rooms open, recruit and retain doctors and nurses, expert commission within 100 days to cut wait times, $50 million health care emergency fund, rural practice support, train more doctors in BC, bring home Canadians who studied medicine abroad, speed credential recognition, keep system universal and publicly funded, "no cuts" - [party release 2026-10-05](https://conservativebc.ca/keep-emergency-rooms-open/)

DRIPA and Indigenous relations
- "Repeal DRIPA" (first stated 2026-09-23) while promoting "economic reconciliation" and building projects with First Nations. Doerkson: DRIPA "has to be repealed", the province is "in court everywhere", and it "hasn't helped" relations. The party plan page for this topic is thin; no replacement framework is described and Cowichan and aboriginal title are not mentioned on it - [party DRIPA page](https://conservativebc.ca/plan/first-nations-dripa/); [party release 2026-09-23](https://conservativebc.ca/every-conservative-party-of-bc-mla-approved-to-run-as-a-candidate/)
- LNG: "put First Nations at the centre of LNG development, with expanded ownership and partnership" - [party release](https://conservativebc.ca/make-british-columbia-an-energy-superpower/) (URL per BC Ballot key: make-british-columbia-an-energy-superpower)

SOGI and schools
- "SOGI has to be repealed" "while providing a safe place for our kids to go to school"; Doerkson calls SOGI "an absolute lightning rod for division". The 2026 page does not mention parental rights, replacement anti-bullying programs, or details - [party Safe Schools page 2026-10-07](https://conservativebc.ca/plan/safe-schools/)

Drugs, recovery, involuntary care
- "Compassionate intervention" to take people with severe addiction, mental illness or brain injury into care; amend the Mental Health Act so officers can connect people to mandatory treatment; end "safe supply"; expand naloxone; stop illegal drug use in hospitals, shelters and supportive housing; expand psychiatric services, crisis response and residential treatment; end open drug use in towns and cities - [party "Safe Streets for BC" 2026-10-06](https://conservativebc.ca/safe-streets-for-bc/)

Crime, bail, policing
- 250 more police via JIBC expansion; 500 RCMP officers plus 150 civilian staff over five years in rural and regional areas; 50 Crown prosecutors; work with Ottawa to fund 20 trial judges; more sheriff and court capacity; public reporting on repeat offending, bail compliance, court delays, treatment access. No costs stated. No specific bail law change stated (bail is federal). Extortion not mentioned in this release - [party "Safe Streets for BC"](https://conservativebc.ca/safe-streets-for-bc/)
- Rooting out "extremism": no public money or institutional legitimacy for organizations connected to terrorism or glorifying political violence (2026-10-07) - [party release](https://conservativebc.ca/october-7-rooting-out-extremism-at-home/)

Energy, LNG, pipelines
- Double LNG production by 2032 and triple by 2035; "BC Energy Superpower Plan"; faster approvals; competitive and stable LNG royalty framework; Montney Formation as a strategic asset; credit for BC emissions when LNG replaces coal; 120 Day Permitting Act for major projects over $250 million; harmonize with federal Bill C-39 to approve BC-led projects within one year - [party release 2026-09-28](https://conservativebc.ca/make-british-columbia-an-energy-superpower/); [party release 2026-09-29](https://conservativebc.ca/bc-conservatives-welcome-lng-canada-phase-2-we-will-treat-energy-as-an-urgent-mission/)
- Welcomes federal designation of the Pacific Link pipeline as a project of national interest; if premier, would request a trilateral working group with Alberta and Ottawa; the release does not mention the tanker ban. Doerkson also expressed support for a West Coast oil pipeline - [party release 2026-10-01](https://conservativebc.ca/conservative-party-of-bc-welcomes-designation-of-pacific-link-pipeline/); [Wikipedia: Lorne Doerkson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lorne_Doerkson)

Economy and public sector
- Red tape cut back to 2017 levels by end of first term; "one-in, two-out" red tape law; red tape reduction minister; Premier's Technology Council; targeted investment incentives - [party release 2026-10-07](https://conservativebc.ca/supercharge-british-columbias-economy/)

ICBC and insurance
- Restore "meaningful rights to full compensation" for seriously injured people through a "hybrid" system that the party says eliminates most litigation and legal costs, with legal fees within law society caps; keep ICBC as a public option with service standards; expand choice where competition improves price and service. No costs or premium estimates; "hybrid" is not defined on the page - [party insurance page](https://conservativebc.ca/plan/insurance/)

Wildfire
- Implement the Protect Communities from Wildfire Action Plan, procure firefighting aircraft, reduce forest fuels, easier prescribed fire, early-detection fund - [party release 2026-10-02](https://conservativebc.ca/fighting-wildfires-protecting-communities/)

Other
- $15 million toward the Filipino Cultural Centre in Vancouver - [party release](https://conservativebc.ca/conservatives-commit-15-million-to-build-the-filipino-cultural-centre/)
- No commitments found yet on transit, AI, child care, carbon tax, or a deficit path (BC Ballot lists "No commitment published" for AI and transportation) - [BC Ballot](https://bcballot.ca/platforms/conservative-party/)
- Surrey: party released a ten-name Surrey slate (including Garry Purewal in Surrey-Fleetwood and Trevor Halford in Surrey-White Rock). No Surrey-specific project commitments (SkyTrain to Langley, Surrey Police transition, Surrey hospital) were found - [Western Standard](https://www.westernstandard.news/amp/story/bc/day-9-of-the-2026-bc-election-national-day-of-truth-and-reconciliation/77440)

### Inferences
- Inference: the headline tax-cut package has no published costing, and combined with "no new taxes", "no cuts to health" and new spending items, the platform does not show how the projected $13.8B deficit would be addressed; the 2024 platform did include a stated aim to eliminate the deficit within two terms.
- Inference: the DRIPA repeal commitment lacks stated detail on what replaces it, so the actual legal approach (repeal, amendment, or repeal-and-replace) is unknown.

### Gaps
- No single published, costed 2026 platform document; the party site pages are summaries and the fetch tool returned incomplete text for several pages. Check conservativebc.ca/plan for later additions.
- Carbon tax: consumer carbon tax was already ended by the NDP in April 2025, so it is not a 2026 commitment; party position on the industrial carbon price in 2026 not found.
- Housing detail beyond one headline item, public sector size, child care, deficit targets, Surrey projects, extortion response, parental-rights policy: not found in 2026 releases.
- BC Ballot's own counts varied (66, 89, 48, 94 commitments across pages and dates); it is a third-party tracker, so confirm against party pages.

## 3. Comparison with the 2024 platform and what changed

### Takeaway
The 2024 platform ("Common Sense Change for B.C.") was a broader, partly costed document. In 2026 the DRIPA repeal and SOGI repeal remain, safety and addiction policy remain (mandatory treatment is now framed as "compassionate intervention"), but the Rustad Rebate, carbon tax scrap (already enacted by the NDP) and housing/Bill 44 detail do not appear in the 2026 releases found.

### Cited Findings
- 2024 platform title was "Common Sense Change for B.C.", released 2024-10-15 (the "Take Back BC" label in the research brief did not turn up in sources) - [Global News 2024-10-15](https://globalnews.ca/news/10812384/affordability-safety-scrapping-carbon-price-bc-conservatives-full-platform); [CBC](https://www.cbc.ca/1.7352588)
- 2024 tax and fiscal items: scrap the carbon price including "hidden carbon taxes"; cancel PST on affordable used cars; small business tax to 1%; review WorkSafeBC's $2.1B surplus; eliminate the nearly $9B deficit within two terms; voter approval for any new tax - [Global News 2024](https://globalnews.ca/news/10812384/affordability-safety-scrapping-carbon-price-bc-conservatives-full-platform)
- "Rustad Rebate": described as the largest housing tax cut in BC history, about $3B a year when fully implemented ($1.5B in year one). Reports differ on the monthly cap: CBC reported up to $1,500/month of housing costs exempted from income tax; a Ubyssey candidate profile said up to $3,000/month - [Global News 2024](https://globalnews.ca/news/10812384/affordability-safety-scrapping-carbon-price-bc-conservatives-full-platform); [CBC](https://www.cbc.ca/1.7352588); [Ubyssey](https://ubyssey.ca/news/point-grey-paul-ratchford-bc-conservatives-2024/) (conflicting; platform document not directly read)
- 2024 housing: repeal Bill 44 unless a municipality supports it (also described as a $1B/year Civic Infrastructure Renewal Fund as incentive); amend Bill 47; never force low-barrier housing on a community; end tent cities by moving people into services - [Global News 2024](https://globalnews.ca/news/10812384/affordability-safety-scrapping-carbon-price-bc-conservatives-full-platform); [Storeys](https://storeys.com/bc-conservatives-housing-plan-election-2024/)
- 2024 SOGI: replace SOGI 123 with zero-tolerance anti-bullying programs; remove SOGI 123 lesson plans and policy guides; ensure parents are immediately informed of a child's suicidal intentions; restore letter grades Grades 4-9 and provincial exams Grades 10 and 12 - [Global News 2024](https://globalnews.ca/news/10812384/affordability-safety-scrapping-carbon-price-bc-conservatives-full-platform)
- 2024 drugs and safety: lock up repeat and violent offenders; mandatory treatment for adults and youth; eliminate free hard drugs and paraphernalia - [Global News 2024](https://globalnews.ca/news/10812384/affordability-safety-scrapping-carbon-price-bc-conservatives-full-platform)
- 2024 Indigenous relations: return 20% of forests to First Nations; "honour UNDRIP as it was intended". The Global summary does not list a DRIPA repeal, but later reports state the party campaigned in 2024 to repeal DRIPA - [Global News 2024](https://globalnews.ca/news/10812384/affordability-safety-scrapping-carbon-price-bc-conservatives-full-platform); [Tyee 2026-02-10](https://thetyee.ca/News/2026/02/10/First-Nations-Would-Not-Exist-Without-Canada-Rustad/) (says the party "has promised to repeal DRIPA if it forms government")
- 2024 energy: approve proposed LNG plants; Canadian Northern Corridor - [Global News 2024](https://globalnews.ca/news/10812384/affordability-safety-scrapping-carbon-price-bc-conservatives-full-platform)
- Analysts noted the 2024 platform lacked a breakdown of costs for major capital promises - [Global News 2024](https://globalnews.ca/news/10807973/bc-conservatives-no-costed-platform-provincial-election)

### Inferences
- Inference: the 2026 platform is narrower and more tax-simple ("no new taxes", exemption increase) than 2024's fiscal framework, and drops the headline rent and mortgage deduction from the releases seen so far; whether the rebate is formally abandoned or just not yet re-announced is unknown.

### Gaps
- The actual 2024 platform PDF and costing document were not read; the DRIPA repeal pledge in 2024 is not confirmed from the platform text. Rebate cap conflicts between sources.
- No 2026 statement found on the Rustad Rebate, Bill 44 or Bill 47.

## 4. Record in opposition 2024-2026

### Takeaway
The Conservatives voted against the NDP's tariff-response and fast-track bills in 2025 (Bills 7, 14, 15), opposed process on the carbon tax repeal, pressed for the industrial carbon price to go too, and called for the Cowichan title case to go to the Supreme Court of Canada and DRIPA to be repealed.

### Cited Findings
- Bill 7 (Economic Stabilization (Tariff Response) Act) passed 48-44 with the Conservatives, Greens and independents opposed; the party said it granted excessive powers - [CFNR](https://www.cfnrfm.ca/2025/03/19/conservative-party-of-bc-urges-opposition-to-bill-7/); [CBC](https://www.cbc.ca/news/canada/british-columbia/bc-ndp-bill-15-1.7546636)
- Bills 14 and 15 (fast-track major projects) passed 2025-05-28; Conservatives, Greens and independents voted no. Tally reported as a 46-46 tie broken by Speaker Raj Chouhan (CBC/search summary) while Global reported 47-46 for Bill 15, a source conflict. Rustad disputed the vote after an NDP MLA voted by video with a blurred background ("Blurgate") - [CBC](https://www.cbc.ca/news/canada/british-columbia/bc-ndp-bill-15-1.7546636); [Smithers Interior News](https://www.interior-news.com/news/blurgate-bc-conservatives-take-issue-with-bill-15-vote-8039510); [Global News](https://globalnews.ca/news/11201383/bc-ndp-fast-track-legislation-passes)
- Note: the Conservatives' 2026 platform now promises its own 120 Day Permitting Act for major projects, similar in aim to the NDP's fast-track laws they voted against in 2025 - [BC Ballot](https://bcballot.ca/platforms/conservative-party/) versus [CBC](https://www.cbc.ca/news/canada/british-columbia/bc-ndp-bill-15-1.7546636)
- Carbon tax: the NDP ended the consumer carbon tax on 2025-04-01 via a bill passed in one day; finance critic Peter Milobar objected to the accelerated process ("ham-fisted"); Rustad pressed to eliminate the industrial carbon tax as well. How the Conservative caucus voted on the repeal bill was not found - [Global News/National Newswatch](https://nationalnewswatch.com/2025/03/31/b-c-legislature-back-in-session-government-tables-bill-to-end-consumer-carbon-tax); [Western Standard](https://www.westernstandard.news/amp/story/watch/watch-rustad-calls-on-bc-ndp-to-axe-industrial-carbon-tax/63616)
- 2025 budget: Conservatives called it a "stand pat, do-nothing" budget and cited a $10.9B deficit. How they voted was not found - [Global News search summary of party release](https://globalnews.ca/news/11030167/bc-carbon-tax-future/amp)
- Bill 44 (2023, density) passed 45-29; BC United, Greens, the then-Conservatives and an independent voted against - [The Narwhal](https://thenarwhal.ca/bc-ndp-bill-44-urban-forests/)
- Women's and girls' sports private member's bill introduced by Rustad in 2024, defeated at first reading - [Wikipedia: John Rustad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Rustad)
- Cowichan Tribes ruling (2025-08-07): BC Supreme Court found Aboriginal title over the Tl'uqtinus village lands in Richmond (about 300 to 324 ha). Rustad said private property and Aboriginal title "cannot coexist", wrote to Eby and the federal attorney general asking for a Supreme Court of Canada reference, and called for DRIPA repeal. BC and others appealed. First Nations leaders called his statements "baseless" fear-stoking. Interim leader Halford later called for DRIPA repeal pending clarity - [CBC video](https://www.cbc.ca/player/play/video/9.6947108); [CTV](https://www.ctvnews.ca/vancouver/politics/article/bc-conservative-rustad-stoking-baseless-land-ruling-fears-first-nations-leaders/); [CHEK](https://cheknews.ca/bc-conservatives-call-for-dripa-to-be-repeal-following-court-ruling-1293364/) (fetch hit a rate limit; relied on search summary); [Global News](https://globalnews.ca/news/11451054/supreme-court-cowichan-bc-conservative-john-rustad-says)
- 2026-02-07 DRIPA town hall in Smithers: Rustad said "First Nations wouldn't exist without Canada", that he voted for DRIPA in 2019 because the BC Liberals gave him "no choice", and that Section 35 "has created two classes of people". Banman was noncommittal on repeal versus modification - [The Tyee](https://thetyee.ca/News/2026/02/10/First-Nations-Would-Not-Exist-Without-Canada-Rustad/)
- Pipelines: BC Conservatives supported the Carney-Smith MOU and opposed the north coast tanker ban - [CTV search summary](https://www.ctvnews.ca/vancouver/article/weve-got-an-important-role-to-play-eby-says-in-response-to-pipeline); [party release](https://conservativebc.ca/david-eby-is-talking-out-of-both-sides-of-his-mouth-on-the-north-coast-tanker-ban-helping-donald-trump/)

### Inferences
- Inference: the party's pattern in opposition is to oppose government bills expanding executive power or Indigenous-law reform, while favouring faster project approvals under its own framework.

### Gaps
- BC Hansard / Votes and Proceedings were not accessed; no confirmed division records for budgets, carbon tax repeal (Bill 8) or motions the Conservatives introduced.
- Appeal status of Cowichan not found.

## 5. Caucus controversies, expulsions, candidate controversies

### Takeaway
2025 saw expulsions and departures over residential-school comments and staffer issues (Brodie, Armstrong, OneBC); 2026 saw the Findlay-era exodus, a private investigator scandal, and a candidate dropped on 2026-10-08.
