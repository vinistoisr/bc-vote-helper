## 1. DRIPA and reconciliation

### Takeaway
DRIPA (2019) obliges BC to work toward making its laws consistent with UNDRIP and enables joint or consent-based decision agreements. A December 2025 Court of Appeal ruling (Gitxaala) found it legally enforceable, which led the NDP to propose changing it, then back off in April 2026 after First Nations opposition. The Conservatives (and OneBC) would repeal it; the Greens would legislate to implement it; the NDP says it will respond to the court decision by amending, details unpublished; CentreBC has no stated position on DRIPA by name.

### Plain-language explainer
BC passed a law in 2019 saying the province will bring its laws in line with the UN Declaration on the Rights of Indigenous Peoples, working with First Nations. It also allows the government to sign deals where a First Nation shares or has the final say on certain decisions (for example, permits for a mine). Courts later said parts of this are enforceable, which made governments and businesses worry about lawsuits; First Nations say the law is a human-rights promise that should not be weakened.

### Expert detail (what the Act requires)
- s.2 purposes: affirm the Declaration applies to BC laws, support implementation, build relationships with Indigenous governing bodies. s.3: government must, with Indigenous peoples, "take all measures necessary to ensure the laws of British Columbia are consistent with the Declaration." s.4: develop and carry out an action plan with Indigenous peoples. s.5: annual report (12 months to March 31) tabled by June 30. s.6: ministers may enter agreements with Indigenous governing bodies. s.7: cabinet may authorize agreements for joint exercise of a statutory power of decision, or "the consent of the Indigenous governing body before the exercise of a statutory power of decision"; agreements are published in the Gazette - [BC Laws (fetched)](https://www.bclaws.gov.bc.ca/civix/document/id/complete/statreg/19044)
- The Act also states it does not diminish s.35 rights; passed 2019 unanimously (87 MLAs in favour per The Narwhal) - [BC Ballot explainer (fetched)](https://bcballot.ca/platforms/issues/dripa-and-indigenous-relations/); [The Narwhal (fetched)](https://thenarwhal.ca/bc-declaration-act-rushed-amendments/)
- s.7 examples: Tahltan agreement 2022 supporting Eskay Creek mine; Red Chris and Galore Creek advanced under s.7 agreements - [The Narwhal explainer (fetched)](https://thenarwhal.ca/bc-declaration-act-explainer/)

### Cited Findings: key court and agreement facts
- Gitxaala v BC (BC Court of Appeal, 2025-12-05, 2-1, 2025 BCCA 430): majority held the mineral claim-staking ("free entry") regime inconsistent with UNDRIP art. 32(2); held DRIPA incorporates UNDRIP into BC law as an interpretive aid, imposes a duty under ss.2-3 to align laws, and courts can in some circumstances assess whether a BC law is consistent. This reversed the BC Supreme Court (2023) which had held DRIPA not legally enforceable - [Young Anderson (search summary)](https://www.younganderson.ca/publications/bulletins/british-columbia-court-of-appeal-finds-undrip-applicable-to-law-in-bc); [Wikipedia Gitxaala (search summary)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gitxaa%C5%82a_v._British_Columbia); [Global News (fetched)](https://globalnews.ca/news/11568633/bc-conservatives-david-eby-reconvene-legislature-repeal-dripa/amp/)
- Eby called the result "unlimited legal liability"; said more than 20 lawsuits involving the province were launched or amended after the ruling - [The Narwhal explainer (fetched)](https://thenarwhal.ca/bc-declaration-act-explainer/)
- Eby's amendment attempt: March 2026 leaked proposals would narrow the "all measures" duty to priority laws; 2026-04-02 he proposed suspending parts of DRIPA and the Interpretation Act (UN Declaration provisions) for up to three years while BC appeals to the Supreme Court of Canada, framed as a confidence vote; action plan and joint-decision sections unaffected - [The Narwhal (fetched)](https://thenarwhal.ca/bc-declaration-act-rushed-amendments/)
- 2026-04-13 Government House Leader Mike Farnworth said the bill would not come forward and would not be a confidence measure; NDP MLA Joan Phillip had said she would not support suspension - [The Tyee (fetched)](https://thetyee.ca/News/2026/04/14/BC-Backs-Off-DRIPA-Amendments/)
- April 2026: Eby and the First Nations Leadership Council (FNLC) agreed no amending legislation in the spring session and to seek a solution before the fall session; the legislature rose 2026-05-28 - [The Narwhal explainer (fetched)](https://thenarwhal.ca/bc-declaration-act-explainer/); [Daily Hive (search summary)](https://dailyhive.com/vancouver/bc-dripa-premier-david-eby-fnlc-spring-2026-legislation-cancelled)
- Cowichan Tribes v Canada (BC Supreme Court, 2025-08, 2025 BCSC 1490): Justice Young declared Aboriginal title over part of the Tl'uqtinus lands in Richmond, found Crown fee simple grants invalid and said title and fee simple can coexist; first declaration of its kind over private fee simple land in Canada (except Canada's interest in the airport fuel delivery lands). All parties appealed; 2026-06-29 court dismissed Montrose Industries' application to reopen trial; BCCA hearing could be in 2027 - [BLG June 2026 (fetched)](https://www.blg.com/en/insights/2026/06/recent-developments-in-aboriginal-title-litigation); [Blakes (search summary)](https://www.blakes.com/insights/aboriginal-title-claims-and-private-land-recent-case-law-developments)
- Cowichan turned on s.35 of the Constitution and was launched before DRIPA existed - [The Narwhal explainer (fetched)](https://thenarwhal.ca/bc-declaration-act-explainer/). BC and Cowichan have begun negotiations - [Torys / Blakes (search summary)](https://www.blakes.com/insights/aboriginal-title-claims-and-private-land-recent-case-law-developments)
- Conflicting appellate law: New Brunswick Court of Appeal (Wolastoqey, 2025 NBCA 129) held title cannot be declared over private fee simple land; Supreme Court of Canada denied leave 2026-05-28 without reasons - [BLG (fetched)](https://www.blg.com/en/insights/2026/06/recent-developments-in-aboriginal-title-litigation)
- Nuchatlaht (2026 BCCA 137, 2026-04-02): BCCA declared Aboriginal title over about 201 sq km of Crown land on Nootka Island; BC filed for leave to the SCC on 2026-06-01 - [BLG (fetched)](https://www.blg.com/en/insights/2026/06/recent-developments-in-aboriginal-title-litigation)
- Haida Nation: 2024-04-14 provincial "Rising Tide" Haida Title Lands Agreement recognizing Haida Aboriginal title on Haida Gwaii, with a process to reconcile laws; it does not affect private property or local governments on Haida Gwaii. Separate federal agreement signed later - [Fasken (search summary)](https://www.fasken.com/en/knowledge/2024/04/province-of-bc-and-the-council-of-the-haida-nation-enter-into-landmark-agreement)
- Gitxaala-related note: Eskay Creek permits approved January 2026 after Tahltan members voted in favour in December 2025 - [The Narwhal explainer (fetched)](https://thenarwhal.ca/bc-declaration-act-explainer/)
- Initiative petition to repeal DRIPA (sponsored by Conservative MLA Harman Bhangu) approved by Elections BC; signing period 2026-10-26 to 2027-01-25; needs 10% of registered voters in all 93 ridings, then the Legislature must pass the draft bill or put it to referendum - [Resource Works (fetched)](https://resourceworks.com/dripa-reconciliation-promises-to-be-a-top-election-issue/); [Daily Hive (search summary)](https://dailyhive.com/vancouver/elections-bc-dripa-repeal-petition-approved). OneBC runs its own DRIPA referendum petition page - [1bc.ca](https://1bc.ca/petitions/dripa)
- Brodie's private member's bill to repeal DRIPA was defeated 44 to 47 at first reading on 2026-04-22 - [search summary of CentreBC/OneBC coverage, unverified against Hansard](https://1bc.ca/petitions/dripa)

## 2. SOGI 123, gender identity in schools

### Takeaway
SOGI 123 is an optional teaching resource for educators (not a curriculum) created by the ARC Foundation with the Ministry of Education starting in 2016. The province cut ARC's operating funding to zero in the 2026-27 budget; resources remain online. Conservatives and OneBC would repeal it; the Greens support it; the NDP defends anti-bullying and anti-discrimination protections but ended the ARC relationship; CentreBC opposes repealing anti-discrimination protections.

### Plain-language explainer
SOGI stands for sexual orientation and gender identity. SOGI 123 is a set of tools and lesson ideas teachers can choose to use to make schools safer for kids of all identities. Each school district decides which learning resources to use.

### Cited Findings
- SOGI 123 is an inclusive education initiative by the ARC Foundation; "not itself a curriculum"; resources are created with educators and described as age appropriate and curriculum aligned. ARC is winding down and exploring transferring SOGI 123 - [SOGI Education site (fetched)](https://www.sogieducation.org/)
- History: 2016 BC Human Rights Code amendment added gender identity or expression as protected grounds (July 2016); ARC Foundation and BC Ministry of Education created the resource in 2016 under the then BC Liberal government, implemented October 2016; John Rustad was a BC Liberal cabinet minister at the time - [CBC (search summary)](https://www.cbc.ca/1.7333988); [CBC via Yahoo (fetched)](https://ca.news.yahoo.com/where-b-c-provincial-political-130000614.html). Sources differ on the exact attribution (one says the resource was "introduced by the BC Liberals with NDP support"); I could not fetch a primary Ministry document.
- Controversy: 2017 petition and rallies against it (e.g. Langley meeting by Kari Simpson); critics say it is not evidence-based and lacks parental involvement; supporters cite reduced discrimination and suicidality; educators say conflict is based on misinformation - [Langley Advance Times (search summary)](https://www.langleyadvancetimes.com/home/top-five-in-2017-sogi-ignited-a-battle-between-parents-2502055); [UBC News 2018 (search summary)](https://news.ubc.ca/2018/09/sex-education-and-gender-identity-in-schools-rife-with-controversy). 2023 protests: Rustad said thousands had protested SOGI 123 and called it an assault on parental rights - [The Tyee 2023-10-04 (search summary)](https://thetyee.ca/News/2023/10/04/Rustad-Anti-SOGI-Stance-Blasted-Legislature/). I did not find a source naming or sizing the September 2023 "1 Million March" events.
- Funding: Ministry says ARC received $350,000 in each of 2023/24 and 2024/25 and $200,000 in 2025/26, and reduced to zero in the February 2026 budget (2026/27) as "responsible expenditure management"; ministry says SOGI 123 was not defunded and resources remain online; districts choose resources. Conservatives claim they "forced" a defunding (partisan release) - [Castanet / ground.news summary (search summary)](https://www.castanetkamloops.net/news/Kamloops/627143/SD73-schools-wont-feel-change-as-province-ends-funding-for-ARC-Foundation-superintendent); [Conservative caucus release](https://www.bcconservativecaucus.ca/bc-conservatives-force-ndp-to-defund-sogi/)

## 3. Drug policy

### Takeaway
Toxic-drug deaths peaked in 2023 (2,511 at first report) and have fallen sharply since (1,826 preliminary for 2025; about 109-138 per month in spring 2026). BC ended its decriminalization pilot on 2026-01-31 and has moved prescribed safer supply to witnessed dosing while expanding involuntary care. The Conservatives would end "safe supply" and expand mandatory care; the Greens would expand regulated pharmaceutical alternatives and supervised consumption; the NDP's platform on drugs is incomplete (reported involuntary care in Kelowna).

### Plain-language explainer
Since 2016 BC has been in a public health emergency because illegal street drugs, mostly containing fentanyl, kill people. Governments have tried different tools: sites where people use drugs under supervision, medications prescribed as alternatives to street drugs ("safer supply"), not charging people for small amounts ("decriminalization"), and treatment/recovery beds (including care people are required to accept).

### Overdose deaths by year (BC Coroners Service, first-reported figures; later revised upward as investigations conclude)
- 2016: 914 (public health emergency declared April 2016) - [UBCM chronology (fetched)](https://www.ubcm.ca/node/106160); [Global News 2018 (search summary)](https://globalnews.ca/news/3979853/b-c-saw-1422-overdose-deaths-in-2017/)
- 2017: 1,422 initial - [Global News (search summary)](https://globalnews.ca/news/3979853/b-c-saw-1422-overdose-deaths-in-2017/)
- 2018: about 1,543 (one report; another reference to a 1,549 figure from memory is unverified) - [Global News (search summary)](https://globalnews.ca/news/6589323/bc-2019-overdose-numbers)

## 4. Public safety

### Takeaway
BC's overall Crime Severity Index has fallen two years in a row (92.4 in 2025 per reporting) while the violent crime index and youth crime moved up slightly in 2025 per a think tank. Surrey extortion peaked in 2025 (133 reported, 49 shootings) and is down in 2026 (142 reported but 21 shootings by 2026-09-28). Surrey's police-of-jurisdiction changeover to Surrey Police Service is still in wind-down. Bail and sentencing law are federal; provinces fund police, prosecutors and courts.

### Plain-language explainer
Police, prosecutors and courts are mostly run by the province, but the Criminal Code (including bail rules) is written by Ottawa. So BC parties can promise officers, prosecutors and programs, and can lobby Ottawa on bail, but cannot change the bail law themselves. (General background; the federal-provincial split is standard constitutional knowledge and not separately sourced here.)
