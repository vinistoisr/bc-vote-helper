# Surrey-Fleetwood provincial riding, 2026 BC general election (voting day 2026-10-24)

Research date: 2026-10-09. All dollar figures CAD. Sources are listed inline. Items I could not verify are in the Gaps lists. Search-tool summaries sometimes conflicted with the primary documents; where that happened I fetched the primary document and say so below.

## 1. Does the riding exist under that name for 2026, and what are its boundaries (2023 redistribution)?

### Takeaway
Yes. Surrey-Fleetwood is one of 10 Surrey ridings (93 province-wide) under the 2023 Electoral Boundaries Commission report, used for the first time in 2024. The riding was kept and adjusted only slightly; the old Surrey-Green Timbers riding was dropped from the Surrey set.

### Cited Findings
- The BC Electoral Boundaries Commission final report (April 3, 2023) lists Surrey-Fleetwood among the proposed 10 Surrey districts (it replaced 9 current ones, which included Surrey-Green Timbers and Surrey-Whalley; new ones include Surrey City Centre, Surrey North, Surrey-Serpentine River) - [BCEBC Final Report 2023, p.151](https://votebymail.elections.bc.ca/docs/rpt/BCEBC-Final-Report-April-3-2023.pdf)
- Proposed Surrey-Fleetwood: 60,668 people (2021 census), 12.8% above the provincial quota, 15 sq km. The previous version had 59,428 people and 17 sq km - [BCEBC Final Report 2023, Tables 16 and comparison pages](https://votebymail.elections.bc.ca/docs/rpt/BCEBC-Final-Report-April-3-2023.pdf)
- The Commission recommended changes to the boundaries of Surrey-Newton and Surrey-Fleetwood to reduce population disparities with neighbours. It considered adding the Fleetwood Park area to Surrey-Fleetwood but declined because it would have significantly increased the population. It also moved the boundary between "Surrey Central" (now Surrey-Serpentine River) and Surrey-Fleetwood to follow Mahood Creek - [BCEBC Final Report 2023, pp.154 and 222](https://votebymail.elections.bc.ca/docs/rpt/BCEBC-Final-Report-April-3-2023.pdf)
- Fraser Highway runs through the middle of the riding (riding profile summary; the Commission map shows 160 St and Fraser Hwy, Fleetwood Park, Johnson Heights, Tynehead Regional Park inside or adjacent) - [BCEBC Final Report 2023, p.159](https://votebymail.elections.bc.ca/docs/rpt/BCEBC-Final-Report-April-3-2023.pdf)
- The riding was first contested in 2009 (Electoral Districts Act, 2008) - [Wikipedia, Surrey-Fleetwood](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Surrey-Fleetwood)
- The BC Legislature passed the Electoral Districts Act to implement the Commission's report (Bill 25) - [Legislature Bill 25](https://lims.leg.bc.ca/pdms/ldp/42nd4th/1st_read/PDF/gov25-1.pdf)

### Inferences
- Because the changes were minor, 2020 and earlier results are broadly comparable to 2024 and 2026, though not exact. (Inference.)

### Gaps
- I could not extract an exact street-level boundary description in text form; the Commission report has maps only. Elections BC's riding page (not fetched) would be the place to confirm.

## 4. Demographics (2021 census)

### Takeaway
Riding population is about 60,668 (2021 census, 15 sq km). Detailed riding-level census data was not found. City of Surrey's Fleetwood neighbourhood profile (a different, larger geography, 65,565 people) gives a proxy: about half immigrants, high home ownership, average household income $124,000, South Asian as the largest group.

### Cited Findings
- Riding population 60,668, 15 sq km (2021 census count used by the Commission) - [BCEBC Final Report 2023](https://votebymail.elections.bc.ca/docs/rpt/BCEBC-Final-Report-April-3-2023.pdf)
- City of Surrey Fleetwood community profile (2021 census; neighbourhood, not the electoral district): population 65,565 (12% of Surrey); average household income $124,000 vs $116,600 city; renters 23% vs 30%; owners 77%; immigrants 50% vs 45%; unemployment 8.5%; take transit to work 11% vs 13%; average age 40 vs 39; population growth 5% from 2016-2021 vs 10% city; 2021 average dwelling value $1,109,000; 29% of renters and 21% of owners spend 30%+ of income on housing - [City of Surrey, Fleetwood profile](https://www.surrey.ca/sites/default/files/media/documents/Neighbourhood-Profile-Fleetwood.pdf)
- Language most commonly spoken at home in the Fleetwood neighbourhood: English 58%, Punjabi 19%, Mandarin 5%, Tagalog 3%, Korean 2% - same source
- Dwelling mix in the neighbourhood: 52% row/townhouses, 36% single-family, 11% low-rise apartments, 1% high-rise - same source
- Top origin of recent immigrants (2016-2021): India 46%, China 11%, Philippines 10% - same source
- South Asian share: the City profile chart shows South Asian at 38% for Surrey (matches 37.81% from Wikipedia citywide). The Fleetwood panel is garbled in the PDF text (the chart shows South Asian 38% and Caucasian 25% while the text says 38% Caucasian), so I could not state the Fleetwood neighbourhood share with confidence - same source; [Wikipedia, Surrey](https://Www.Wikipedia.org/wiki/Surrey,_BC)
- The federal riding Fleetwood-Port Kells (a different geography and boundary) was 34.56% South Asian in 2021 - [Wikipedia, Fleetwood-Port Kells](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fleetwood%E2%80%94Port_Kells)

### Inferences
- A riding that is roughly 35-40% South Asian (inferred from adjacent geographies) with high owner-occupancy and townhouse housing makes extortion, policing, housing costs and transit likely top local issues. (Inference.)

## 5. Local issues and party commitments to Surrey

### Takeaway
SkyTrain, policing/extortion, health care, and cost of living are the confirmed local topics with primary-source material. For Surrey-specific platform commitments I found campaign announcements delivered in Surrey but no party platform document with a Surrey chapter; full Conservative, Green and NDP platforms had not all been released in the sources I found.

### Cited Findings

A. Surrey-Langley SkyTrain (Fleetwood stations):
- 16 km elevated extension of the Expo Line from King George to 203 St in Langley City mostly along Fraser Hwy; eight stations, including Green Timbers (140 St), 152 Street, Fleetwood (160 St and Fraser Hwy, in the Fleetwood Town Centre), and Bakerview-166 Street (east Fleetwood). Three of these (152 St, Fleetwood, Bakerview-166) fall in or border Surrey-Fleetwood - [SLS Monthly Status Report, April 2026](https://surreylangleyskytrain.gov.bc.ca/app/uploads/sites/938/2026/06/SLS_Monthly-Status-Report_April_2026.pdf), [project site](https://surreylangleyskytrain.gov.bc.ca/)
- Budget $5.996 billion; major construction started Nov 2024; anticipated in-service date late 2029; spending to date $2.508 billion (April 2026 report), federal recoveries $577.0 million; guideway segment erection started at Fleetwood and Clayton station sites; delivered by TI Corp - [SLS Monthly Status Report, April 2026](https://surreylangleyskytrain.gov.bc.ca/app/uploads/sites/938/2026/06/SLS_Monthly-Status-Report_April_2026.pdf)
- The cost was previously about $4 billion; the estimate rose to about $6 billion in Aug 2024 and the opening moved from 2028 to 2029 - [CTV, Aug 15, 2024](https://www.ctvnews.ca/vancouver/article/cost-of-surrey-langley-skytrain-extension-balloons-to-6b/); [Global News](https://globalnews.ca/news/10701039/surrey-langley-skytrain-cost/amp)
- Party commitments for 2026 on SkyTrain: none found. In 2020 the NDP committed to complete the full Surrey-Langley extension - [Global News 2020](https://globalnews.ca/news/7402779/surrey-infrastructure-policing-bc-election)

B. Surrey Police Service (SPS) transition:
- SPS became the police of jurisdiction on Nov 29, 2024; in July 2025 Mayor Brenda Locke said the full transition would take a year longer than the planned 2026 completion, to 2027; SPS then covered two of five districts and lacked officers to patrol the whole city; Locke was elected in 2022 pledging to keep the RCMP, and a city legal challenge failed; the province funded the transition - [Global News, July 18, 2025](https://globalnews.ca/news/11294476/surrey-poilce-transition)
- Conservative position on the SPS transition in 2026: I found no source stating it. No NDP 2026 statement found either; the NDP government drove the transition from 2021 onward (inferred from the above reporting).

C. Extortion and shootings:
- Provincial extortion task force formed Sept 2025, RCMP-led, about 40 members; Surrey reported 98 extortions as of May 11, 2026, 16 involving gunfire (monthly threats: 44 Jan, 17 Feb, 14 Mar, 20 Apr); Public Safety Minister Nina Krieger called it the top public safety priority; CBSA has used immigration enforcement; Surrey police are covertly installing cameras and deploying round the clock - [Narcity/Canadian Press, May 12, 2026](https://www.narcity.com/combating-extortion-top-priority-b-c-minister)
- Early 2026 (Jan 15 report): 16 extortion cases, 12 victims and 3 shots-fired calls in Surrey since Jan 1; Eby said he was unhappy with the lack of task force updates and that "we need better results, we need to see more arrests" - [Global News, Jan 15, 2026](https://globalnews.ca/news/11615290/extortion-cases-surrey-2026)
- Conservative position (2025): called for a provincial extortion tip line and closer work with Ottawa; accused the NDP of inaction; government cited $100 million+ annually for specialized enforcement - [Conservative Party of BC statement](https://conservativebc.ca/extortion_shooting_in_surrey/)
- In 2026 the Conservative Surrey slate release also criticizes the NDP on extortion and violence against small businesses - [Conservative Party of BC release, Sept 29](https://conservativebc.ca/conservative-party-of-bc-announces-strong-surrey-candidates-for-serious-times/)

D. Health care (Cloverdale hospital, Surrey Memorial):
- New hospital in Cloverdale: reported cost about $2.88 billion (up from about $1.72 billion), 168 beds, 55 ER treatment spaces, BC Cancer Centre; completion about 2029, opening about 2030; structural construction reached halfway when the fifth floor was poured - search summary of [CTV](https://www.ctvnews.ca/vancouver/article/new-surrey-hospital-reaches-construction-milestone/); the CTV (Sept 12, 2023) page reports the $1 billion overrun and opening delay to 2030 - [CTV, Sept 12, 2023](https://guelph.ctvnews.ca/vancouver/article/new-surrey-hospital-will-cost-1b-more-than-planned-wont-open-until-2030). I did not verify the $2.88 billion figure on the primary page.
- Eby campaign announcement in Surrey on Oct 9, 2026: 20 new primary care clinics, fast-track international medical graduates already in BC ("bridging to practice"), 1,000 more training seats for nurses, nurse practitioners, allied health and care aides; event at SFU Surrey - [Surrey Now-Leader, Oct 9](https://surreynowleader.com/2026/10/09/b-c-election-battleground-in-surrey-eby-promises-more-doctors-to-fast-track-intl-grads/), [Global News, Oct 9](https://globalnews.ca/news/12097726/bc-leaders-campaign-trail-health-care-supporting-seniors-october-9)
