## DRIPA position

### Takeaway
CentreBC has no published position of "keep", "amend" or "repeal". Its only formal statement (Aug 13, 2026, then-leader Mike Bernier) rejects repeal as political messaging, calls DRIPA "largely symbolic", and proposes a single provincial framework for treaties and reconciliation agreements instead. Treat as: not repeal, no commitment to amend, and a treaty-framework alternative. Sturko herself has not been found on record on DRIPA.

### Cited Findings
- Statement "Reconciliation, Certainty, and Prosperity", by Mike Bernier as CentreBC leader, Aug 13, 2026 - [CentreBC](https://www.centrebc.ca/news/statement-reconciliation-certainty-and-prosperity/). My reading of the page (WebFetch summary, not verbatim except where quoted):
  - Bernier says he was the only MLA who did not vote for the 2019 DRIPA legislation; his concern was the design, not Indigenous rights. He called the Act "largely symbolic" and said it created expectations without clarity.
  - He says promises to repeal DRIPA are "more about political messaging than practical solutions"; later changes such as Interpretation Act amendments would not be undone by repeal.
  - He proposes "a single provincial framework for treaties and reconciliation agreements which would coordinate negotiating mandates" (also funding, standards, timelines), and says "Modern treaties have already demonstrated that negotiated agreements can create stability."
  - Further actions: involve First Nations, local governments, workers and others earlier; transparent timelines and decision criteria for major projects; sector agreements in forestry, mining, energy, infrastructure and housing; measure outcomes rather than process.
  - The statement does not mention UNDRIP and does not name the Cowichan decision.
- BC Ballot lists four Indigenous-relations commitments, all from this Aug 13 statement - [BC Ballot](https://bcballot.ca/platforms/centrebc/)
- Bernier's July 31, 2026 opinion piece criticises both the NDP government's handling of land title and the BC Conservatives' opposition to Indigenous rights, with no specific proposal - [CentreBC, "BC Deserves Better Than Chaos or Extremism"](https://www.centrebc.ca/news/bc-deserves-better-than-chaos-or-extremism/)
- The party's vision page says CentreBC "supports Indigenous economic and cultural development" and seeks cooperation among Indigenous communities, public land users and private property owners - [Our Vision](https://www.centrebc.ca/vision/)
- Pipeline opinion (Kirkpatrick, July 7, 2026): "ownership should not be treated as a stand-in for consent"; wants consultation turned into a transparent participation framework - [CentreBC](https://www.centrebc.ca/news/ottawa-and-bc-just-moved-toward-centrebc-pipeline-conditions/)
- Other parties' stated DRIPA stance for comparison: Conservatives repeal, NDP not justiciable with sections suspended - [Castanet column](https://www.castanetkamloops.net/news/Writers-Bloc/607493/Shaw-Ebys-DRIPA-reversal-leaves-voters-with-a-policy-few-can-understand) (columnist summary; other researchers hold the primary sources)

### Inferences
- Bernier's Aug 13 statement was issued under a prior leader (Bernier resigned Sept 18). Sturko may adopt it, but no source shows she has. Label CentreBC's DRIPA position as "no repeal; treaty-framework alternative; leader-level confirmation pending".
- Bernier's personal 2019 "no" vote is a Bernier record, not a CentreBC party record (CentreBC did not exist in 2019).

### Gaps
- No Sturko statement on DRIPA found. Sturko was not an MLA in 2019 (elected 2022), so she has no DRIPA vote. Whether she voted on 2025 DRIPA-related bills (Bills 14, 15; Interpretation Act amendment) was not checked.
- No position on the Cowichan title ruling found.

## Drug policy

### Takeaway
CentreBC has published no drug policy. Sturko's pre-2026 record as an MLA (BC United, then Conservative, then independent) supports compassionate involuntary care, opposes non-medically supervised safer supply, and pushed to end decriminalization. These are her personal record, not CentreBC commitments.

### Cited Findings
- BC Ballot: "No commitment published" for drugs and addiction - [BC Ballot](https://bcballot.ca/platforms/centrebc/)
- Feb 16, 2026 budget release (Kirkpatrick): pair stronger justice with treatment systems "so lawbreakers face consequences and people with addictions get help" (paraphrase from my fetch) - [CentreBC](https://www.centrebc.ca/news/budget-2026-managing-decline-is-not-leadership/)
- May 30, 2026 release says "the toxic drug crisis continues to kill" with no remedy stated - [CentreBC](https://www.centrebc.ca/news/news-release/bc-conservatives-elect-hard-right-leader/)
- Bernier opinion (July 31): "the treatment and justice resources to back it up" for street safety; no drug policy stated - [CentreBC](https://www.centrebc.ca/news/bc-deserves-better-than-chaos-or-extremism/)
- Sturko's record as MLA (shadow minister for Mental Health, Addiction, Recovery and Education from Dec 2022; Solicitor General and Public Safety critic Nov 2024): introduced a private member's Mental Health Act amendment (2023, re-introduced 2025) requiring contact with family or carers before involuntary admission decisions; called for compassionate intervention legislation; said a Conservative government would build at least 100 low-secure beds; criticised the NDP's 18 involuntary-care beds as too few - [search summary of Conservative BC releases](https://conservativebc.ca/?p=642); [Times Colonist](https://www.timescolonist.com/local-news/bc-liberal-mla-opens-up-about-her-recovery-and-why-she-supports-compassionate-involuntary-care-6558013) (403 to fetch; headline and search summary only); [Cloverdale Reporter](https://www.cloverdalereporter.com/news/surrey-south-mla-elenore-sturko-introduces-bill-aimed-at-helping-prevent-suicides/)
- Safer supply and decriminalization: per a CBC search summary, Sturko (then BC United) rejected non-medically supervised safer supply, saying clinicians should stay involved; in early 2024 she wrote to the federal minister arguing the province had not shown the decriminalization pilot worked - [CBC](https://www.cbc.ca/lite/story/1.7017256) (403 to fetch; relies on search summary); [JURIST, Mar 2026](https://www.jurist.org/news/2026/03/canada-dispatch-british-columbias-failed-drug-decriminalization-pilot-leaves-toxic-crisis-unresolved/)
- Her Sept 22, 2025 Conservative-caucus removal and Wikipedia's career summary list no drug-policy votes - [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elenore_Sturko)

### Inferences
- Likely CentreBC direction (inference): treatment-and-recovery oriented, supportive of compassionate involuntary care, sceptical of non-medical safer supply and of decriminalization, but with no stated commitment to supervised consumption sites either way.
- Because the 2026 platform is silent, score CentreBC on drugs as "low confidence".

### Gaps
- Position on supervised consumption sites, decriminalization status now (the pilot ended in 2026 per Jurist), and involuntary care expansion as a party commitment: not published.
- Sturko's Hansard votes on drug-related bills were not retrieved.

## Taxes, deficit, balanced-budget timeline

### Takeaway
Published tax commitment: scrap PST on food production and farm equipment. CentreBC opposes expanding PST to professional services and calls bracket indexation pause "stealth taxes", but has proposed no income-bracket change, wealth tax or speculation-tax position and no balanced-budget date. Vision page says it aims to balance "over the long term".

### Cited Findings
- "We will scrap the PST on food production and farm equipment to lower the cost of B.C.-grown food" - [CentreBC budget release, Feb 16, 2026](https://www.centrebc.ca/news/budget-2026-managing-decline-is-not-leadership/); repeated Feb 19 - [CentreBC](https://www.centrebc.ca/news/budget-2026-a-blueprint-for-bankruptcy/)
- Feb 19, 2026 release on Budget 2026: calls the NDP deficit "record-breaking $13.3 billion"; says the plan offers no route to a balanced budget; criticises raising the lowest personal income-tax bracket by 0.6% (est. $76 for the average taxpayer) and the bracket-indexation pause until 2030 as "Stealth Taxes"; opposes expanding PST to "professional services like accounting and engineering"; warns debt will "nearly double to a staggering $40,935 for every single person in B.C. by 2029" - [CentreBC](https://www.centrebc.ca/news/budget-2026-a-blueprint-for-bankruptcy/)
- The Feb 16 release gives no deficit figure, debt target or costing - [CentreBC](https://www.centrebc.ca/news/budget-2026-managing-decline-is-not-leadership/)
- Vision page: "balance the budget over the long term through smarter investment and spending, while protecting health care, education, and social supports" (paraphrase) - [Our Vision](https://www.centrebc.ca/vision/)
- Sturko called the snap election "quite insulting" given a "$13.8 billion deficit" (her figure; differs from the party's $13.3 billion Feb figure, likely a later forecast, not verified) - [Penticton Western News, Sept 22](https://pentictonwesternnews.com/2026/09/22/surreys-elenore-sturko-is-the-sole-incumbent-mla-for-centrebc-ahead-of-election/)
- New spending announced: $60M additional skilled-trades training (Oct 1); double the Manufacturing Jobs Fund to $60M and raise job-training funding to $60M (Oct 9); funding for long-term care homes and vaccines for those who want them (Oct 6) - [BC Ballot](https://bcballot.ca/platforms/centrebc/), [Surrey Now-Leader](https://surreynowleader.com/2026/10/09/centrebc-leader-promisesmanufacturing-boost). The two $60M items may overlap; Surrey Now-Leader's text reads as manufacturing fund $60M plus job training $60M, while BC Ballot dated the trades item Oct 1. Treat as unresolved.

### Inferences
- Pattern is a PST cut for farm/food inputs paired with "no new taxes" criticism and unspecified savings. Whether CentreBC would raise any tax or cut spending is unknown; no costing exists yet.

### Gaps
- Income-tax brackets, wealth/speculation taxes, corporate tax, a balanced-budget year: none published.
