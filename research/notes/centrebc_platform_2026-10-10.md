## Platform at a glance (from p.4 and p.13)

- Headline: deficit falls every year after the first budget, to about $9.0B in 2030/31 (from $13.3B in Budget 2026 for 2026/27), budget balances in 2034/35. Balanced-budget path is to be put in law (Balanced Budget and Spending Control Act in year one) and checked by a new Independent Budget Officer.
- Platform cost: about $13.7B over four years (2027/28 to 2030/31) including interest, plus $7.8B borrowed capital. "Cautious case": no extra revenue from growth counted.
- Typical two-income family saves about $640 a year by 2030/31 (of which $540 is the first-bracket cut).
- Economy section costing: $4.5B day-to-day spending + $4.5B tax relief, offset by $5.6B savings and $0.8B new revenue; net $3.6B including interest; plus $3.5B more in transportation, rapid bus and port connections (borrowed).
- Section costs (4 yrs, day-to-day): Health about $4.0B (+ $3.2B capital); Education about $2.0B (+ $750M capital); Public safety about $1.8B (+ $255M capital); Environment about $745M (+ $60M campsites + $500M repaid home-upgrade loans); Good government about $188M; AI and work about $141M.
- Scorecard: public platform scorecard within 100 days of taking office, updated quarterly, Auditor General checks yearly.

## 1. Cost of living

**Position**
- Income tax: on Jan 1, 2027, cut the first bracket rate from 5.6% back to 5.06%, "fully reverses the Budget 2026 increase". Two-income family saves about $540/yr. Will NOT extend the Budget 2026 freeze on bracket and credit indexing (frozen through 2030); indexing "will return on schedule" (from 2031). No change to the basic personal amount is proposed (not mentioned).
- Renters: BC renter's tax credit from $400 to $600/yr, indexed to inflation; full credit up to $80,000 household income (up from about $66,000), partial credit up to $100,000.
- Power and insurance: cap BC Hydro residential rate increases at inflation and never more than 3%/yr from 2027 to 2030. ICBC basic rates frozen until spring 2027, then increases held at or below inflation through 2030.
- Child care: 20,000 new $10-a-day spaces by 2030, starting where waitlists are longest, using federal funding "already secured to 2031".
- Cost-of-living check: from Apr 1, 2027 every major new policy, fee or tax gets a published household cost estimate 30 days before adoption.
- Commuter credit: new refundable BC Commuter Credit, 15% back on monthly and annual transit passes and BC Ferries multi-trip fares.
- PST: rebate PST on building materials for new purpose-built rental buildings (4+ homes) started 2027-2032; widen the PST exemption for production machinery, equipment and repair parts (tariff-hit sectors). No general PST cut. Farm-equipment/food-production PST removal was a Feb 2026 budget-response ask (see bcballot) but is NOT in the platform text I read; the platform mentions farm loan guarantees, farm water stream and farm tech grants instead.
- Employer Health Tax: halve it for two years for tariff-hit sectors (forestry, steel, aluminum, manufacturing). Example given: $5M payroll saves about $49,000/yr.
- Price caps on groceries or gas: none. The platform only cites CPI (food +3.3%, gasoline +20.8%) as context.
- Income assistance / minimum wage: no commitment on rates. The new $400/month BC Caregiver Benefit (about 9,000 family caregivers of high-need people with low or modest incomes; $4,800/yr) will not reduce income or disability assistance.
- Other: school food programs from about 20% to 40% of students by 2030; BC Access Grant for sub-2-year programs from $4,000 to $5,000/yr, indexed; tuition increases capped at 2% or less.

**New since Oct 9?** Yes, essentially all of it is first published in the Oct 10 PDF. Earlier items: $10-a-day child care as "economic infrastructure" (Feb 16, 2026 budget reaction, per bcballot); farm PST removal (Feb 16 and 19, 2026, per bcballot, not repeated in platform); $60M/yr trades training (Oct 1 Bluesky, per bcballot).

## 2. Taxes, deficit and debt

**Position**
- Balanced budget in 2034/35 (no year was named in Budget 2026); deficit falls every year after the first budget; about $9.0B in 2030/31. Path legislated (Balanced Budget and Spending Control Act, first year). Existing day-to-day spending grows at least 1.5 points/yr slower than Budget 2026 planned (1.6 points in 2033/34 and 2034/35), front-line protected. Cabinet ministers lose part of their pay if the overall budget target is missed. A recession or declared emergency can pause the rule, only with a public recovery plan.
- Independent Budget Officer reporting to the legislature, costs bills on MLA request.
- Spending cuts and savings (about $5.6B over four years in the economy-section costing):
  - 10,000 fewer public-sector positions over four years by attrition, no layoffs, focused on health authority management, agencies and ministry administration; about $1.1B/yr saved by 2030/31. Front-line roles protected.
  - Zero-based review of every ministry's administration and operating budget in the first budget; first review published within 180 days; each program faces six public tests once a term; about a quarter of ministries reviewed per year.
  - Public-sector pay mandate 2%/yr for new agreements.
  - Cut outside consultant/advisory contracts 20%; government travel and non-health/safety advertising 25%; 20% fewer comms staff; fewer ministries and smaller cabinet; triple billing audits; BC Housing corporate functions merged into ministry; InBC investment fund makes no new commitments.
  - One back office for all health authorities with half the senior executives; one buying system for health authorities, school districts, colleges, universities; phase out private nursing agencies outside remote areas.
  - If savings fall short, these slow first, in order: housing performance and growth funds phase in at half speed; Manufacturing Jobs Fund holds at $30M/yr (conflicts with the Oct 9 "double to $60M" pledge; see topic 9); highway chargers spread over two more years. About $230M/yr by 2030/31. Health care, schools, policing not on the list.
  - Crown efficiency targets for BC Hydro, ICBC, BCLC; specialized audit teams for PST, property transfer tax and speculation tax (revenue collection, about $0.8B new revenue in total attributed to measures).
  - Year-end spending rules (no spending-rush penalty, cap on last-quarter commitments, publish contracts over $25,000 signed in last six weeks).
  - Independent cost review for public projects over $100M; public report if cost rises more than 10%.
- Tax relief is $4.5B over four years (mainly the 5.06% bracket, renter credit, commuter credit, R&D credit to 15% from 10%, EHT halving, PST rebates).
- No new broad tax is proposed. No wealth tax, no corporate rate change.

**New since Oct 9?** Yes (platform). Earlier: critiques of Budget 2026 as "Managing Decline" (Feb 16) and "A Blueprint for Bankruptcy" (Feb 19), per voche.me. The 2034/35 date was first reported by Castanet (Oct 9-10).

## 3. Housing

**Position**
- Approval timelines: by Jan 1, 2028, provincial standard of 6 months for rezonings and 8 weeks for building permits, one set of rules across municipalities; fees refunded if a deadline is missed.
- $125M/yr housing performance fund for cities meeting approval timelines and housing targets.
- Development cost charges (DCCs): cut in half on new rental and below-market homes everywhere in BC, building on the 2026 Canada-BC agreement; province adds $240M/yr to a growth fund cities apply to. Savings up to $40,000 a home in high-cost communities (per the Canada-BC agreement's estimates).
- Supply: 5,000 pre-fabricated and BC-made mass-timber homes a year by 2030 (bulk materials purchasing); double BC Builds to 20,000 below-market rent/sell homes by 2031; PST rebate on materials for new purpose-built rentals of 4+ homes (2027-2032).
- Non-market / supportive: 1,000 new supportive homes with on-site health care and conduct rules, 1,500 rent supplements, 400 complex-care spaces by 2030/31; about 300 "Intensive Housing" homes by 2030/31 (province provides land and care, asks Ottawa to pay to build). Rent supplement for youth leaving care extended from two years to age 27.
- Public land: buy land near new transit stations before announcement and share in value created; sell or lease surplus public land, "much of it for housing".
- Renters: credit to $600 (see topic 1). No rent control or vacancy control proposed.
- Bill 44 / Bill 47 (small-scale multi-unit housing, transit-oriented areas): NOT mentioned by name. One line says the transit plan "builds on provincial rules that allow more homes near transit", i.e. leaves the transit-oriented-area rules in place. No commitment on repeal, amendment or on small-scale multi-unit zoning. Treat as "no explicit position; no repeal".
- Speculation/foreign buyer taxes: no new or repealed tax. Only audit teams for the speculation tax and property transfer tax. No foreign buyer tax commitment.
- Context cited: asking rents in Vancouver down about 10% since early 2024 (CMHC), "where new supply has arrived, it works".

**New since Oct 9?** Yes (platform). Earlier (Feb 16, 2026, via bcballot): "Housing policy must be linked to actual affordability outcomes rather than just counting units."

**Sources**: Platform PDF pp 7, 12-13, 27

## 4. Health care

**Position**
- Family doctor or nurse practitioner team for everyone who wants one by end of 2031; from 2029 no one waits more than 12 months; automatic registration; stable funding for team-based clinics; 600 more nurse practitioner positions in the public system by 2030; assessment program for internationally trained family doctors from 96 to 200/yr by 2028; keep the Longitudinal Family Physician payment model and pay more for complex patients; licensing within 30 days for doctors and nurses from other provinces, US, UK, Australia; 100 more residency positions by 2030 (60 family medicine); physician assistants fully licensed 2027; Canadian-hosted AI scribes for willing doctors/NPs from 2027; one provincial e-referral standard by 2028.
- Surgeries and scans: 15,000 more scheduled surgeries a year by 2030/31; five more MRI machines in public hospitals with extended hours; 20,000 publicly funded scans a year contracted at existing clinics for two years (wait-time conditions, audits, no patient pay); public health dashboard by spring 2028; health authority CEO pay tied to wait-time targets.
- ER: ER Accountability Act with public reporting of every closure; halve unplanned ER closure hours by 2029/30; make rural retention bonus permanent and extend to every community with repeated closures; $25M/yr rural ER stabilization fund; trained protection officers in every ED and rural hospitals; reduce patients stuck in hospital awaiting long-term care from about 1,600 to 1,000 by 2030.
- Private delivery: "Public health care, not private pay." No private-pay options that pull doctors from public system, no Alberta-style dual practice; NPs fully in public system; "the private-pay route to faster public services will be closed". Phases out private nursing agencies outside remote areas. Does use contracted private clinics for publicly funded scans (temporary, 2 yrs). Fix (not repeal) the Health Professions and Occupations Act in 2027: half of college board members elected by profession, fewer government appointees on discipline committees, no publishing of minor/dismissed complaints, appeal to a neutral body.
- Seniors / long-term care: 1.5 million more home support hours/yr by 2029/30; halve home support fees for low-income seniors; $400/month BC Caregiver Benefit for about 9,000 family caregivers; pre-fabricated long-term care, starting with about 500 beds in Abbotsford open by 2029/30 and restarting the other six paused projects; by March 2031, 2,500 new beds under construction and 1,000 open; quality and staffing published per home.
- Surrey: build the new acute-care tower at Surrey Memorial announced in 2024 (published budget and timeline; new operating rooms, surgical spaces South of the Fraser, cardiac intensive care unit; construction by 2030, open about 2035); complete the business case, site and design for a children's hospital for Surrey and Langley, construction starting 2031.
- Mental health and addiction: see topic 7. Free counselling program (vouchers or public counsellors; 100,000 people/yr by 2030/31; goal up to 12 free sessions/yr); 12 adult crisis centres by 2030 next to busiest EDs.
- Cost: about $4.0B day-to-day plus $3.2B capital over four years; health admin savings about $400M/yr by 2030/31.

**New since Oct 9?** Yes (platform). Earlier: "Build long-term care homes for seniors and fund vaccines for those who want them" (Oct 6 Bluesky); expand home care and cut doctor paperwork (Feb 16 budget response); Aug 5 criticism of NDP HIV care cuts at Spectrum Health.

**Sources**: Platform PDF pp 14-19, 12; https://bcballot.ca/platforms/centrebc/

## 5. DRIPA / reconciliation

**Position (a reversal of the Aug 13 stance)**
- Platform: "In our first session, one bill will repeal DRIPA ... and section 8.1 of the Interpretation Act, and replace them with a new law built on negotiated treaties." The new law puts $30M/yr into faster treaty talks and settling overlapping claims, restores treaty advisory committees, protects private land and homeowners, requires a yearly treaty progress report. "The elected legislature, not the courts, will decide how BC laws reflect the Declaration"; the UN Declaration is used "as a guide". Constitutionally protected rights and the duty to consult unchanged.
- Cowichan: "No family will lose their home, or its value, because of a court ruling on Aboriginal title." Law guaranteeing the Province compensates homeowners for loss in value up to assessed value, and keeps them able to get a mortgage or sell; will ask Ottawa to share cost. Will NOT finalize agreements that affect private land until the Cowichan appeals are settled (talks continue; revenue sharing and non-private-land agreements proceed). The platform notes the August 2025 Cowichan ruling found title over about 300 hectares in Richmond, about 145 private properties affected, and that all parties have appealed.
- Private land rule: no private land transferred without willing owner in any agreement (title agreements and court settlements included); hospitals, schools, university campuses, provincial parks not transferred; surplus land first offered for public needs; guaranteed public access where public-use land goes into a treaty.
- Process: announce when talks begin; publish draft agreements at least 60 days before signing; treaties still need an act of the legislature; other major agreements (title, land) tabled in legislature for debate before cabinet signs.
- Economy: expand the First Nations Equity Financing Program to $3B in loan guarantees within six months, with revenue sharing, covering forest tenures and manufacturing as well as major projects; $25M/yr top-up to end long-term drinking water advisories in First Nations by 2029; $20M/yr fund for First Nations preparing for or running their own child and family services; clean power calls require First Nations partners. Fed-fast-tracked projects get one review under renewed Canada-BC agreement "with First Nations consent processes built in".
- "Consent": no explicit adoption of free, prior and informed consent as a veto; platform states the legislature decides how laws reflect UNDRIP.

**New since Oct 9?** Yes. Important difference from earlier statement: Aug 13, 2026 (Mike Bernier, then leader) said repeal of DRIPA "is political messaging rather than practical policy" and repeal "would not reverse" the Interpretation Act changes; it proposed a single provincial treaty framework, earlier involvement, project certainty, sector agreements, results-based measurement. The Oct 10 platform now commits to repeal DRIPA plus s.8.1 of the Interpretation Act, while keeping the treaty-framework idea. Score from the platform, not the August statement.

**Sources**: Platform PDF pp 8-9, 32, 35-36; https://www.centrebc.ca/news/statement-reconciliation-certainty-and-prosperity/ (Aug 13, 2026)

## 7. Drugs

**Position**
- Decriminalization: the platform notes the pilot "ended on January 31, 2026" and public drug use has been illegal again since May 2024; it does not propose bringing the pilot back. Public possession and possession by repeat offenders: within 100 days formally ask Ottawa to change the federal prosecution directive so these are charged; use alternative measures/restorative justice to steer people into care. Supports an appeal of the Sept 2026 BC court ruling in the Drug User Liberation Front (DULF) case and will seek to intervene; opposes drug-buying clubs.
- Safer supply: not mentioned. Supervised consumption/overdose prevention sites: not mentioned.
- Treatment on demand: 150 more detox beds and 500 more treatment/recovery beds by 2030 (650 total), all publicly funded beds free to the user; same-day detox for urgent cases by 2028; treatment within 14 days by 2030, linked to recovery housing; wait times published quarterly; up to 5,000 people/yr able to detox at home or local clinic by 2029; voluntary treatment in every provincial correctional centre by 2029; two voluntary recovery units in BC Corrections by 2028/29 (one men, one women).
- Involuntary care: "Voluntary care always comes first." But ensure the Mental Health Act assesses each patient's capacity individually (as other provinces do), after the July 2026 BC Supreme Court finding that "deemed consent" breaches the Charter, while keeping secure care for those who need it. Expand Red Fish Healing Centre by about 50 beds (first new beds 2029); expand Forensic Psychiatric Hospital at Colony Farm by about 80 beds; 400 complex-care homes; 12 crisis centres by 2030.
- Safe Downtowns Fund: safety and outreach teams in 20 BC downtowns by 2028; province will "clearly support municipal rules on public drug use and street disorder, with a police option to steer people into treatment".
- Public safety dashboard from Apr 1, 2027 including treatment wait times and overdose deaths.

**New since Oct 9?** Yes (platform). Earlier: bcballot listed "no commitment published" on drugs; that is now out of date.

**Sources**: Platform PDF pp 18-19, 25-27

## 8. Public safety

**Position**
- Police: 500 more provincial RCMP officers (100 a year for five years); keep vacancies low; expand BC-based police training. (No new municipal police funding announced; no position on the Surrey police transition.)
- Prosecutors: 60 more Crown prosecutors with support staff by 2030/31.
- Bail: independent review of how bail works in BC, quarterly public data on detention requests, releases and reoffending; review to report before Parliament's 2028 review of federal bail reforms. No promise to change bail law.
- Repeat offenders: double SITE and CSTEP (targeted enforcement of repeat and violent offenders) to about $26M/yr with dedicated prosecutors.
- Extortion and gangs: make the extortion task force permanent with its own budget "so the capacity built in 2025-26 isn't lost"; 40-officer joint port enforcement team (with CBSA, RCMP, BC's organized crime unit) by 2028; press Ottawa for a federal port police paid by a container levy.
- Disorder/public spaces: see topic 7; Safe Downtowns Fund in 20 downtowns.
- Emergency readiness (extra): FireSmart grants back to $40M/yr, guaranteed in law for five years; Forests Against Fire to $25M/yr; $10M/yr wildfire tech fund; Flood and Heat Resilience Fund $75M/yr; 40,000 free cooling units by 2030; disaster aid decisions within 60 days, advances up to $50,000 within 14 days; Community Resilience Fund $15M/yr; $6M/yr mental health care for first responders.
- Quarterly public safety dashboard from Apr 1, 2027.
- Cost: about $1.8B day-to-day plus $255M capital.

**New since Oct 9?** Yes (platform). The platform text I read does NOT repeat the cell, portal, micro-grants or 48-hour standard; it only commits to a permanent task force and 60 prosecutors. ## 9. Economy, tariffs, resources

**Position**
- Major projects: "one project, one review": single coordinated provincial review, 12-month deadline for provincial decisions; publish next list of priority projects; within 90 days of taking office begin talks to renew the Canada-BC "one project, one review" agreement, agreement within the first year.
- LNG: back LNG projects with First Nations partners and a credible plan to be net-zero ready (as BC requires). Press for Canadian steel in LNG Canada Phase 2.
- Pipelines: platform says nothing about new pipelines. It asks to keep the northern tanker ban in writing from Ottawa. Earlier (Oct 23, 2025) CentreBC set "five hard conditions" for any northern pipeline: Indigenous participation framework, dedicated BC Energy Future Fund, real safety and climate guardrails, guaranteed local jobs and procurement, clear legal pathway with full transparency. Jul 7, 2026 statement credits the Ottawa-BC agreement for keeping the North Coast tanker ban and routing capacity to Roberts Bank. Per platform: "no federal deal affecting BC's coast or land without BC at the table".
- Mining: permitting decisions on 10 new mines by 2030; target of 3 processing facilities by 2032.
- Power: expand BC Hydro by at least 2,000 MW by 2033; annual competitive clean power call of at least 3,000 GWh with First Nations partners.
- Forestry: work toward 45 million m3 harvest, reaching 40 million by 2030; Forestry Fibre Task Force; BC Timber Sales to 9 million m3/yr by 2029; Working Forest zones set in law; $100M over three years to keep skilled forestry workers and grow mass-timber markets; Forestry One-Stop with one decision within 90 days.
- Manufacturing: double Manufacturing Jobs Fund to $60M/yr with new streams (prefab homes, mass timber and finished wood, food processing and storage, plant upgrades), public scorecard per grant project (announced Oct 9 in Bridgeview; also in platform). Note internal tension: the savings-fallback list says the fund "holds at today's $30 million a year" if savings fall short.
- Trade: double non-US exports to $62B by 2033 (from about $31B in 2025); US share of exports below 40% by 2032; five new trade offices (Gulf, Nordics, Poland, Australia, Brazil); $10M/yr Export BC top-up; remove remaining Canadian Free Trade Agreement exceptions by Jul 1, 2027; buy-Canadian target 60% of eligible procurement; Canadian steel on provincial projects; $25M/yr shipyard fund; $100M fund to bring supply chains home; R&D tax credit 10% to 15%; full-time BC representative in Washington within 90 days; $1.5B ask of Ottawa for Prince Rupert and Roberts Bank matched by $750M province.
- Red tape: cut provincial regulatory requirements 25% by 2030, one-in-one-out after; single business permit portal by end of 2028; routine permits decided in 90 days or deemed approved (not for First Nations consultation or environmental review).
- Jobs: $60M/yr more for skilled trades training (about 3,500 more seats/apprenticeships a year by 2028); $50M main streets fund; $125M/yr post-secondary stabilization fund.
- Agriculture: 10-year targets, loan guarantees for farms, update farm-classification rules, farm water stream ($15M/yr once Watershed Security Fund is doubled).

**New since Oct 9?** Manufacturing Jobs Fund $60M and trades $60M were announced Oct 9 / Oct 1; the rest is new in the Oct 10 platform.

## 10. Climate and energy

**Position**
- Targets: admits BC "will miss its 2030 target"; sets in law an "honest milestone" of 20% below 2007 by 2030 (down from the 40% target), keeps 2040 and 2050 targets; independent Climate Accountability Office reporting yearly to legislature.
- Carbon pricing: keep carbon pricing on large industrial emitters; "no consumer carbon tax"; at least $100M/yr of industrial carbon pricing revenue in law to the CleanBC Industry Fund. (BC's consumer carbon tax was already removed in 2025; platform commits not to bring one back.)
- Methane: put target in law, oil and gas methane 75% below 2014 by 2030, with yearly independent aerial survey.
- CleanBC: platform cites the 2025 independent review (BC on track for about half of 2030 target); does not use the "CleanBC" label for new programs apart from the Industry Fund. EV mandate/ZEV standard: not mentioned. EV rebates: noted as paused since 2025; platform commits to keep funding rebates for building-owner chargers and "right to charge" for renters, fast chargers at least every 80 km on main highways by 2030 (about 1,500 new ports), spread over two more years if savings fall short.
- Home upgrades: legislation in first year letting homeowners pay for heat pumps, insulation, solar through property tax or BC Hydro bill; low fixed-rate loans up to 20 years; about $500M lent by 2030/31, about 25,000 homes (loans recovered).
- BC Hydro: stays public; rate increases capped at inflation and 3% max 2027-2030; efficiency targets; +2,000 MW by 2033; new data centres pay full cost of power from 2027; sites over 10 MW need waste-heat reuse plans; up to 100 MW of AI data-centre power reserved for Canadian-controlled operators.
- LNG emissions: only the "net-zero ready" condition and the methane law. No cap on LNG emissions.
- Other: Watershed Security Fund doubled to $200M; 1,000 new provincial campsites by 2030; $15M/yr trails fund by 2028.

**New since Oct 9?** Yes (platform). Earlier: bcballot showed "no commitment published" on environment and energy.

**Sources**: Platform PDF pp 8, 11, 30-33, 39

## 11. Education and child care

**Position**
- Child care: 20,000 new $10-a-day spaces by 2030 (see topic 1).
- School construction/portables: 25,000 new school seats by 2031, fastest-growing districts and those with most portables first; design and permits 2027/28, building starts after the 2027 construction peak; standard designs and pre-fabricated additions; about $750M capital over four years. Context: 2,116 portables in overcrowded districts in 2022/23.
- Class size: keep the class-size and class-composition limits in the teachers' contract and fund them fully; publish average and largest class sizes by district yearly.
- Staffing: one counsellor per 400 students by 2030 (about 330 more than the new teachers' contract provides); 1,500 more education assistants by 2030; behaviour support teams in every district (about 350 workers); threat assessment teams; 120 education and family navigators; cut teacher paperwork 25% by 2028.
- Learning: math screening K-3 by 2028, about 250 literacy and numeracy coaches, yearly district results; targets 80% of Grade 4 on track in reading and 72% in math by 2030; letter grades with comments Grade 4-9 from Sep 2028; Foundation Skills Assessment in Grades 4 and 7 for every school, results by school and district; school food to 40% of students by 2030.
