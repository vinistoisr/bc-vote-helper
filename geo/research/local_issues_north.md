- "Claim" means a party, union or advocacy group assertion that was not independently checked.

### 2.1 Forestry: mill closures, timber supply, the Working Forest model

Facts:
- Canfor announced on July 14, 2026 that it will permanently close the Northwood pulp mill in Prince George in Q4 2026. About 300 employees are directly affected. Canfor cited ongoing pulp-division losses, global oversupply, and limited fibre supply in BC linked to beetle, wildfire and shrinking harvest levels. https://ckpgtoday.ca/2026/07/14/canfor-announces-permanent-closure-of-northwood-pulp-mill/ (Jul 14, 2026)
- Forests Minister Ravi Parmar's statement listed WorkBC services, retraining and the Canada-B.C. Workforce Tariff Response Forestry Grant. It gave no job-loss figures. https://news.gov.bc.ca/releases/2026FOR0038-000827 (Jul 14, 2026)
- Unifor asked the province on August 20 to reinstate the Bridging to Retirement program for forestry workers. More than 200 Unifor Local 603 members are affected. https://www.unifor.org/news/all-news/letter-premier-eby-urgent-support-request-forestry-workers (Aug 20, 2026). I found no party response to this request.
- The government's Working Forest Landscape Model would split public forest into three zones (Working Forest, Multiple Use, Conservation and Protection). It "aims to stabilize an economic land base that contributes to an annual harvest of 45 million cubic metres." Candidate areas were to be shared in fall 2026. https://www2.gov.bc.ca/gov/content/industry/natural-resource-use/working-forest-landscape-model (page last updated Aug 27, 2026). The first candidate area, near Campbell River, was announced September 15, 2026 (summary only: https://news.gov.bc.ca/releases/2026FOR0034-001069).
- Parmar said the plan includes a "no net loss provision": "any time we take from the working forest, we have to add back in." https://theprogress.com/2026/08/27/b-c-piloting-landscape-level-forestry-zone-based-management/ (Aug 27, 2026)
- Current harvest: the Ancient Forest Alliance puts it at roughly 30 million cubic metres a year (advocacy claim). https://ancientforestalliance.org/media-release-working-forest-proposal-revived-from-the-dead/ (Sep 10, 2026). CentreBC's platform says the 2025 harvest was 29 million cubic metres against a 45 million target.
- Doerkson (Nov 15, 2025 column) claims the allowable cut was "traditionally 60 million cubic metres" and was reduced to 45 million, and that 2025 output was the lowest since 1959. Treat as his claim. https://wltribune.com/2025/11/15/doerkson-west-frasers-permanent-mill-closure-in-100-mile-house-shocking/
- COFI's count of about 15,000 forestry jobs lost since 2022 appears in CP and CTV reporting. https://www.ctvnews.ca/vancouver/article/bcs-forestry-sector-faces-mounting-pressure-as-northwood-pulp-mill-closure-adds-to-job-losses/. The "21 mills closed since 2023" figure is used by the Conservatives (https://conservativebc.ca/supercharge-british-columbias-economy/, Oct 7, 2026) and is not independently verified here.

### 2.3 LNG and natural gas

Facts:
- LNG Canada Phase 2 final investment decision announced September 29, 2026: two more trains, capacity from 14 to 28 million tonnes a year, estimated cost about $33 billion, up to 4,000 construction jobs in Kitimat (company and government figures, from a search summary of Coast Mountain News and BNN Bloomberg; the Coast Mountain News page itself returned 404 when fetched, so confirm before quoting). https://www.bnnbloomberg.ca/business/company-news/2026/09/29/lng-canada-to-go-ahead-with-phase-2-expansion-project-in-kitimat-bc/
- NDP page: LNG Canada Phase 1 operating; Cedar LNG and Woodfibre LNG permitted and under construction; LNG Canada Phase 2, Tilbury Phase 2 and Ksi Lisims LNG permitted and moving to FID; capacity 14 million t/yr now and nearly 48 million by 2035 with those five projects; new projects must be net-zero-ready by 2030. These are the NDP's own figures. https://www.bcndp.ca/releases/bc-conservatives-lng-plan-out-step-reality (Sep 28, 2026)
- Note for question design: on the NDP's own numbers, its 2035 capacity (nearly 48 Mt) is above the Conservative "triple by 2035" target (about 42 Mt from a 14 Mt base). The real disagreement is on emissions rules, carbon pricing and approvals, not on the volume.
