# Southern Interior: regional issues for the BC election, October 24, 2026

Researched 2026-10-12. Region: Okanagan, Kamloops and North Thompson, Shuswap, Kootenays, Boundary-Similkameen, Fraser-Nicola.

Method: every fact below comes from a page fetched during this session (web search plus page fetch). Where a page was only seen as a search-result snippet, it is marked "snippet only". Party positions are checked against the party's own page or PDF wherever I could fetch it. BC Ballot (bcballot.ca, a non-partisan aggregator run by forelection.ca, "party sites checked October 11, 2026") was used to find the party lines, then verified at the party source. "Campaign claim" means a party's statement about its opponent or about a fact. "Fact" means a government or news source.

No em dashes are used. All money is CAD.

### 2.1 Wildfire (Bald Range fire, FireSmart, aircraft)

Facts
- The Bald Range fire started Aug 7, 2026 about 15 km north-west of Summerland. All of Summerland (about 12,000) and about 8,000 people in Peachland were ordered out. About 150 structures were damaged or destroyed, one 80-year-old woman died, and the fire was held at 25,185 hectares by Aug 28. A provincial state of emergency was declared Aug 8. Source: Wikipedia "Bald Range fire" (secondary, cites CBC, Global News, Kelowna Capital News): https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bald_Range_fire . Wikipedia also says many Faulder owners were uninsured or underinsured (cites CHEK, Sept 13).
- FireSmart (provincial community wildfire-prevention grants): the intake closed Jan 30, 2026 for lack of funding, fuel-management projects were no longer funded and requests were limited to one year. Source: UBCM, Jan 2026: https://www.ubcm.ca/about-ubcm/latest-news/lack-funds-drive-changes-firesmart-program . UBCM's president had asked Premier Eby to renew the funding.
- Budget 2026 added $15 million for core FireSmart; the 2027 intake opened Oct 1, 2026. Source: BC Gov News release 2026FOR0046 (undated in the fetched text, posted during the campaign period): https://news.gov.bc.ca/releases/2026FOR0046-001061

### 2.3 Kelowna and Okanagan street disorder, involuntary care, supervised consumption

Facts
- Kelowna council asked Health Canada to deny the 2027 renewal of the Outreach Urban Health supervised consumption site (1649 Pandosy St), citing 3,466 RCMP calls since it opened in 2021. The same letter repeats Kelowna's request to the province for "a regional mandatory care facility within the Okanagan". Mayor Tom Dyas shared it Sept 1. Kelowna Capital News: https://kelownacapnews.com/2026/09/01/kelowna-council-pushing-to-close-downtown-supervised-consumption-site/
- Same article: BC Coroners Service reported 17 toxic drug deaths in the Okanagan in June; year to date Interior Health has 132 deaths, 70 in the Okanagan (Kelowna 37, Kamloops 17, Vernon 11, Penticton 6).
- Okanagan mayors have repeatedly called for the Oliver Correctional Centre to be used to capacity for involuntary care (Kelowna Capital News, Oct 2).
- Provincial record: decriminalization was a federal exemption that expired Jan 31, 2026; the Health Minister said Jan 14, 2026 BC would not seek renewal; involuntary care beds so far are at Surrey Pretrial (10 beds, Apr 2025) and Alouette Homes, Maple Ridge (18 beds, Jun 2025), plus a $131 million announcement that includes Prince George, Maple Ridge and Surrey. None are in the Okanagan. Source (aggregator citing BC Gov): https://bcballot.ca/platforms/issues/drugs-and-addiction/
- Doerkson visited Kelowna businesses on Oct 2 and "stopped short of announcing specific measures". Global News, Oct 2: https://globalnews.ca/news/12086166/bc-ndp-conservative-leaders-campaign-okanagan/

### 2.4 Long-term care (Kelowna and Vernon)

Facts
- Budget 2026 (Feb 17) paused seven long-term care projects including the Cottonwoods replacement in Kelowna; no completion year is listed. Kelowna Capital News: https://kelownacapnews.com/2026/02/17/kelowna-among-7-scheduled-long-term-care-projects-delayed-by-b-c-budget/ . The province cited costs of up to $1.8 million per bed (Kelowna Capital News and Global News snippets).
- A private 250-bed Vernon project (150 LTC beds, 100 assisted living units) was pulled by Park Place Seniors Living, which cited uncertainty about operating costs and funding. The BC Care Providers Association said "the current government has no discernable plan for seniors care". Interior News, Oct 7: https://interior-news.com/2026/10/07/seniors-care-project-cancellation-draws-concerns/

Party positions
- NDP: resume the seven paused projects (Oct 5), funded from the July BC-Canada agreement, on a new model with lower per-bed cost and faster timelines. Source: https://www.mysouthokanagannow.com/8300/news/elections/2026-bc-election/b-c-ndp-plans-to-resume-seven-paused-long-term-care-projects-if-re-elected/
- Conservatives: build more LTC beds faster, cut design rules, portable public funding for seniors waiting for a bed including non-government providers, monthly waitlist publication (Oct 9). Source: https://conservativebc.ca/conservatives-will-build-the-long-term-care-beds-the-ndp-shelved/ (line list seen via BC Ballot; page title confirmed in site news feed)
- CentreBC: restart all seven paused projects with a pre-fabricated design (Abbotsford first, about 500 beds, open 2029/30); 2,500 beds under construction by March 2031 and 1,000 open. Source: platform PDF, Oct 10.
- Greens: plan to increase publicly funded LTC facilities; rural seniors strategy (Oct 4). Source: https://bcballot.ca/platforms/issues/health-care/
- OneBC: no LTC-specific line found; allows private healthcare alongside public.

### 2.5 Forestry and mill closures

Facts
- BC Council of Forest Industries tally: about 15,000 forestry jobs lost since 2022 (CHAT News, July 15, 2026: https://chatnewstoday.ca/2026/07/15/union-says-b-c-mill-closure-leaves-workers-reeling-as-industry-woes-persist/ ). Conservatives say 21 lumber mills have closed since 2023 (campaign claim, https://conservativebc.ca/supercharge-british-columbias-economy/ , Oct 7).
- The province is piloting a Working Forest Landscape Model near Campbell River (three zones; 45 million cubic metre annual harvest objective versus about 30 million now; "no net loss" rule). CBC: https://www.cbc.ca/news/canada/british-columbia/working-forest-model-cbc-explains-9.7345295 . This is a coastal pilot, not an Interior one.
- Gap: I found no Interior-specific campaign story on mills (Kamloops, Merritt, Grand Forks, Castlegar) in the outlets searched.

Party positions
- NDP: support mills to retrofit for value-added second-growth products (Oct 10); keep US alcohol off shelves until forest workers get a fair deal (Sept 22). Record: sponsor of the Working Forest model pilot (Forests Minister Ravi Parmar). Sources: https://www.bcndp.ca/releases/ebys-plan-protects-bcs-natural-spaces-funds-climate-action ; https://www.bcndp.ca/releases/david-eby-launches-election-plan-build-bc-strong-amid-trumps-attacks
- Greens: reject the Working Forest model and its 45 million m3 target; end conventional clearcutting; end raw-log exports; 20% of annual allowable cut to community forests; retraining for forestry workers (Oct 4). Source: https://bcgreens.ca/announce-plan-to-reform-forestry-sector-protecting-forests-jobs-and-communities/ (via BC Ballot)
- CentreBC: work toward 45 million m3 harvest, Working Forest zones set in law, Forestry Fibre Task Force, $100 million over three years for workers and mass timber, Forestry One-Stop with 90-day decisions, seat at softwood talks with Ottawa (Oct 10). Source: platform PDF.
- OneBC: raise the annual allowable cut, reduce stumpage fees, speed permits and consultations. Source: https://1bc.ca/priorities
- Conservatives: no published 2026 forestry policy found. Their only forestry content is the wildfire plan (more logging in high-risk areas) and the claim above.

- D. Change the public staffing model: nurse-to-patient ratios, an end to private agency nurses, and a community health centre in every riding.
