# Metro Vancouver (outside Surrey): regional issues for the Oct 24, 2026 BC election

Research date: October 12, 2026. Region: Vancouver, North Shore, West Vancouver, Sea to Sky, Burnaby, New Westminster, Richmond, Delta, Coquitlam, Port Coquitlam, Port Moody. All money is CAD. Every fact below came from a page fetched in this session (or is marked otherwise).

## How to read this file

- FACT = government record, court ruling, statistic, or a party's own published text.
- CLAIM = a campaign assertion by a party or candidate. Not independently checked.
- "No published position found" means I looked at the party's own pages and the BC Ballot aggregator (bcballot.ca) and found nothing.
- Party text comes from: BC NDP news releases (bcndp.ca) as indexed by BC Ballot (the NDP has not published a 2026 platform), the BC Conservatives' site (conservativebc.ca), the BC Greens' policy briefs (bcgreens.ca), the CentreBC platform PDF (44 pages, dated Oct 10, 2026), and OneBC's plan page (1bc.ca/priorities) and X posts. BC Ballot lists them: https://bcballot.ca/platforms/ (updated Oct 12, 2026).

## Honest limits of this research

1. "Most coverage" is my judgement from what I could fetch. I could not measure coverage volume. CBC, Vancouver Sun, Province, Global, North Shore News, Richmond News, Burnaby Now, Tri-City News and Delta Optimist pages mostly returned 403 or did not surface in search. What I did read: Daily Hive, CP24, CTV (summary only), Sitka Media, The Narwhal, North Delta Reporter, Angus Reid, Liaison Strategies, party sites and BC Ballot.
2. Very little of the 2026 campaign coverage I found is region-specific. The Vancouver (and other municipal) civic elections are on Oct 17, 2026, one week before the provincial vote, so a lot of local coverage is about city councils, not the province. Source: https://dailyhive.com/vancouver/bc-ndp-bc-ferries-priority-advance-reservations-routes-proposal (Oct 11, 2026).
3. The NDP has no 2026 platform document. Its positions come from news releases and leader statements. Where it has said nothing, I say so.
4. OneBC publishes a short plan and X posts. It has no transport, transit or Metro Vancouver content.
5. The first leaders' debates are Oct 13 (Board of Trade, Vancouver) and Oct 14 (broadcast). Only Eby, Doerkson and Lowan are invited. Sturko and Brodie are not. Anything said there is not in this file. Source: Wikipedia, 2026 BC general election (citing CBC and Global, Oct 6, 2026).

### Issue 1. TransLink funding and transit

**Background (FACT, Daily Hive, Oct 2, 2026, https://dailyhive.com/vancouver/bc-ndp-david-eby-translink-vehicle-levy):** The province committed $312 million in operating subsidy in 2025, covering about half of the shortfall through 2027. It promised legislation in 2027 for a new revenue source starting in 2028 but did not name one. TransLink needs at least $112 million a year in new revenue from 2028. **The trigger (CLAIM, partly reported):** CKNW host Jas Johal reported that the province and TransLink had discussed a $100 to $150 a year vehicle levy collected at ICBC renewal. The Daily Hive piece says this was not independently confirmed.

### Issue 2. Cowichan ruling in Richmond, DRIPA, private homes

**Background (FACT):**
- Aug 2025 BC Supreme Court ruling recognized Cowichan Tribes' Aboriginal title over land in Richmond including privately owned land. Cowichan Tribes, other First Nations, the Province, Ottawa and Richmond have appealed (CentreBC platform p. 35, CAMPAIGN text, but the appeals are also in The Narwhal piece below). CentreBC says about 145 private properties are affected. Sources conflict on area (CentreBC says about 300 hectares, one search result says about 7.5 square kilometres). Do not use an area figure in the app.
- Dec 2025 BC Court of Appeal ruling (Gitxaala) made DRIPA obligations legally enforceable. April 20, 2026: Eby and the First Nations Leadership Council announced no DRIPA amendments in the spring session (reported via CTV and National Newswatch search results, https://nationalnewswatch.com/2026/04/20/eby-moving-forward-with-first-nations-after-latest-climbdown-on-indigenous-law; I did not open the page). The Narwhal (Oct 7) says the Cowichan and Gitxaała cases are often confused in the property-rights debate.
- Angus Reid, March 11-17, 2026 (n=499 BC adults, +/-4): 53% say DRIPA goes too far; 65% say private property rights should come first if the two conflict. https://angusreid.org/bc-eby-musqueam-cowichan-property-rights-conservative-leadership/
