## 0. Read this first: limits of the evidence

The Fraser Valley Current's 2024 candidate Q&As on floods and the ALR are from the 2024 election, not this one. Absence of a position for these two means "not yet published," not "opposed." Recheck before launch.

### Issue 1: Farmland (ALR) and food processing

**Background facts (government and reporting)**
- July 23, 2026 (updated Aug 18): the province proposed letting food processors build on ALR Class 5 to 7 soil, and on Class 4 soil that is already serviced. The local-product minimum would fall from 50% to 5%, and that product could come from anywhere in B.C. Land covered would be capped at 0.25% of the 2.1 million hectares of privately owned ALR land. The soil test would exclude about 90% of ALR farmland in the Lower Mainland and Fraser Valley; 500 to 1,000 hectares could be available. Source: https://news.gov.bc.ca/releases/2026AF0017-000863
- Consultation closed Sept 18, 2026. Eby called the election days later, which paused the proposal. Source: The Tyee, Oct 7, 2026.
- Supporters named by The Tyee: BC Food & Beverage, Greater Vancouver Board of Trade, Abbotsford Chamber of Commerce, BC Fresh. Opponents: the Agricultural Land Commission (17-page submission calling it "one of the most significant policy shifts" in decades), BC Agriculture Council, former NDP minister Joan Sawicki (The Tyee, Sept 14, 2026, https://thetyee.ca/News/2026/09/14/BC-Eyes-Controversial-Changes-Agricultural-Land-Rules/).
- Eby said the proposal affects 0.3% of the ALR; the BC Agriculture Council gave a much larger figure. The Tyee notes the figures do not reconcile. Treat both as claims.

## 3. Recommended three local questions

Selection logic against the three tests:
- (a) Matters to many voters in the region: all three affect all seven communities or at least five of them.
- (b) At least three parties differ: yes for each, with the gaps noted.
- (c) Provincial decision: ALR rules are provincial regulation; ER staffing and hospital operations are provincial health policy; Highway 1 is a provincial highway and transit funding is provincial.

Why not flood protection or public safety: flood protection has positions from only two parties (NDP record, CentreBC fund), so options cannot be scored with confidence. Public safety and extortion overlap heavily with the province-wide public safety question and with Surrey's local question. They are the two runner-ups.

Caution on question 2: it overlaps with the province-wide health care question. It is kept because the Mission ER closures are a regional, specific, and live issue, and the answer options focus on ER staffing and the care model, not general health policy. If the team wants zero overlap, swap in the flood question (see section 4).

Scoring scale: 4 = the party's stated position, 0 = the opposite. Where a party has no published position, the score is a neutral 2 and is labelled NO POSITION. Those cells should be shown to voters as "no published position" or be excluded from the match, not counted as agreement.

### Local question A: Farmland and food processing

**Question text (draft):** The province proposed allowing food processing plants on some lower-quality farmland in the Agricultural Land Reserve. What should happen?

**Options (neutral wording, no party names)**
- A1. Keep the rules as they are. Farmland is for farming. Processing plants belong on industrial land.
- A2. Keep the rules, and focus on who owns farmland: limit foreign and corporate buyers, create a public land bank for farmers, tax unused farmland.
- A3. Keep the rules, and help processors with money instead: loan guarantees, grants and a processing fund, plus faster industrial land approvals.
- A4. Allow processing on a small, capped share of lower-quality farmland, with a soil assessment and local permits.
- A5. Loosen the limits further and let landowners and local governments decide what is built on farmland with weak soil.

### Local question B: ER closures

**Question text (draft):** Emergency rooms in places like Mission have closed overnight because there are not enough doctors. What should the province do first?

**Options**
- B1. Put money into keeping existing community and rural ERs open: staffing funds, retention bonuses, flexible staffing, and public reporting of every closure.
- B2. Recruit faster: fast-track foreign-trained and out-of-province doctors, add training seats, and offer tuition and moving help.
- B3. Change how care is delivered: community health centres and team-based clinics in every area so fewer people need the ER, with one provincial health authority.
- B4. Build and staff larger regional hospitals and send patients there overnight instead of keeping smaller ERs open.
- B5. Allow private clinics and private surgery alongside the public system to take pressure off ERs.
