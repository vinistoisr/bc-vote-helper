## 2. Party positions by issue

Format: position, date, source. RECORD = verified government action. CLAIM = a party or advocate statement I did not independently verify.

### 2.1 BC Ferries

**BC NDP (governing since 2017)**
- Oct 10, 2026: locals-first reservation pilot. 12% of advance booking space for residents of ferry-dependent communities (10% until four days before sailing, plus 2% until 24 hours before). Starts on two routes only: Horseshoe Bay to Langdale and Westview (Powell River) to Little River (Comox). Then "work with BC Ferries and other ferry-dependent communities" on other routes. Swartz Bay to Tsawwassen is not included. Sources: https://www.bcndp.ca/releases/bc-ndp-would-put-locals-first-new-ferry-reservation-pilot and https://victoriabuzz.com/2026/10/heres-what-bcs-major-political-parties-are-promising-for-bc-ferries-ahead-of-the-election/
- Sep 18, 2026 (UBCM convention): commitment that future BC Ferries vessels be built in BC. No timeline announced. Source: Victoria Buzz, Oct 11 (link above).
- RECORD: in May 2025 BC Ferries picked a Chinese state-owned shipyard for four large hybrid-electric vessels. The province says no local yard bid. A recent report (cited by Black Press) estimates a $1.5 billion loss of Canadian GDP; I did not open the report or check who funded it. Source: https://nanaimobulletin.com/2026/10/09/b-c-greens-release-transportation-plans-includes-free-transit-for-youth-seniors/
- No fare measure found.

**BC Conservatives (interim leader Lorne Doerkson)**
- Oct 11, 2026: BC Ferries Charter with standards for reliability, on-time performance, capacity, affordability and customer service; hold leadership to those standards; cut duplication at the board level; negotiate with Ottawa for "fair share" of federal ferry funding; high-speed Wi-Fi on major routes and terminals through private contracts "with no new burden on fares or taxpayers". No timeline. Source: https://conservativebc.ca/conservatives-will-fix-bc-ferries/
- CLAIM (same release): the NDP "devastated" BC Ferries.
- No position found on where new ferries are built.

**BC Greens (leader Emily Lowan)**
- Oct 9, 2026: work with the federal government for funding parity with Atlantic Canada; review governance and long-term funding; expand terminal and dock infrastructure and plan for electric ferries; build and maintain ships locally with union labour. Source: https://bcgreens.ca/bc-greens-commit-to-funding-for-fast-frequent-transit-pledge-to-fight-for-province-wide-passenger-rail/

**CentreBC (leader Elenore Sturko)**
- Platform (by Oct 11, 2026): future BC Ferries vessels and other provincial ships built in Canada; $25 million a year fund to grow BC shipyards and train shipbuilders; a refundable "BC Commuter Credit" returning 15% of the cost of transit passes and BC Ferries multi-trip fares. Source: https://www.centrebc.ca/wp-content/uploads/2026/10/CentreBC-2026-Platform-Web.pdf (Economy and Affordability section, "Buy Canadian" and "Transportation that works").

**OneBC (interim leader Dallas Brodie)**: no published position found.

Fact check note: Victoria Buzz states that none of the three announcements sets a new Swartz Bay-Tsawwassen schedule or a fare cut.

### 2.2 Forestry, mills and old growth

**BC NDP**
- Oct 10, 2026: $50 million to convert old-growth deferrals of "the most irreplaceable and rarest forests" into permanent protection, while helping mills retrofit to make more value-added products from second growth. Also 30 by 30 land protection with the federal government and $30 million a year for the BC Watershed Security Fund. Source: https://www.bcndp.ca/releases/ebys-plan-protects-bcs-natural-spaces-funds-climate-action
- RECORD: the Forests minister's mandate letter sets a 45 million cubic metre annual harvest target. Minister Ravi Parmar (Langford-Highlands) described a "working forest" land base at the 2026 COFI convention (article date not shown, search snippet says April 2026), said Budget 2026 projects about 30 million cubic metres for three years, and said Bill 14 would unlock about 800,000 cubic metres. Source: https://treefrogcreative.ca/minister-parmar-outlines-working-forest-vision-commits-to-structural-shift-as-sector-presses-for-fibre-flow/
- RECORD: Dec 3, 2025, Premier Eby said government restrictions on old-growth harvesting raised fibre prices on Vancouver Island but were "a necessary decision", and said pulping old growth to extend the Crofton mill was not an option. Domtar's Crofton mill (350 jobs) then closed. Source: https://nationalnewswatch.com/2025/12/03/eby-says-no-to-harvesting-old-growth-to-extend-life-of-b-c-pulp-mill
- Sep 22, 2026: keep US alcohol off BC shelves "until we get a fair deal for forest workers". Source: https://www.bcndp.ca/releases/david-eby-launches-election-plan-build-bc-strong-amid-trumps-attacks

**BC Conservatives**
- No 2026 forestry plan found on their site. What exists:
  - Oct 7, 2026, "Supercharge" release: cut red tape to 2017 levels, "one-in, two-out" law, 120-day permitting for major projects over $250 million, PST break on business machinery. CLAIM: "Twenty-one lumber mills have closed since 2023". Source: https://conservativebc.ca/supercharge-british-columbias-economy/
  - Nov 7, 2025, forestry critic Ward Stamer blamed "restrictive policies", permitting delays and shrinking fibre access for mill closures and called the NDP "Path to 45" "a path to collapse". Source: https://westknews.com/2025/11/07/conservative-forestry-critic-calls-closure-of-100-mile-west-fraser-mill-devastating/
  - May 27, 2025 Stamer op-ed: wants sustainable harvest decided with First Nations, community forests and licensees, plus wildfire fuel reduction. Source: https://armchairmayor.ca/2025/05/27/stamer-how-long-will-it-take-to-get-45-million-cubic-metres-of-viable-fibre/
  - Oct 2, 2026 wildfire plan: clear dangerous fuels around communities, easier prescribed burns, aircraft purchase (a news summary puts it at half a billion dollars; search snippet only, not opened, and the release gives no amount). Source: https://conservativebc.ca/fighting-wildfires-protecting-communities/
- No stated position on old-growth deferrals found. The BC NDP claims the Conservatives oppose "30 by 30" (CLAIM by NDP, Oct 10 release above).

**BC Greens**
- Oct 4, 2026 forestry plan: reject the NDP "Working Forest Model"; set harvest by regional, area-based planning; end raw-log exports; permanently protect big-treed old growth and large second growth; implement all 14 Old Growth Strategic Review recommendations with conservation financing for First Nations; shift to partial harvesting, selective logging and longer rotations. Source: https://bcgreens.ca/announce-plan-to-reform-forestry-sector-protecting-forests-jobs-and-communities/
- CLAIM (Greens): provincial forest revenue was $397 million in 2025/26, below the Forests ministry operating cost; the NDP model would raise the harvest to 45 million cubic metres.
- Oct 7, 2026: phase out industrial clear-cutting; criticises Domtar for closing mills including Crofton. Source: https://pqbnews.com/2026/10/07/lowan-launches-greens-economic-vision-for-b-c/

**CentreBC**
- Platform: work toward 45 million cubic metres of "economically viable" timber, reaching 40 million by 2030; Forestry Fibre Task Force; BC Timber Sales to 9 million cubic metres by 2029; Working Forest zones set in law alongside multiple-use and conservation areas; $100 million over three years for forestry workers and mass timber; Manufacturing Jobs Fund doubled to $60 million a year; Forestry One-Stop permits decided within 90 days (not where First Nations consultation or environmental review applies). The platform text has no old-growth section (searched). Source: CentreBC platform PDF (link in 2.1).

**OneBC**: no published position found.

### 2.3 Hospitals, rural emergency rooms and seniors' care

**BC NDP**
- Oct 9, 2026: training for internationally trained doctors at the new SFU medical school, discounted tuition, loan forgiveness and moving costs for health workers going to rural areas, incentives for same-day and evening care, 1,000 training seats, 20 more primary care clinics. Source: https://www.timescolonist.com/2026-bc-votes/bc-election-campaigns-promise-support-for-doctors-seniors-12881010
- Oct 5-6, 2026 (Victoria): $90 million toward redeveloping the former Oak Bay Lodge site as a "campus of care"; restart seven paused long-term care builds, including a 153-bed facility in Campbell River; free shingles vaccine for people 50 and over. Source: https://www.timescolonist.com/2026-bc-votes/bc-ndp-promises-90m-to-redevelop-oak-bay-lodge-site-free-shingles-vaccines-12859483
- Oct 6, 2026: eight more MRI machines, 10 CT scanners, regional surgical waitlists. Source: https://nanaimobulletin.com/2026/10/06/b-c-ndp-promises-faster-health-care-with-new-diagnostic-equipment/
- RECORD (Nanaimo): the 2024 NDP pledge of a new patient tower and cardiac cath lab at Nanaimo Regional General Hospital was not in the 2025 budget. Budget 2026 (passed Feb 17) funds only initial planning; the ministry says concept planning takes up to 18 months. The regional hospital district agreed to front $50 million for planning the cath lab, and Island Health issued a request for a concept-plan consultant on July 23. Sources: https://nanaimobulletin.com/2026/02/24/acute-care-tower-and-the-expansion-of-cardiac-services-advancing-at-nanaimo-hospital/ and https://nanaimonewsnow.com/2026/07/28/consultant-sought-to-advance-nanaimo-hospital-patient-tower-cath-lab/
- RECORD: government paused seven long-term care builds in Budget 2026 and says it paused them to adopt a lower-cost uniform design (Times Colonist, Oct 6, link above).
- RECORD: about one million people connected to primary care since 2018; about one million still without a family doctor (Canadian Press, Oct 9, link above).
- CLAIM (advocate, Jan 2026): the new Cowichan hospital "started as $850 million" and is now $1.2 billion. Source: https://nanaimobulletin.com/2026/01/18/were-going-to-ask-for-a-billion-dollars-nanaimo-health-care-advocates-heading-to-ottawa/

**BC Conservatives**
- Oct 5, 2026: $50 million emergency fund for rural practices to prevent closures and aid recruitment (no answer on whether one-time); keep the 2026 health budget; expert commission within 100 days; prioritise BC students for medical training; fast-track credentials. Sources: https://nanaimobulletin.com/2026/10/05/lorne-doerkson-pledges-to-create-50m-fund-to-address-emergency-room-closures/ and https://conservativebc.ca/plan/keep-emergency-rooms-open/
- Oct 9, 2026: portable public funding for seniors waiting for long-term care, usable at "any qualified provider, public or non-government"; build beds faster by cutting design rules. Source: https://conservativebc.ca/conservatives-will-build-the-long-term-care-beds-the-ndp-shelved/
- Nanaimo-Lantzville candidate Kelsey Pringle says cardiac care should be brought closer to home (CHEK candidate list). No 2026 party commitment to the Nanaimo tower or cath lab found. A CHEK headline from 2024 says the Conservatives pledged the tower then (search snippet only, article not opened).
- CLAIM: ERs "closed 2,400 times in two years" (Canadian Press attributes this to the Conservatives).

**BC Greens**
- Our Plan page (undated, 2026): Community Health Centres in every region, safe nurse-to-patient ratios, "keep every public health dollar in public care", public coverage of mental health care. No Island hospital item found. Source: https://bcgreens.ca/our-plan/

**CentreBC**
- Platform: ER Accountability Act with public reporting of every closure; cut unplanned ER closure hours in half by 2029/30; make the rural retention bonus permanent and extend it to every community with repeated closures; $25 million a year rural ER stabilization fund; cut patients stuck in hospital waiting for long-term care from about 1,600 to 1,000 by 2030; "public health care, not private pay". The PDF has no Nanaimo, Cowichan or Island Health item (searched). Source: CentreBC platform PDF.

**OneBC**: general remark that "people are dying in waiting rooms" (CBC, Oct 3); no policy. https://www.cbc.ca/news/canada/british-columbia/dallas-brodie-onebc-q-a-2026-election-9.7366117
